Glossary (each term once): **orchestrator / executor** — the model instance that holds the question and writes one brief per fact, and
the fresh instance of the same model with web tools that answers one brief. **Brief** — the message down; **return** — the message up.
**Return rate** — the length of the return in tokens. **Matched loss** $L$ — single-agent accuracy minus delegated accuracy at the same
pooled tool budget and tool-use instruction; **unmatched loss** $\Delta_u$ — the paper's. **Recovered fraction** $\rho_{80}$ — the share
of $L$ an 80-token structured return recovers. **Relay** — 2607.16133's word for any bounded message between agents. **Handoff tax** —
2610.06069's per-boundary log-loss increment $h = G_M - G_H$ of a chunk started from a note $M$ against the same chunk started from the
full history $H$, with the context-load term removed. **Post-cutoff** — facts dated after the model's training data ends.

## 3. The two closest papers' limitation and scope text (verbatim, grep-verified)

**2606.05304 PACT, sec. 3.3:** "Full Content forwards the entire free-form output, including the explanations, which is consistently
expensive and rarely gives the best performance." … "These results suggest that generic conciseness is not a reliable communication rule: a
message can be short while still omitting the information needed by the next agent." … "Thus, removing thinking processes is helpful, but
keeping only the conclusion is not a general solution: it depends on whether the surrounding MAS topology already supplies the missing
action and state information." **Sec. 5.3 (Table 3, HotpotQA, Qwen3-8B):** "Removing State also hurts performance, which indicates that the
action result is less useful when it is not grounded in the sender's observed evidence. The largest degradation appears when both action
and state are removed and only Result is forwarded: F1 falls to 64.3 and token usage increases by 12.9%." **Limitations:** "PACT is designed
for MAS settings where shared conversational history is a major source of token cost, its benefits on systems with short interactions or
architectures that do not repeatedly expose prior agent outputs are not fully explored. Our experiments cover two controlled MAS topologies
and two agentic coding harnesses, but they do not exhaust all forms of multi-agent collaboration, such as open-ended debate, tool-heavy
planning, or dynamically routed agent networks."

**2610.06069 Handoff Tax, sec. 10:** "note allowances of 100, 300, and 900 tokens (produced notes 137, 183, and 255 tokens). The model is
gpt-oss-20b" … "Starting from a note, at a mean load of 1.6k, they failed at 3.2, 2.0, and 1.8 per cent for the three note lengths." …
"the total reference-chunk taxes are 0.077, 0.036, and 0.015 nats, still nonincreasing in note length." … "The increment tax has a 95%
interval of [−0.11, 0.16] nats." **Sec. 11:** "The handoff tax is one scalar per boundary, related to the information-theoretic loss of Ao et
al. [2] by analogy." … "The measurements cover one model on two tasks."

**Third, 2607.16133, sec. 7:** "We assign δ by task-structure analysis rather than estimating it quantitatively. Future work should develop
quantitative estimators for both, and use them to train adaptive relays." **Sec. 6:** "SAS-Plan uses the same decomposed sub-instructions as
MAS but maintains a single shared context." … "we construct higher-loss relay variants by deliberately removing downstream-required
information while keeping the same MAS decomposition".

**Fourth, the card 2611.16526, sec. 7:** "Instructing executors to report failures yields only limited improvements. Passing complete
executor traces to the orchestrator could mitigate the loss of information, but would undermine the context-saving benefits of delegation."
… "More effective mitigation may require targeted model training to induce specialization, tracking search progress, or mechanisms for
selectively communicating evidence and failures." **Sec. 6:** "For GPT-5, accuracy on GDP increases from 0.43 to 0.51 with optional
delegation and from 0.35 to 0.44 with forced delegation, but remains well below the single-agent baseline (0.89). For GPT-OSS, accuracy on
the GDP task drops from 0.70 to 0.62 for optional delegation and from 0.73 to 0.64 for forced delegation." **App. A:** "The orchestrator
receives neither the executor's intermediate trajectory nor the contents returned by its web tools."

**Harness owners (fetched 2026-11-15):** Cognition: "Share context, and share full agent traces, not just individual messages"; "context
isn't able to be shared thoroughly enough between the agents". Anthropic: "Subagent output to a filesystem to minimize the 'game of
telephone.' Direct subagent outputs can bypass the main coordinator for certain types of results, improving both fidelity and performance."
The two shipped positions bracket the claim: one says the fix is the full trace, the other that the fix is to route artifacts around the
return; neither measures a rate, and both already treat the answer-only return as the fault.

## 4. Why has this not been done

The mechanism was done (PACT, 2026-06-03: evidence-grounded compact return beats answer-only and full trace at fewer tokens), the
measurement design was done (2610.06069, 2026-10-05: the handoff loss at three note lengths against a single-agent reference at matched
per-step budget), and the context-separation isolation was done (2607.16133, SAS-Plan). The claim's cell — the same measurement on
2611.16526's fact sets at matched tool budget and instruction, post-cutoff — is undone because the paper is three days old. Its authors
hold the harness, the prompts and the no-tool outputs, and their conclusion names the arm ("mechanisms for selectively communicating
evidence and failures"). "Why now" for us is therefore the race against the owners of the testbed, and the enabling piece (public-API ground
truth) is theirs too.

## 5. Killing experiment check

The cheapest probe (C-W46-07: frozen executor traces replayed at five return rates, about 12 GPU-hours and \$415 in 8 days) is under one
GPU-week and its kill clauses are satisfiable. But its first four arms are PACT's strategies (Conclusion Only $= M^{(0)}$, Brief Summary
$= M^{(2)}$, Full Content $= M^{(3)}$, Artifact/action-state $\approx M^{(1)}$) and its curve-with-a-knee readout is 2610.06069's
note-length tax, so a result in either direction is an application of two published designs to one paper's data. The design hint that
survives: the matched loss $L = \bar p(A_0) - \bar p(D(1,1))$ is the only quantity none of the occupants reports, and it must be measured
before any return arm, because if $L < 2$ points there is nothing to recover and if $L \approx \Delta_u$ the paper's reading stands with the
protocol cleared. Three arms (single agent at the paper's protocol; forced delegation at the paper's protocol; forced delegation at the
single agent's pooled budget and instruction), gpt-oss-120b on GDP, $r = 5$, about 12 GPU-hours and \$200 of search, five days. The
two-player Shapley split and the five-rate replay are addenda to that number, not claims. C-W46-10's prior-leak share is a mechanism label for
the same footnote; 2609.05587's "corrupted returns also frequently override correct answers that agents give without tools" is the datum
against its expected direction and must be in its pre-registration if it ever runs.
