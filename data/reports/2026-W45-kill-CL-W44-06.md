Glossary (each term once): **compaction** — replacing an agent's earlier context with a model-written summary when the
window fills. **Full-history pair** — the same task continued from the uncompressed transcript, used as the control arm.
**Compaction-attributable failure** — a failure under the summary that the full-history arm does not show.
**Lost binding** — a value or rule that survives into the summary but has lost what tied it to its referent: a withdrawn
approval kept as current (stale), a look-alike authority or target read as the real one, a rule whose scope or exception
was cut. **Dropped constraint** — a rule absent from the summary. **Unsourced entry** — a summary line nothing in the
history supports (SJC's "unsupported"). **Rule state judge / agent behavior judge** — ARCBench's two diagnostic labels:
whether the compacted history still carries the rule (retained / partially retained / absent) and the reason the agent gives
for breaking a rule it still has (approval / scope / unregistered / acknowledged). **Ledger** — 2611.06545's per-task
machine-readable permission state, which makes "unsupported" checkable without a judge. **Same-snapshot replay** — rerunning
the successor from the compaction snapshot with one summary sentence edited. **Count-matched control edit** — the same
number of sentences replaced at random positions.

## 3. The two closest papers' limitation text (verbatim, grep-verified against the fetched HTML)

**ARCBench 2611.03223.** Limitations: "ARCBench contains seventeen English cases from a few domains: messaging and email,
file access, software operations, and purchasing. Operating rules in other domains, such as clinical, financial, or legal
work, depend on domain knowledge that these cases do not test, so our findings may not carry over to them". Conclusion:
"Future studies may use ARCBench to explain why agents still violate retained rules, test adaptive attacks and defenses, and
cover more models, domains, and longer sessions with repeated compaction." Related work: "A yes-or-no retention label also
cannot show a rule whose wording was weakened, for example by losing a scope, an exception, or a negation." Section 4.2:
"Of these 37.8 points, the agent's own violations account for 8.9, and the other 28.9 appear only after compaction. Most
compacted runs still retain the rule (70.1%); 26.9% retain it partially, and only 3.0% lose it." ... "After compaction, 70.1%
of runs retain the rule (Table 3), and 21.1% of these runs still violate, which is 2.6 times than runs under the full-context
baseline." Section 4.3: "Pi + RP (Pi with rule preservation) is the only strategy below the full-context baseline (9.0%
against 11.9%)". Models: DeepSeek-V4-Flash, GLM-5.3-Flash, Qwen3.7-Flash, GPT-Luna via OpenRouter, $k = 3$ runs per case.

**Handoff-audit 2611.06545.** Limitations: "Our tasks are synthetic, built so that the ledger is unambiguous." ... "F4 fixed
the compaction point, but most of its sources were still overgeneralized. Templates that vary the two independently would be
needed to separate the explanations in Section 5.4." ... "The oracle arms add information the summary did not have, and the
full-history arm changes both the content and the length of the successor's context. Neither edit changes only the claim:
the neutral edit also removes whatever else a target sentence says, such as a next step or a reported rejection, and the
count-matched control often replaced the line that states the task." Section 5.4: "Of the 76 held-out ones (F1 and F2

confirmation rate (197/236 to 190/236; 18 sources down, 12 up, $p = 0.36$). Writing in the true permission brought it to

## 4. Which side this week's cards fall on

  compactors (3.0% of compacted runs; 84.1% under selective ones); the compaction-added violations sit in the retained and partially retained cells, with the
