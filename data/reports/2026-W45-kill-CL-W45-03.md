Glossary (each term once): **turn cap** $T$ — the maximum number of agent actions an episode may use; $T_{\max}$ is the cap
used in training. **Announced prompt** — the paper's protocol: the system prompt states the cap, the turns used and the turns
remaining before every action. **Held (silent) prompt** — the prompt keeps saying $T_{\max}$ (or says nothing) while the episode is
cut at a smaller $T$. **$\mathrm{Pass}@(K,T)$** — the probability that at least one of $K$ independent episodes, each cut at $T$
turns, is scored a success; $K = 1$ is single-attempt success. **Full-budget RL** — the paper's standard RL arm, trained only at
$T_{\max}$ with the announced prompt. **BudgetMix** — the paper's recipe: sample the training cap uniformly from ten fractions of
$T_{\max}$ and announce it. **Budget-blind policy** — a policy whose prompt never mentions a budget. **Censoring** — in the
statistics of life tests, a unit still running when the test stops is not a failure; its event time is only known to exceed the
stop time. **Catastrophic truncation** — AdaTurn's name for an agent that keeps calling tools until the cap fires without having
answered. **Coverage term** — the part of a $\mathrm{Pass}@8$ gap that comes from spread across eight tries rather than from
per-try success.

## 0. Verdict in one paragraph

**Survived — weak, with a mandatory rewrite of the mechanism clause and the probe design.** The control the claim names (the same
Full-budget RL checkpoint evaluated at a tight cap once with the cap announced and once with the prompt held at $T_{\max}$, read at
$K = 1$ beside $K = 8$) has no occupant: the card paper never runs it — every evaluation is announced ("The budget is specified in the
system prompt before generation begins", App. B.1; the repository's own word is "budget-visible rerolls") — and no other paper
evaluates an RL policy trained with a fixed announced cap under a different announced cap against a held-prompt arm. The Gate
0/2B question the brief asked is therefore answered no: the paper does not control the prompt line. But the claim's substance,
"not a budget dependence", is pressed from two sides by evidence that was free to read. (i) Inside the paper: GiGPO-Full and
PPO-Full, trained under the identical announced prompt at $T_{\max}$ and evaluated under the identical tight-cap protocol, keep
their lead over the base on FrozenLake at 25% of cap (Qwen3-4B: 38.4 and 38.5 vs 30.3 $\mathrm{Pass}@8$, card; App. D.1: "rather
than losing their advantage as the budget tightens, models trained with these algorithms achieve relatively consistent gains
across budgets"), so the announced-but-unfamiliar counter is not by itself sufficient to collapse a Full-budget policy; what
separates the collapsing GRPO arm from the non-collapsing arms is the credit signal, which is what RL learned. (ii) Outside the
paper: the one published tight-cap, single-attempt, RL-versus-base series with no budget line anywhere (AdaTurn 2607.14547, Table 1,
Avg@32 at 4 versus 32 turns) shows a budget-blind RL agent losing 56% to 95% of its full-cap lead over the base and falling below the
base on one benchmark, with the mechanism named ("the agent may continue requesting tools until the system terminates the rollout
without any valid answer. We call this failure mode catastrophic truncation") — budget dependence under a silent cap, in a
neighbouring domain. Against that, the metric's origin paper (2604.14877, Table 2, silent cap, separate rollouts per $T$) shows the
RL-minus-base gap flat across $T$ on HotPotQA (about $-3$ points at $k = 1$ and $+4$ at $k = 64$ at both $T = 1$ and $T = 5$), so the
silent-cap evidence is domain-dependent and FrozenLake is undecided. The claim survives because its pre-registered surprise is not
the field's stated knowledge and nobody has run its control; it survives weakly because the nearest free evidence lowers the
judgment probability of its expected result from 0.55 to about 0.25, because its headline bundles a prompt term and a coverage term
that must be filed as two one-quantifier claims (checklist 2026-11-01), and because the race is with three groups that hold the
stack and have each written the missing run into their own text.

## 4. Why not done, and why now

The announced-versus-held control on a fixed-cap RL checkpoint has not been run because every paper in the budget-aware line
(AdaTurn, AnySearch, On the Clock, 2610.28409, BudgetMix) treats the announced counter as part of the fix and varies the cap, never the
visibility; the one paper with a hidden-versus-stated control (On the Clock) runs it on untrained agents for wall-clock budgets. The
card paper is five days old, ships the prompt verbatim and the evaluator but no checkpoints ("Model weights ... are not bundled",
README), so the control needs one GRPO run under its recipe. Enabling pieces: the public code and tutorial (2026-10-30), the
Pass@(k,T) grid as the lane's standard reading (2026-04), the censoring reading imported 2026-11-08, and three limitation sentences
that each ask for one piece of the run (2604.14877's temperature sweep for the $k = 1$ versus $k = 64$ confound; 2610.28409's prompt
ablation; 2611.06483's own D.1 contrast). "Why not done" is therefore "the stack is a week old", not "no reason". The groups that can
run it tomorrow: the 2611.06483 authors (checkpoints), the 2610.28409 authors (budget-prompted GRPO-Std on BabyAI turn budgets), the
2604.14877 authors ("the infrastructure is already in place"). "They have the stack" is a guess about priorities; the step-0 letter
is the re-open test.

## 5. Gate-by-gate verdict (claim level)

**Gate 0 (premise hygiene).** Passes, with one correction. The paper does not control the prompt line (three sentences above), so the
confound is real. FrozenLake is deterministic after the action ("known deterministic transition dynamics", App. B.4); the quantity
$p_T(x) = \Pr_{\tau \sim \pi(\cdot \mid x, T_{\max})}[R(\tau) = 1,\ \ell(\tau) \le T]$ — $x$ the map, $R \in \{0,1\}$ the verifier,
$\ell(\tau)$ the turns used — is a property of the (policy, prompt, cap) triple at the stated temperature 0.6. Correction: the headline's
"to or below its base model at a quarter of the turn cap" is a Qwen3-8B GRPO cell (31.9 vs 34.0, Table 1); at 4B the FrozenLake 25%
cells in print are GiGPO 38.4 and PPO 38.5 against base 30.3, and the 4B GRPO cell is not in the card — the probe's training run is at
4B, so the premise number must be the 4B GRPO cell, read off Figure 3 or asked of the authors.

**Gate 1 (cheap to kill).** Passes: S8xF9 at about 50 GPU-hours, with the kill written (at 25% of cap $|\Delta_{\mathrm{RL}}| < 3$
points, interval excluding $-5$, deficit mostly wrong commits). Both outcomes are citable only if the held-prompt $K = 1$ and $K = 8$
numbers are reported either way; the negative is the first $\mathrm{Pass}@(1,T)$ sweep in this lane, a footnote the paper's v2 would cite.

**Gate 2 (pressure; route B).** Passes narrowly. The pre-registered surprise (RL above base at 25% under the held prompt) is not the
field's stated knowledge; the field says budget dependence (2611.06483, AnySearch). But the field's knowledge now includes the
silent-cap collapse of a budget-blind RL agent (AdaTurn Table 1) and the silent-cap non-collapse in a saturating domain (2604.14877
Table 2), so the claim's expected result is one of two published patterns and FrozenLake decides between them; the surprise is
therefore a domain fact, not a reversal of the field's belief. Judgment probability of the expected result: 0.25 (down from 0.55).

**Gate 3 (consumers).** Unchanged: multi-turn RL evaluators (hold the prompt; report $K = 1$), BudgetMix adopters (prompt-mixing
control), deployers under step caps, framework maintainers (log termination type). One community; proper-shaped.

**Gate 4 (mechanism and consequence).** Fails as written; rewrite owed. The mechanism ("a prompt the RL model never trained on") must
say why it collapses GRPO-Full and not GiGPO-Full or PPO-Full under the same prompt on FrozenLake (App. D.1). A candidate sentence

probe must add GiGPO-Full as the in-print reference and, if affordable, as a second checkpoint. The coverage term is a second mechanism
in the same sentence (checklist 2026-11-01: conjunction headlines); it is filed separately below.

**Gate 5 (durability).** A reading rule for budget sweeps is durable while $\mathrm{Pass}@(k,T)$ sweeps are standard; the FrozenLake
number expires with the authors' v2. Lab-adjacent (a control, not a recipe).

**Gate 6 (ownership, density, race; position clause).** Density of the exact control: 0 occupants. Density of the vocabulary:
budget-aware agent training 8 papers in 12 months (about one per 6 weeks); silent-cap RL-versus-base sweeps 2 (2604.14877, AdaTurn

## 6. Consequence for the probes, and the narrowed headlines

The claim is re-filed as two one-quantifier claims, both tested by the same two rollout sets:

- **CL-W45-03a (prompt term):** "On FrozenLake at 25% of the cap, holding the prompt's budget line at $T_{\max}$ recovers at least half of
  GRPO-Full's lost $\mathrm{Pass}@8$ lead over the base, and GiGPO-Full's lead moves by less than 3 points under the same switch." Kill:
  recovery under one quarter of the lost lead with the band excluding one half.
- **CL-W45-03b (coverage term):** "Under the paper's announced protocol at 25% of the cap, $\mathrm{Pass}@(1,T)$ puts GRPO-Full above the
  base on FrozenLake while $\mathrm{Pass}@(8,T)$ puts it below." Kill: the $K = 1$ gap at or below zero with the band excluding $+3$.

Probe consequences:

- **import:2026-W45-S8xF9 — survives; redesign owed.** Step 0 shrinks from instrumentation to a report: the released evaluator already
  records each rollout's termination `reason` in {success, environment_terminal, budget_exhausted} (the three event types), and the
  tutorial's outputs omit them; the step is to render the three curves from existing records and verify the identity on the scripted
  demo. The wrong-commit definition on FrozenLake (falling into a hole) stands. P1's kill is unchanged. Add the AdaTurn Table 1 row as
  the published silent-cap reference and 2604.14877 Table 2 as the published non-collapse reference, so the FrozenLake curve is read
  against both.
- **C-W45-06 — survives; redesign owed.** The $2 \times 2$ stays; add GiGPO-Full (Figs. 15-16) as the in-print reference row and, within
  the same training budget, a GiGPO checkpoint if the GRPO run finishes under 60 GPU-hours; split the readout into the 03a and 03b
  kills; the premise cell is the 4B GRPO 25% number, to be read off Figure 3 or requested. Its claim sentence's "keeps more than half of
  its full-cap lead" is the 03a quantifier and must be the one reported.
- **import:2026-W45-S8xF4 (orphan) — unchanged, with two new neighbours.** 2610.28409's Trunc-versus-Gate result (letting rollouts

## 7. Checklist lessons (2026-11-08)

- Before proposing a protocol control, read the paper's other-algorithm appendix figures: the same protocol applied to a second
  training arm (2611.06483 Figs. 15-16, App. D.1) is a free partial control of the protocol term, and the claim was filed without it.
- The metric's origin paper carries the metric's full grid: 2604.14877 Table 2 reports $k = 1$ at every $T$ under a silent cap; "the
  paper never reports $\mathrm{Pass}@(1,T)$" is true of the card paper and false of the lane. Read the origin paper's tables before
  writing "unreported".
- A release's rollout schema can already hold the event type the import wants (the repository's `reason` field); open the evaluator
  source before costing instrumentation. Fifth occurrence of "code, curves and labels are not the transcripts", inverted: here the
  labels exist and the paper never reports them.
- The repository's own word for the protocol ("budget-visible") is a role word; it found the decisive neighbour (AdaTurn, "trained as if
  the rollout budget were hidden") that fifteen queries in the claim's and the communities' words had missed.
- A silent-cap datum is domain-dependent: collapse where trajectories are long and the cap binds (AdaTurn), none where the task
  saturates at two rounds (2604.14877). A claim about one environment must name the structural bound at the cap (App. B.3's shortest
  path per map) as part of its premise, not as an overlay.
