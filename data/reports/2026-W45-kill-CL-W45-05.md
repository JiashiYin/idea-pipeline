Glossary (each term once): **prefix cache** — the engine keeps the attention key–value (KV) state of a prompt so a later prompt
that begins with the same tokens re-computes only its new suffix. **Hit rate** — the share of prompt tokens (or of turns) served
from that cache. **Working set** $W$ — the aggregate context of all active sessions, tool-idle ones included; $C$ — the cache
capacity in tokens. **LRU** — least-recently-used eviction. **Stack distance** — the number of distinct other items touched
between two uses of the same item; under LRU an item hits iff its stack distance, in tokens, is below $C$. **Convoy / lockstep** —
a queue that serves every waiting party once per round, so every returning party finds the whole population ahead of it.
**FCFS / FIFO** — first-come-first-served. **LPM** — SGLang's longest-prefix-match policy, which serves the request with the
longest cached prefix first (resident-first). **Trap** — the state after a finite surge in which the hit rate stays low and the
backlog does not drain at a load the engine served before. **Session cap** — admitting at most $n_{\mathrm{cap}}$ sessions at a time.

## 0. Verdict in one paragraph

**Killed as written (Gate 6, occupied on 2026-11-06; Gate 2B, the surprise is the occupant's stated thesis). A footnote-sized
residue survives, not a claim.** The claim says the trapped hit rate at a given $W/C$ is set by dispatch order, not by cache
contents: a saturated FCFS prefill queue puts every session's call behind every other session's, so each prefix's stack distance is
the whole population and nothing hits past $W = C$, and a non-FCFS order at unchanged arrivals restores hits. Valse and Jannesari,
*Inside the Trap: A Renewal Law for Prefix-Cache Collapse in LLM Agent Serving* (2611.08902, posted 2026-11-06, concurrent with
and independent of the card paper), state exactly this: the trapped engine is idealized as "a fluid FIFO server on which every turn
misses and every turn rejoins the tail of the queue (model F)", a model they themselves call "round robin with feedback to the tail
of the queue, which model F is at the level of sessions"; their Proposition 6 proves that "request-level FCFS makes reuse vanish in
congestion whatever the online eviction rule", with the scope sentence "It covers SGLang's stock LPM through its 128-request
fallback, but not a pure resident-first order"; their sustaining-variable analysis reads the trap as a stack-distance effect ("Turn
latency, not tool time, carries the gap past $R_M$", $R_M$ the pool's turnover time); and their Appendix E.8 ran the claim's arm (d)
— LPM with the 128-request fallback disabled — in an established trap at unchanged arrivals: "LPM without the fallback did not
collapse: its token hit fraction over the evaluation window was 0.59 and 0.42, against 0.000", and "It is not a fix: no run
recovered by the registered rule". The dispersed branch is occupied too: Yao et al. (2609.02027, 2026-09-02) derive the mean-field
LRU hit ratio of multi-turn sessions under i.i.d. inter-turn gaps and name the regime they exclude ("The resulting end-to-end (E2E)
latency may then substantially affect the observed prompt interarrival times"). The card paper itself isolates the ordering lever
in simulation ("To isolate engine ordering, we keep predicted admission but remove the priority from both engine queues: goodput
falls to 0.81–0.95 with the host tier, 0.31–0.34 with GPU-only caching, 0.36 during recovery"), and its "cache hysteresis" number
is a simulated one ("the simulated hit rate remains at 0.26–0.37 after collapse"), which the claim record calls measured (Gate 0
defect). What nobody has run is the arm that carries no prefix information at all — SGLang's shipped `random` dispatch order, or a
one-shot phase jitter — which would separate desynchronization from LPM's prefix matching; 2611.08902 says so in its own words
("the test does not say which of the two prevents the collapse"; "We do not claim that LPM fails, and we have not isolated why it
recovered"). That is a one-day experiment on the occupants' registered harness, an answer to their appendix, and not a claim.

## 3. The two closest papers' limitation and scope text (verbatim, grep-verified)

**2611.08902, sec. 7 Limitations.** "Model F is an idealization: it assumes that every turn misses, that requests complete in FIFO
order and that a finished turn rejoins at once. The engine runs about 28 requests concurrently at 300k (14–90 across our pools),
vLLM preempts, and hits return as the backlog falls toward the onset, so model F describes the trap, not the transitions into and
out of it. We do not prove that the engine converges to model F". ... "Sessions are synthetic (random tokens, exponential 5 s tool
times, geometric turn counts, fixed tool outputs and output lengths, no shared prefixes), clients never retry or abandon, and real
agent traces have tool times far shorter and more dispersed [97]. Engines are colocated, with one GPU each, dense 7–8B models and
pools of 150k to 956k tokens; disaggregated serving, offloading, larger models and parallelism are untested." **App. E.14, scope of
Proposition 6:** "what the proposition adds is that request-level FCFS makes reuse vanish in congestion whatever the online
eviction rule." ... "A pure resident-first order, such as LPM without the fallback, violates (A2) outright: a returning session
whose KV is still resident is admitted ahead of the queue." **App. E.8:** "With the fallback off, LPM keeps both its ordering and
the LRU touch that its prefix matching gives waiting requests, so the test does not say which of the two prevents the collapse."
... "We do not claim that LPM fails, and we have not isolated why it recovered." **App. E.4:** "That the good state persists after
the cap is removed is hysteresis of the system ... it differs from the cache hysteresis that Lu et al. report in simulation, a lower
hit rate after a collapse at a comparable working set [60]". **Sec. 1:** "Quasi-static models treat it as the falling branch of a
throughput curve ... On our engines, where every trapped turn misses, both statements are wrong."

**2611.02543 (the card), sec. 9.** "All hardware experiments serve the hybrid MoE model Qwen3.6-35B-A3B on SGLang." ... "In colocated
deployments, prefill and decode share compute and memory; the corresponding model bounds would reflect that shared resource pool."
**Sec. 5.2:** "On host-tier 1P3D, the simulated hit rate remains at 0.26–0.37 after collapse at working sets 1.6–1.7 times cache
capacity; no-surge runs at comparable working sets retain 0.81–0.93. This is cache hysteresis: similar demand for cached context
produces different hit rates because the cached contents differ." **Sec. 7.9:** "To isolate engine ordering, we keep predicted
admission but remove the priority from both engine queues: goodput falls to 0.81–0.95 with the host tier, 0.31–0.34 with GPU-only
caching, 0.36 during recovery and 0.55 under sustained overload."

**Third, 2609.02027, sec. B.5.** "In practice, increasing the physical conversation arrival rate proportionally with $N$ may
overload the prefill and decode workers and make computation be the system bottleneck. The resulting end-to-end (E2E) latency may
then substantially affect the observed prompt interarrival times." **Fourth, RR-Evict 2609.32278, sec. 3.3 and 6:** "A deployment
must also account for bursts in the instantaneous active set, which this mean-based model does not capture." ... "Our evaluation is
limited to relatively short idle intervals and prefix caches resident in GPU HBM."
