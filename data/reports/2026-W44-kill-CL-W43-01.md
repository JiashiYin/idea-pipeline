**Terms, one gloss each.** *Solver iteration cap* (`option/iterations`): the maximum number of passes the engine's
constraint solver runs per physics step to find contact forces; MuJoCo's default is 100, MuJoCo Playground ships 1
(Go1) and 5 (Leap hand). *Trade clause*: the first half of the headline, that caps buy speed at the cost of physics.
*Exploit clause*: the second half, that a policy trained under the cap earns return from the cap's errors — return it
loses when the physics is solved properly. *Interaction term*: with $J_e(\pi)$ the return of policy $\pi$ evaluated
in engine setting $e$, $\pi_{\text{cap}}$ and $\pi_{\text{conv}}$ the policies trained at the cap and at convergence,
$$\Delta = \big[J_{\text{conv}}(\pi_{\text{conv}}) - J_{\text{conv}}(\pi_{\text{cap}})\big] - \big[J_{\text{cap}}(\pi_{\text{conv}}) - J_{\text{cap}}(\pi_{\text{cap}})\big]$$
is the cap-isolated exploit (the probe P-W43-01-2x2's estimand). *Simulation optimization bias (SOB)*: Muratore et
al.'s 2019 name for the gap between a policy's return in the simulator it was optimised in and its return elsewhere —
the optimiser's exploitation of simulator error, estimated from held-out domains. *Contact artefact*: SimuGuard's
2026 name for motion that starts at a contact and that neither the robot's contact nor gravity accounts for.
*Depenetration velocity*: PhysX's cap on how fast it pushes overlapping bodies apart. *Sim-to-sim*: evaluating a policy
in a second engine as a transfer proxy. *Domain randomisation (DR)*: training over randomly perturbed physical
parameters so the policy does not depend on any one setting.

## 0. Verdict in one paragraph

**Killed as written.** The exploit clause, in the generality the headline states it, is the field's stated knowledge
and has been for seven years: Peng et al. 2017 ("behaviours developed by agents in simulation are often specific to the
characteristics of the simulator"), Muratore–Gienger–Peters 2019 (SOB: "the optimizer exploits modeling errors of the
simulator", with an estimator and a stopping rule), Kadian et al. 2019 (agents "exploit simulator imperfections,
abusing collision dynamics to 'slide' along walls", SRCC 0.18), and, for contact-rich GPU simulation specifically,
IndustReal/SAPU 2023 ("spurious interpenetrations between assets are unavoidable, especially when executing in
real-time ... an agent can exploit inaccurate collision dynamics to maximize reward"); the in-window theory papers
(2605.15960, 2605.29032) restate it for learned models, and PolySim 2510.01708 measures the whole-engine version
(+52.8 points of sim-to-sim success from training across engines) while naming the "contact solver" as one source of
the bias it randomises over. A ruler whose expected reading is what everyone already believes is a service (Gate 2B,
`taste.md` §3). The trade clause is the owners' own text, confirmed again this run: the MJX page says the caps "should
be brought down to just low enough that the simulation remains stable. Accurate solver forces are not so important in
reinforcement learning in which domain randomization is often used to add noise to physics for sim-to-real." What is
*not* in print is narrower than the headline and is now contested by a three-day-old paper: SimuGuard 2610.41857
(2026-10-30) records every physics step of policy rollouts on LIBERO, RoboTwin, RoboCasa and RoboDojo, flags contact
artefacts, replays bit for bit and reruns "with one simulation parameter changed: the mass of the manipulated object,
the number of solver iterations, the maximum speed at which PhysX separates overlapping bodies ..., or the time constant
of MuJoCo's contacts" — and finds that "Mass and solver iterations leave the share of thrown poses at 90%" while a
collision-detection constant is the cause. That is the first published rerun of policy rollouts under a solver-iteration
change, and its sign is against the headline's attribution: the artefact that decided those episodes came from the
collision pipeline, not from the iteration cap, and more iterations made the thrown can faster, not slower. SimuGuard
audits frozen policies at evaluation time and never retrains, so the train-in / evaluate-in interaction $\Delta$ at
shipped caps remains unmeasured on every engine; that cap-isolated, quantified, task-class-contrasted measurement (the
`proposed_headline` recorded on 2026-10-21) **survives weakly** as a new claim, and only with a collision-pipeline arm
added to P-W43-01-2x2 so that a non-zero $\Delta$ is attributed to the cap and not to the geometry constant SimuGuard
found. Pre-registered expected results (Go1 $\Delta \approx 0$, Leap $\Delta > 0$) equal, respectively, the engine
owners' assumption and SAPU's; the only pre-registered surprise left is the attribution. Verdict is the same from the
solo and the lab position; the lab position turns the negative result into a documentation page, which is a service.

## 1. The question at the concept level

The headline carries two clauses with different owners. The trade clause ("engines trade physics for speed through
caps") is asserted by the engine builders themselves (§5) and measured on fixed scenes by 2304.06372; it is not news.
The exploit clause ("policies trained in them exploit the difference") is the claim's only possible headline, and the
question for this search is whether anyone has shown in print that policies exploit *cap-induced* artefacts — the
solver's unconverged impulse — as opposed to simulator error in general. The searches below were run in the engine
builders' words (solver iterations, substeps, convergence, overflow, depenetration, contact offset) and in the
robot-RL words (simulator exploitation, sim-to-sim, reality gap, overoptimisation, interpenetration, low-fidelity,
simplified simulator, fidelity curriculum, domain randomisation of solver parameters).

## 4. Pre-window precedents (named with their tricks)

- **1710.06537 Peng et al. (2017)** — randomise the simulator's dynamics during training so the policy cannot depend on
  any one setting; the paper that made "policies exploit the simulator" and "DR is the answer" the field's default.
- **1907.04685 SPOTA (2019)** — simulation optimisation bias as a named quantity with an estimator from held-out domains
  and a stopping criterion; the exploit clause as a measurement, seven years before this claim.
- **1912.06321 Kadian et al. (2019)** — sim-vs-real correlation; agents exploit collision dynamics; tuning the simulator
  restores predictivity.
- **2305.17110 IndustReal / SAPU (2023)** — interpenetration is exploited in GPU assembly RL; filter and down-weight.
- **2304.06372 Le Lidec et al. (2023)** — iterations-to-convergence and self-consistency against tolerance and time step
  on fixed robot scenes; the trade clause measured without a policy.
- **1910.12284 (2019)** — fidelity toggled per object for speed with a quality check; the speed clause's ancestor.
- **2010.04304 (2020)** and **2011.02404 (2020)** — environment-design and DR ablations with sim-to-sim as the test.

## 5. The owners' text (fetched 2026-11-01)

- MJX performance page: "The iterations and ls_iterations attributes—which control solver and linesearch iterations,
  respectively—should be brought down to just low enough that the simulation remains stable. Accurate solver forces
  are not so important in reinforcement learning in which domain randomization is often used to add noise to physics
  for sim-to-real. The NEWTON Solver delivers excellent convergence with very few (often just one) solver iterations,
  and performs well on GPU."
- MJWarp page: "Reducing these limits too much may prevent the constraint solver from converging and can lead to
  inaccurate or unstable simulation." and "With MJWarp, in contrast, once all worlds have converged the solver can early
  exit and avoid unnecessary computation. As a result, the values of these settings have comparatively less impact on
  performance." and "In practice, selecting good values typically involves trial-and-error."

The first sentence is the field's assumption the exploit clause has to beat, written by the engine owner. The MJWarp
sentence weakens the speed half of the trade clause on the newest engine: the cap is a JAX-era throughput knob; under
early exit the time cost is the slowest world's convergence (the lockstep mechanism of C-W43-27), not the cap.

## 6. Limitation quotes of the two closest papers (grep-verified in the fetched text)

- **SimuGuard 2610.41857, §5 Limitations:** "The closed loop measurement in the repaired engine covers two policies on
  five tasks with 20 seeds each, and the difference in success on LingBot-VA's 83 shared seeds is not significant on
  its own (p=0.11), unlike the difference in artifacts. We changed one constant inside PhysX 5.3.1 and did not run
  RoboTwin on a complete later version, in which the steps after the faulty distance are unchanged. On Stack Bowls
  Three, 4 of the 21 deep contacts we traced are real penetrations and one is not reproduced outside the simulator. The
  cause of the false contact on RoboDojo is not located, the MuJoCo benchmarks were audited at a smaller scale, and the
  detector's precision was measured on one task." And on the cap, §D.2: "Mass and solver iterations leave the share of
  thrown poses at 90%; only the maximum depenetration velocity, the parameter PhysX's documentation names for bodies
  that start in overlap (NVIDIA Corporation, 2024), changes it." §D.3: "More solver iterations give 86.7% (Wilcoxon
  signed rank test on success per seed, p=0.17)" against 74.4% as run. §2: prior work "neither connects an anomalous
  contact in a rollout to the outcome of that rollout."
- **IndustReal 2305.17110, §IV (SAPU) and §VIII:** "In contact-rich simulators, spurious interpenetrations between
  assets are unavoidable, especially when executing in real-time (Figure S15). Unfortunately, in simulation for RL, an
  agent can exploit inaccurate collision dynamics to maximize reward, learning policies that are unlikely to transfer
  to the real world [47]." (Reference 47 is SPOTA.) Limitations: "our sim-to-real framework currently relies on a
  high-accuracy simulator ... for some tasks of even higher complexity (e.g., assembly of elastic cables onto pulleys),
  the simulator may neither be fundamentally accurate nor efficient enough to smoothly train and deploy policies to the
  real world."
- **PolySim 2510.01708** (third, for the cross-engine measure): "even extensive randomization is structurally limited:
  the transition model remains that of the chosen simulator, determined by its modeling choices, contact solver, time
  integrator, and actuator models." and "Residual mismatches that arise from different contact solvers or integrators
  are recorded as fixed engine meta-parameters to provide controlled dynamics variation rather than uncontrolled
  drift." No limitations section; "future work will expand it with more simulators and diverse tasks like manipulation."

Neither closest paper leaves the cap-isolated training-time exploit open in so many words: SimuGuard's open items are
its own scale and the RoboDojo cause; IndustReal's are assets, slip and friction. The gap is real but it is not one
either group has asked for.

## 7. Why has this not been done

Enabling pieces, dated: MuJoCo Playground (2025-02) shipped per-task caps with public checkpoints and minutes-scale
training, so the train-in / evaluate-in matrix costs GPU-hours; MuJoCo 3.11.0 (2026-07-27) shipped the solver
certificate and per-step statistics; SimuGuard (2026-10-30) made per-step recording, bit-for-bit replay and
one-parameter reruns a published practice with adapters for four benchmarks and PhysX and MuJoCo. Why the matrix is
still unrun: the engine owner's page says solver accuracy does not matter under DR, the robot-RL community's exploit
