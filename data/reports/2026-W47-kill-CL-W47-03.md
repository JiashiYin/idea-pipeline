Glossary (each term once). **Turn** — one agent reply (a batch of shell commands) plus the output the harness returns.
**Cap $T$** — a turn limit stated to the agent, from the grid $\mathcal{T} = \{1, 2, 3, 5, 10, 20, 40, 80\}$. **Depth label $D$** —
the smallest cap under which at least one of up to two runs of a measuring model passes, minimised over the model set:
$D_\tau = \min_m \min\{T \in \mathcal{T} : \max_{r \le 2} X_{\tau m T r} = 1\}$, where $X_{\tau m T r} \in \{0, 1\}$ is the pass bit of
task $\tau$ under model $m$, cap $T$, run $r$. **Floor** — the grid's bottom ($D \le 3$), where 241 of 401 solved existing tasks sit
at $D = 1$ and no model can lower the label. **Facet** — one condition of the reading (model, cap, run, harness, provider).
**G-study** — a random-effects decomposition of a reading into a component for the object (the task) and one per facet and
interaction. **Index of dependability $\Phi$** — $\sigma^2(\tau) / (\sigma^2(\tau) + \sigma^2(\Delta))$, where $\sigma^2(\tau)$
is the task's component and $\sigma^2(\Delta)$ the sum of every other component: the reliability of a label compared with a cut
score. **Newly-informed turn** — a turn whose commands use a string that first appeared in the previous output (the probe
C-W47-06's unit). **Decision density $\rho$** — 2606.22164's quantity, $\rho = \bar C / T$, the expected number of critical
states (turns whose action changes the return distribution) over the trajectory length. **Lowerable label** — a released label
above the floor, which a further model can lower. **Run residual $\sigma^2(\epsilon)$** — the component from both runs failing at
a sufficient cap, serving and timing. **Specialization** — 2611.08285's word for the model-by-issue interaction.

## 0. Verdict in one paragraph

**Killed, Gate 2B (the surprise equals the field's stated knowledge); the second clause also dies on the paper's own numbers.**
The headline has three clauses. The direction of all three — the depth label and the turn count read the measuring model as well
as the task — is the seed paper's own text: "the turn count largely reflects the habits of the model, not only the demands of
the task" (abstract); "$D$ depends on the models, the harness and the single state $e_0$, so it is an estimate rather than a
bound" (sec. 2); "a model that merges poorly overestimates the depth" (sec. 3.1); the release README: "$D$ is an empirical
estimate: it depends on the models, the harness, and the task environment." The third clause's concept, a turn that draws on
the previous output as the unit of depth, is in print twice in the nearest community: TerminalHorizon (2610.13166, App. F)
filters training trajectories with a reviewer told that a trajectory's "value lies in how interactions inform subsequent
decisions, not in trajectory length, command count, or output volume" and that "Waiting, repeated polling, and mechanical loops
receive no additional credit merely for increasing the number of turns"; and 2606.22164 defines decision density, "the fraction
of turns whose actions affect the return", and names its estimation on agentic benchmarks as "the most direct" next step. The
second clause (a four-model minimum lowers at least 30% of the released labels) fails at $0 on App. E.1: 455 labels are
printed, 241 are pinned at the floor, one extra model lowered 44 (9.7% of released; 25% of the lowerable existing labels and 4 of
54 of the paper's own deep tasks); two further models at the observed rates lower 17% of released labels under independence,
and reaching 30% needs a per-model rate of 0.29 on a stratum where the observed rate is 0.07. The first clause ($\Phi < 0.5$)
is the only unoccupied number, and its mechanism is contradicted by the nearest decomposition in print: on SWE-bench with
repeated runs, 2611.08285 finds run-to-run variation at 42% of outcome variance and the model-by-issue interaction at 4.6%, so
the import's expected ordering (model-by-task dominant) has one published data point against it and none for it, and the
paper's own App. E.2 already names the run residual as the error that "could produce a correlation between $D$ and difficulty
that does not come from depth". What survives is a service, not a claim: a G-study of $D$ on the 54 deep tasks that reports
$\Phi$ and the panel — which the owners can run in a revision — and it cannot start today, because the dataset link returns 404
and the release ships no runs (sec. 5).

## 3. The closest papers' own text (verbatim, grep-verified)

**2611.01927.** Abstract: "We find, however, that the turn count largely reflects the habits of the model, not only the demands
of the task, and that the labels barely track how often models pass." Sec. 2: "Unlike the idealized $D^{*}$, $D$ depends on the
models, the harness and the single state $e_0$, so it is an estimate rather than a bound." Sec. 3.1: "Once told the cap, models
differ widely in how far they merge commands into fewer turns, and a model that merges poorly overestimates the depth. We
therefore measure every task with a set of models $\mathcal{M}$ and take the smallest estimate". Sec. 4.2: "runs on these tasks
spend their turns waiting for slow commands, reading long outputs and recovering after version defaults, old command-line
interfaces or truncated files mislead the model." App. E.1: "GLM-5.3-Flash lowers $D$ on 40 of the 160 existing tasks it was run
on, 32 of them by one cap … of the 40 solved existing tasks whose estimate from DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash alone is at least 5,
GLM-5.3-Flash brings 11 below 5. On our tasks, it was run on all 54 solved tasks and lowers $D$ on 4, each by one cap and none
below 20, and the Spearman correlation is 0.95. Measuring with DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash alone would thus make the existing datasets
look deeper, not shallower." App. E.2: "When DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash rarely passes a task, both runs at a sufficient cap can fail and
raise $D$ by a cap; such tasks may also be harder for other models, so this error could produce a correlation between $D$ and
difficulty that does not come from depth. … We do not control for $R$ instead: a deep task has many steps that can each go wrong,
so part of the variation in $R$ may come from depth itself." Release README: "$D$ is an empirical estimate: it depends on the
models, the harness, and the task environment." and "An agent may issue many commands separately even when it could batch them
into one turn."

**2611.08285, sec. IV.** "We decompose the variation in outcomes across repeated runs into parts due to the issue, the model,
their combination (specialization), and run-to-run variation. Specialization accounts for only 4.6%, against 53% for issue
difficulty and 42% for run-to-run variation." … "The same model often solves an issue in some runs and fails it in others; this
run-to-run variation accounts for 42% of the variation in outcomes, far more than specialization (4.6%; RQ2)." Threats to
validity: "The evidence comes mainly from released labels on one benchmark and scaffold, from trajectory-based predictors only,
and partly from the repeated-run pilot with older models and a router with shallow issue features." Future plans: "we will
collect repeated runs of recent models across more benchmarks and scaffolds, so that run-to-run variation can be separated from
issue difficulty and model ability."

**2610.13166, App. F (the gpt-5.6-luna reviewer's criteria).** "Its value lies in how interactions inform subsequent decisions,
not in trajectory length, command count, or output volume. Waiting, repeated polling, and mechanical loops receive no
additional credit merely for increasing the number of turns. Infrastructure operations used solely to connect to or manage the
execution environment likewise do not count toward problem-solving depth." … "Do not judge training value solely from trajectory
length, command count, the presence of mistakes, or final task success."

**2606.22164, Limitations.** "First, no experiments are run on real LLM agents … the magnitude of $\rho$ in such systems have not
been measured. Second, routine states are policy-dependent and not directly observable. Decision density can still be used
conceptually, and a quantitative route is to probe a base agent through rollouts and estimate which action alternatives shift
the downstream return distribution." Future work: "The most direct is to estimate decision density, SNR ratios, and training
gaps on agentic benchmarks with learned critics."

## 4. Gates

**Gate 0 (premise hygiene) — flagged, two defects.** (i) "Released labels" are not released: the header's Dataset link

lowerable labels and 7.4% of the paper's own deep labels (sec. 0 arithmetic). Conditioning set, now read from the release:
`solvers.py` passes no sampling temperature (`llm_call_kwargs` carries only the provider pin), so both measuring models run at

`allow_fallbacks: False`) and GLM-5.3-Flash to `Fireworks`, so a run's serving stack is one of three and not recorded in the

**Gate 5 (durability) — as graded; not reached.**

## 6. Why has this not been done, and why now

Enabling pieces with dates: the first protocol-defined depth label (2611.01927, 2026-11-01); G-theory already carried into
agent evaluation for scores (2608.11323, 2610.00651, 2609.27787); a repeated-run task-model-run decomposition on SWE-bench

the piece as a service.

## 7. Residue

A watch item, not a claim: **"On 2611.01927's 54 deep tasks, the same-model two-run label agreement at $D \ge 40$ is under
0.7, and the run residual, not the model-by-task interaction, is the largest component above the floor"** — the import's
