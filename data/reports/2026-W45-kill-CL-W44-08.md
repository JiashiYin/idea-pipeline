**Terms, one gloss each.** *Solver iteration cap* (`option/iterations`): the maximum number of passes MuJoCo's
constraint solver makes per physics step to find contact forces; the engine default is 100; MuJoCo Playground ships 1
for Go1 and 5 for the Leap hand. *Converged engine*: the same model stepped with the cap lifted (100 iterations,
tolerance $10^{-8}$) so the contact forces are solved to tolerance. *2x2 matrix*: train a policy under each setting,
evaluate each policy under each setting. With $J_e(\pi)$ the return of policy $\pi$ in engine setting $e$,
$\pi_{\text{cap}}$ and $\pi_{\text{conv}}$ the policies trained at the cap and converged,
$$\Delta = \big[J_{\text{conv}}(\pi_{\text{conv}}) - J_{\text{conv}}(\pi_{\text{cap}})\big] - \big[J_{\text{cap}}(\pi_{\text{conv}}) - J_{\text{cap}}(\pi_{\text{cap}})\big]$$
is the claim's estimand. *Exploit cell*: the half of $\Delta$ that measures exploitation by the cap-trained policy,
$G_{\text{cap}} = J_{\text{cap}}(\pi_{\text{cap}}) - J_{\text{conv}}(\pi_{\text{cap}})$: return the policy earns at the cap and
loses when the physics is solved properly. *Frozen-policy rerun*: evaluating an already-trained policy under a changed
engine setting without retraining; it measures $G_{\text{cap}}$ when the policy was cap-trained. *Collision pipeline*: the
geometry and distance code that decides which bodies touch and how deep, before the solver computes forces. *Sim-to-sim
check*: evaluating a policy in a second simulator (or second configuration) as a transfer proxy. *Low-fidelity (LF) /
high-fidelity (HF)*: MFPG-PPO's names for a cheap simplified simulator and the full one. *Harness*: the evaluation
program that rebuilds the model and steps the physics outside the training code.

## 3. The two closest papers' limitation text (verbatim, grep-verified in the fetched text)

**2610.32277, §3 Evaluation harness and §4.1:** "The harness builds the physics model from the robot description with
MuJoCo's specification API, reproducing the training model's floor, contact parameters, 5 ms timestep, solver settings and
implicit-fast integrator." ... "Restoring the training model's settings one at a time located the cause (Table 2): the
robot description has no ground plane, and the harness stepped physics at 2 ms instead of 5 ms. Contact dimension, solver
iterations, friction and contact priority each moved the error by less than 0.006 rad." Abstract: "We also show that the
first sim-to-sim gap we saw came from the harness, a missing floor and a wrong timestep, and not from the physics engines."
Limitations (§5): "All results are in simulation, with idealized actuators (PD with torque limits). One robot, five motions
recovered from third-party video, three training seeds per arm for the mjlab policies and a single run for the PhysX
policy. The seeds of an arm continue from the same base checkpoint, so the seed spread understates the variation that
retraining from scratch would add." ... "The physical robot's command latency has not yet been measured; that measurement,
and deployment, are our next steps." Nothing in the paper asks for a retrain at the harness's solver setting; the open items
are hardware latency and deployment.

**2610.02505 MFPG-PPO, §5 and Appendix:** "The LF simulation simplifies the robot collision model, coarsens the physics time
step, reduces the number of physics solver iterations, and disables self-collisions." Table (Appendix, locomotion
settings): H1 HF/LF — physics time step 0.002/0.01 s, physics steps per action 10/2, position solver iterations 4/2,
velocity solver iterations 4/0, self-collisions enabled/disabled, collision geometry full/simplified; ANYmal-D HF/LF —
0.002/0.0025 s, 10/8, 4/2, 0/0, enabled/disabled, full/simplified. Figure 1 caption: "PPO LF-Only (gray) trains on abundant
LF data alone and is evaluated in the HF environment." §5.2: "PPO LF-Only transfers poorly on half of the seeds, and even the
seeds that transfer are brittle, with roughly twice the cross-episode return variability of MFPG-PPO-FR." §5.3: "LF-to-HF
transfer is easy on ANYmal-D, where PPO LF-Only matches the best methods, but hard on H1, where transfer is poor and
seed-sensitive." Future work (§6): "Three directions call for future research." — estimator bias from re-synchronisation,
raising cross-fidelity correlation, and real-to-sim tools for building coupled LF simulators. The per-knob split of the LF
bundle is not among them; the paper never says which of the four simplifications carries the H1 transfer loss.

## 4. Why has this not been done, and why now

Enabling pieces, dated: MuJoCo Playground (2025-02) shipped the caps with checkpoints and minutes-scale training;
MuJoCo 3.11.0 (2026-07-27) the solver certificate and per-step statistics; SimuGuard (2026-10-30) the per-step audit with
one-parameter reruns; and now 2610.32277 (2026-10-23) the verified-harness protocol — rebuild the model in CPU MuJoCo,
restore settings one at a time, read the knob's effect from a table — which is the attribution arm the parent's redesign
asked for, published two weeks ago for free. The matrix itself costs 6–14 GPU-hours on public assets. Why it is unrun: the
three communities that could run it each already hold the answer and now hold a datum for it. The engine owners' tuning page
says solver accuracy is subordinate to domain-randomisation noise; the sim-to-sim community's one one-at-a-time attribution
on MuJoCo found the solver rows flat and the time step decisive; the simulator-audit community's one rerun under a
solver-iteration change found the collision pipeline decisive and the solver's sign against exploitation; the
multi-fidelity-RL community bundles the knob and treats the bundle's transfer loss as the reason for its estimator, not as a
thing to decompose. This is "it is hard because every community believes the answer and has published a measurement that
agrees" — not "no reason found", but a prior strong enough that the measurement's expected reading is the belief.
