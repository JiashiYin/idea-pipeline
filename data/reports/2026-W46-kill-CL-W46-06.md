Glossary (each term once). **Batch makespan $T$** — wall-clock time from the first task's start to the last task's completion.
**Environment capacity $C_E$** — tool jobs the harness runs at once (MCP-Atlas live in the card paper: 3). **Sample concurrency
$C_S$** — admitted tasks. **Chain** — one task's trajectory of $n_k$ turns, each an inference segment $\tau_{k,m}$ (no slot held)
followed by a tool segment $g_{k,m}$ (one slot held). **Critical path $\mathrm{CP}_k = \sum_m (\tau_{k,m} + g_{k,m})$** — chain $k$'s
duration with no waiting. **Slot work $W = \sum_k \sum_m g_{k,m}$**. **Makespan lower bound $T_{\mathrm{LB}} = \max\{W / C_E,\ \max_k
\mathrm{CP}_k\}$** — no dispatch order finishes before the slots have done all the work or the longest chain has run. **FCFS** —
first-come-first-served. **CAAS** — the card paper's dispatch key (arrival band, most completed turns, shortest mean inference time,
FCFS). **Headroom $H = T_{\mathrm{FCFS}} / T_{\mathrm{LB}} - 1$** — an upper bound on the share of makespan any order can remove.
**Tail interval $L_5 = t_{100} - t_{95}$** — time between 95% and 100% of tasks completing. **Work-conserving** — no slot idles while a
job is ready; **Graham's bound** — any work-conserving list schedule finishes within the longest chain of $W / C_E$.

## 3. The two closest papers' limitation and scope text (verbatim, grep-verified)

**2611.16661, sec. 5.3 (the plateau):** "Admitting more samples allows inference and environment-interaction stages to overlap across
trajectories, yet the limited environment-execution capacity constrains the release of subsequent inference work. Additional samples
may then spend more time waiting for environment interaction rather than supplying concurrent inference work, so the makespan gains
level off." **Sec. 5.3 (the lever):** "it reduces makespan relative to FCFS by 1.9% at $C_E = 3$ and 3.5% at $C_E = 5$"; "These gains
come from changing job dispatch order, not service times." **Sec. 5.4 (the tail share):** "Broader admission lets shorter trajectories
finish while longer ones continue, so the optimized tail is more concentrated in long trajectories. ... so $L_5$ alone should not be
used to compare overall execution efficiency across different admission policies." **Sec. 5.1 (replay assumption):** "Stage
dependencies and environment durations remain fixed, while inference latency and cache reuse arise from actual serving." **App. A.2:**
services "may degrade under high concurrency or encounter upstream rate limits such as HTTP 429 errors." Not in the paper: a bound, a
hindsight order, a chain-length distribution, a spread over the three runs.

**2611.17294 Metal Sandbox, sec. 7:** "Replay estimates scheduling effects while holding model behavior and phase durations fixed. ...
It cannot predict changes in agent behavior caused by different tool feedback, or changes in kernel quality, training outcomes, or
deployment reliability. Eight trajectories have less accumulated tool waiting and eight have more, even though mean tool waiting and
batch elapsed time improve." **Sec. 3.3:** per-node queues balance by migration "at the cost of not enforcing a global arrival order."
**Sec. 5.6:** "Subtracting each session's recorded tool waiting while retaining all other phase durations gives a mean elapsed time
$T_0 = 24.12$ minutes." **Sec. 5.7:** "dynamic-scheduling replay reduces mean tool waiting from 21.0 to 17.1 minutes (18.8%) and batch
elapsed time from 67.6 to 52.5 minutes (22.3%)"; App. B: "All configurations and trajectories are retained in the artifact." The batch
bound $\max\{W / 4, \max_k T_{0,k}\}$ and the share of the 22.3% any schedule could reach are not computed.

## 5. Gate-by-gate verdict (claim level), with the position clause

- **Gate 0 — flag.** Premise numbers fetched and verbed (Table 3: live Opt-CAAS makespan 163.0 and 128.5 min, $L_5$ 66.0 and 35.3 min,
  three-run means, no spread; replay CAAS 1.9% and 3.5%). The flag is the quantifier: for any work-conserving dispatch of chains on
  $C_E$ identical slots, at every instant either all slots are busy or the longest unfinished chain advances, so
  $$T_{\mathrm{FCFS}} \leq \frac{W}{C_E} + \max_k \mathrm{CP}_k, \qquad H \leq \frac{\max_k \mathrm{CP}_k}{T_{\mathrm{LB}}}.$$
  "Within a tenth" is decided by $W / 3$ and the longest chain's own duration before any replay; with 167 chains per slot and 7–12 in
  inference at a time (Table 3), the slots stay busy until a handful of chains remain and the probe measures an end effect whose ceiling
  is one number the authors can print. The cap $C_S = 50$ makes FCFS work-conserving only among admitted chains; that caveat belongs in
  any restatement.
- **Gate 1 — passes on paper** ($0 replay), but the kill clause ($H \geq 0.25$ at $C_E = 3$) is unreachable whenever
  $\max_k \mathrm{CP}_k < 0.25\, T_{\mathrm{LB}}$ — a 40-minute longest chain against a 163-minute batch — so "both outcomes
  informative" needs the longest-chain number first (2026-10-20: every clause of a pre-registered kill must be satisfiable).
- **Gate 2 — fails (Route B).** Route A is not established: no source states "we lack a bound for the environment-side tail", and
