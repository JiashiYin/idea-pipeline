Glossary (each term once): **prefill** — processing the prompt tokens of a request in one parallel pass; compute-bound. **Decode** —
generating output tokens one step at a time; memory-bandwidth-bound. **PD disaggregation** — running prefill and decode on separate
GPU pools and shipping the KV cache (the stored attention state) between them; **colocation** — both phases on the same GPUs.
**Interference** — a prefill batch slowing the decode steps that share its GPUs. **Chunked prefill** — splitting a prefill into pieces
batched with decode steps to shorten each stall. **DP attention** — data-parallel attention ranks that must meet at a synchronised
expert (MoE) phase; a rank doing prefill makes the decoding ranks wait. **Rollout** — the generation stage of RL training.
**Staleness** — how many model versions behind a rollout may run in asynchronous RL. **Overlap statistic $\Omega$** — the claim's
ruler: the share of decode work that happens while some prefill is active, $\Omega = \int \mathbf{1}[\text{prefill active at } t]\,
D(t)\,dt \big/ \int D(t)\,dt$, where $D(t)$ is the number of sequences decoding at time $t$. **Bubble ratio** — PEARL's statistic:
with $f$ the average fraction of trajectories actively decoding (the rest are waiting on tools), $\theta = (1-f)/f$. **Turn** — one
model call of an agent between two tool executions.

## 0. Verdict in one paragraph

**Killed (Gate 2B; Gate 6 partly).** The headline's mechanism half is the source paper's own sentence: Prorsa, explaining why
disaggregation loses on single-turn math and wins on agentic tasks, writes "This is due to much less overlap between prefill and
decode: rollout first prefills all sequences and then executes successive decoding steps" (2610.16592 §5.2), after an abstract that
routes turn count through exactly that mechanism ("multi-turn interactions repeatedly introduce prefill requests, causing prefill
and decode workloads to coexist throughout rollout"). AInfer-PD states the same rule for the in-place alternative ("Asynchronous
trajectories consequently introduce new prefill (P) work while other trajectories remain in decode (D), making P/D coexistence a
persistent property of the rollout", and in its tool-wait sweep "Longer client waits leave less overlap opportunity"). The "not by
turn count" contrast has no occupant on the belief side: no fetched paper says turn count decides the payoff; the agentic-serving
measurements (Agentic AI Workload Characteristics 2605.26297, TurnCost via pressure #9) make turn count a *cost* variable, and
ConServe (2606.01839) explicitly removes the turn as the unit of the disaggregation decision. So the pre-registered surprise equals
the field's stated knowledge (Gate 2B), and the claim was written against a belief nobody holds in print. The ruler slot is partly
occupied too: PEARL's decision engine is not "profiling, not a statistic" as the record says — it is a closed-form makespan comparison
(its eqs. 1–3) whose workload statistic is the fraction of trajectories actively decoding, $f$, refit from telemetry; Basin
(2611.02543, eq. 2) gives the serving side a second regime rule in the same quantity ($C \lesssim D_{\mathrm{KV}}/(2f)$, cache-first
versus decode-first by tool time); Crossflow (2609.27085) sizes the P/D boundary by the uncached-input-to-output token ratio. None of
these is the temporal overlap $\Omega$, so the exact statistic is unclaimed — but a statistic whose expected ordering is the ordering
the source paper already explains is a service, not a paper. What 2611.05991 settles: that cross-engine PD comparisons are confounded
by chunked-prefill defaults (matching one setting removes 78% of the capacity gap), which is a design constraint on any probe here,
not evidence for or against the claim. What survives is one control nobody ran: Prorsa's three workloads confound temporal overlap
with prefill-token share (math: low share *and* phase-locked; agentic: high share *and* spread), so its §5.2 attribution is untested
against the volume reading. **Residue headline:** at matched prefill-to-decode token ratio, phase-locking the turns of a multi-turn
rollout (constant tool time) removes at least half of PD disaggregation's speedup over colocation. One quantifier; footnote-grade
(it tests a sentence the field already accepts); not a new claim unless a lab wants the control for a deployment guide.

## 4. Verdicts by component of the headline

**"set by the measured temporal overlap of prefill with decode".** Occupied as a stated mechanism by Prorsa (§5.2, §3.1, abstract,
conclusion) and AInfer-PD (abstract, RQ5, limitations); formalised as a state-dependent decode service rate by 2602.02987; the
single-engine evidence is P-PAS and DLFP (2609.38386, "changes only prefill work that overlaps active decodes"). Not occupied: the
statistic $\Omega$ as a pre-deployment log computation. But the claim's pre-registered expectation ("$\Omega$ orders all four cells")
is the ordering Prorsa already explains, so the ruler's expected result equals the field's assumption: **Gate 2B fails** (the ruler is
a service). PEARL's decision engine already ships a log-fitted statistic ($f$) for the same decision, and Basin's eq. 2 the serving
analogue, so the ruler slot is dense (Gate 6, partial).

**"not by turn count".** No fetched paper asserts turn count as the rule. Prorsa's abstract routes multi-turn through coexistence;
ConServe makes the turn's unfitness as a decision unit its premise; 2605.26297 and TurnCost (pressure #9) use turn count for cost.
A contrast against an unstated belief cannot carry a surprise; the clause is a strawman, not a finding.

**Consequence, "teams compute the overlap statistic from traces before choosing a topology; rollout-engine docs carry it".** PEARL's
Runtime Tracker and PD Decision Engine are that consequence in a different statistic, shipped in a system paper; Crossflow's
uncached-input-to-output ratio is the fleet operator's descriptor. The docs half is unoccupied and is a service.

## 5. The two closest papers' limitation text (verbatim, grep-verified)

**Prorsa 2610.16592** has no limitations section (the card said so); the sentences that bound its claim are in §3.1, §5.2, §5.4 and §7.
§3.1: "We vary the maximum staleness to capture the effects of asynchronous rollout." ... "For math, prefill is concentrated near the
beginning of each step, primarily due to initial prompt processing and, in asynchronous settings, re-prefill of trajectories carried
over from the previous step." ... "In contrast, the two agentic tasks generate prefill requests throughout the step, and their
prefill-token volumes substantially exceed their decode-token volumes." §3.2: "Chunked prefill (Agrawal et al., 2024) shortens each
stall but cannot eliminate the duration mismatch." §5.2: "In contrast, on the math task, normalized rollout step times range from 0.86
to 1.28 relative to colocation, indicating a slowdown in most evaluated settings. This is due to much less overlap between prefill and
decode: rollout first prefills all sequences and then executes successive decoding steps. For these workloads, PD disaggregation incurs
KV-cache transfer and role-switching overheads that degrade performance." §5.4: "The benefits of role switching depend on workload
characteristics." §7: "Agentic RL introduces continuous prefill requests, making PD disaggregation beneficial for the rollout phase."
(No log statistic is defined anywhere in the paper; the three workloads differ in both temporal profile and prefill-token share.)

**AInfer-PD 2609.00993, §9 Discussion and Limitations:** "AInfer-PD targets rollout workers that keep one model and KV state on the same
devices while P and D coexist. Disaggregation remains preferable when separate phase capacity and KV transfer are acceptable." ...
"We evaluate one- and two-node H20-3E deployments, BF16/FP8, both MoE backends, and MTP on/off; broader scales and hardware remain
open." ... "Live-RL trajectories can diverge and provide operational rather than paired evidence." §7.6 (RQ5): "Longer client waits
leave less overlap opportunity." §7.6: "In a decode-only control, enabling the P/D path with the same kernels lowers request rate by
6.7%, motivating activation only when concurrent P work exists." Abstract: "Asynchronous trajectories consequently introduce new
prefill (P) work while other trajectories remain in decode (D), making P/D coexistence a persistent property of the rollout rather than
a one-time prompt-ingestion event."

**Third, PEARL 2609.35158, §3.2 and §4.3.1:** "First, the preferred execution mode depends on the workload. The best disaggregated
configuration outperforms colocation by 9.7% at BS32, but the advantage narrows to 1.4% at BS80 and reverses to a 6.1% colocation win
at BS96." ... "GPU count alone is insufficient to predict this outcome because performance also depends on PD interference, effective
decode concurrency, KVCache transfers, and pauses for environment interactions." ... "It assumes work conservation: when trajectories
leave decode for environment interactions, prefill consumes the freed GPU capacity." ... "Unlike colocation, disaggregated decode GPUs
cannot reuse idle cycles for prefill, so environment interactions reduce decode throughput. We quantify this effect by the average
fraction of trajectories that are actively decoding, $f$, or equivalently the bubble ratio $\theta=(1-f)/f$." Its colocated makespan
(eq. 1), with $S$ the turns on the slowest trajectory, $B$ trajectories, $N$ workers, $\Delta L$ prompt tokens per turn, $r$ decode tokens
per turn, $t_{\mathrm{prefill}}$ per-token prefill time, $A$ fixed per-iteration cost, $b$ marginal per-sequence cost, $T_{\mathrm{over}}$
switching overhead and $T_{\mathrm{env}}$ environment time:
$$T_{\mathrm{coloc}} = S\Big[\tfrac{B\,\Delta L}{N}\,t_{\mathrm{prefill}} + r\big(A + b\tfrac{B}{N}\big) + T_{\mathrm{over}}\Big] + T_{\mathrm{env}},$$
and the disaggregated decode path (eq. 2), with $N_D$ decode workers:
$$T_{\mathrm{decode}} = S\, r\Big(A + b\,\tfrac{B}{N_D(1+\theta)}\Big)(1+\theta).$$
The colocated term is additive in prefill and decode time with no interference or DP-attention idle term, which is why PEARL's rule and
Prorsa's mechanism can both be in print without either defining $\Omega$.

## 6. Why not done (the $\Omega$ ruler with the crossing of overlap and prefill share)

Not done as a statistic because the two groups that could have done it answered the decision differently: Prorsa by naming the
mechanism and building a switcher, PEARL by a fitted closed-form model with $f$. The crossing control (same prefill-token share,
different phase alignment) is missing because every published RL workload confounds the two; no reason beyond "nobody needed it to
ship". Enabling pieces exist and are dated: SGLang single-node PD disaggregation with Mooncake/NIXL transfer (2025); public tool-time
traces (TraceLab, 2026-06; SWE-agent `.traj` `execution_time`); the time-ordered replay simulator pattern (2609.34663, 2026-09); the
chunked-prefill matching rule (2611.05991, 2026-11-04). None of them is a reason the question was *unanswerable* before; the answer was
written by the source paper in October. "Why now" for the residue: the control is cheap because the hardware and traces are public.

## 8. Killing-experiment check (for the residue)

Restated: on one 8-GPU node (SGLang, Qwen3-30B-A3B FP8, chunk size and prefix cache held fixed across arms per 2611.05991), replay the
same SWE-agent trajectories twice — natural tool times from the logs, and constant tool time equal to their mean, so that prefill-token

## 10. Checklist lessons (2026-11-08)

- A headline of the form "set by mechanism M, not by X" must quote the paper that asserts X. Here no paper asserted turn count; the
  source paper's own abstract routed turn count through M and its §5.2 named M. Grep the source paper's body (not only its abstract)
  for the mechanism word before grading Gate 2B — Prorsa's "overlap" sentence was in §5.2 and invisible to an abstract query.
- "Answers by profiling, not by a statistic" is checked against the system's decision-engine equations before it enters
  `current_belief`: PEARL's eqs. 1–3 carry a named workload statistic ($f$, $\theta$) refit from telemetry; the record's sentence was
  false and carried the claim through two ranking rounds.
- When a source paper attributes a reversal to a mechanism and all its workloads confound that mechanism with a volume ratio, the
  residue is the crossing control, not the mechanism restated as a ruler; and a cell where the two readings agree (prefill share near
  zero) cannot separate them and is dropped from the design.
- Cross-engine or cross-topology serving comparisons hold chunked-prefill size, prefix cache and run length fixed (2611.05991: one
  default setting accounted for 78% of a "cache" gap); write the matched settings into the probe before costing it.
