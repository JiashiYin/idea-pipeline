Glossary (each term once): **OPD** — on-policy distillation: the student samples its own rollouts and is trained to match a frozen
teacher's per-token distribution on them. **Self-GRPO teacher** — the student's own GRPO checkpoint used as that teacher. **$G$** —
rollouts per prompt; **$P$** — prompts per optimizer step; the per-step rollout batch is $B = PG$. **FLOP ledger** — the card paper's
cost rule, $10 N_s$ per generated token for GRPO and $10 N_s + 2 N_t$ for OPD, with $N_s$ and $N_t$ the student's and teacher's
parameter counts. **Node-hours** — wall-clock on one 8-GPU node. **$t_{\mathrm{step}}(B)$** — the time of one decode step (one token
for every active sequence) at active batch $B$. **Decode-bound / memory-bound** — a step whose time is set by bytes read from GPU
memory (weights plus KV cache), not by arithmetic. **KV cache** — the per-token key and value vectors kept in GPU memory so that each
decode step reads them instead of recomputing attention. **Roofline** — the model that puts a step's time at the larger of its
bytes-over-bandwidth and its FLOPs-over-peak. **Time-to-target** — wall-clock until a validation accuracy is first reached.
**Critical generation batch** — the smallest rollout batch that realizes most of the attainable generation throughput.

## 0. Verdict in one paragraph

**Killed (Gate 2B: the surprise is the occupant's stated thesis; Gate 6: mechanism, two-term cost model and GRPO measurement in
print since 2026-08-29; Gate 0: the headline's mechanism clause is false at the pair's response length). A footnote-sized residue
survives — the node-hour row for one published pair — and it is not a claim.** The claim's substance is that a FLOP saving bought by
shrinking the per-step rollout batch does not appear on the clock because decode time is nearly flat in batch; its ceiling is a two-term
cost model that predicts wall-clock across recipes and batch shapes. Li et al., *When Do Larger Batches Help Scale LLM Reinforcement
Learning?* (2608.29296, Tencent Hunyuan, 2026-08-29) state and measure this: "doubling the rollout batch doubles the number of requested
responses, but often increases generation time by substantially less than $2\times$"; the two-term model is their eq. 4,
$t_{\mathrm{gen}}(B) \approx d_0 + d_1 B$ and $t_{\mathrm{train}}(B) \approx \gamma B$ ($d_0$ the batch-independent generation latency,
$d_1$ and $\gamma$ time per response); the decision rule is "a larger batch helps precisely when its throughput gain exceeds its sample
penalty"; the fixed-hardware GRPO measurement is $4\times$ the responses per step for $2.93\times$ the collection time (PPO: $4\times$
for $1.74\times$), with a decode-step Roofline appendix that carries the KV-cache term. What remains of the claim is one pair's number,
and that number is an output of the occupant's model: the card paper's figures fix the step counts (about 200 GRPO steps against about
26 OPD steps at either $G$, since recovery FLOPs are linear in $G$: 0.2, 0.8, 1.6 EFLOP at $G = 1, 4, 8$, Fig. 32), so
$\rho_{G=1} \approx 6.8\, r$ with $r = t_{\mathrm{step}}(512)/t_{\mathrm{step}}(64)$; the ledger's 50 needs $r \approx 7.3$, the claim's 25
needs $r < 3.7$, and the headline's "nearly the same time" asserts $r \approx 1$. The KV-cache read is linear in $B$: for Qwen3-4B (147 KB
per token in bf16) against 8 GB of weights, the Roofline gives $r = 1.9$ at 1,000 tokens of context, $3.2$ at 3,000 and $4.6$ at the
7,168 cap, and the self-GRPO OPD rollouts average 3,741 tokens (Fig. 22). Predicted $\rho_{G=1}$: 15–25, on the claim's own kill line —
a coefficient fit, not a belief test. The direction is conceded in the card paper (App. I: "It takes longer in wall-clock time because it
runs more optimizer steps"); the FLOPs-versus-GPU-hours gap is stated by the source of the 50x (Thinking Machines: "the cost reduction in
GPU hours is closer to 18x" against a 9x FLOP figure); and a published GRPO datum shows an $8\times$ rollout saving arriving as
$4.8\times$ on the clock (2-GRPO, 2510.00977: "12.5% of its total rollouts and 21.0% of its training time"). Nobody has timed the
self-GRPO pair; that row is a replication note or a question to the authors, who hold the node and the App. I number.

## 3. The two closest papers' limitation and scope text (verbatim, grep-verified)

**2608.29296, sec. 7 (closing paragraph).** "Because our experiments retune only the learning rate, future work should derive scaling
rules for other batch-dependent hyperparameters and pursue tighter batch-size invariance as a basis for algorithm design, comparison,
and transfer. It should also measure how the training and generation critical batches shift across models, algorithms, hardware, and
execution modes, and clarify the coupled actor–critic batch scales in PPO." **Sec. 4.2, Definition 3:** "Unlike
$B_{\mathrm{train}}^{\mathrm{crit}}$, $B_{\mathrm{gen}}^{\mathrm{crit}}$ depends on the model, decoding engine, response-length
distribution, and hardware allocation, and must be profiled for the target deployment." **Sec. 5, Fig. 7:** "The curves are schematic
rather than fitted to our experiments." **App. A, Fig. 9:** "The resulting values are analytical per-layer lower bounds. They are not
end-to-end measurements and do not estimate the critical batch." **Sec. 6.1:** "both GRPO and PPO perform one global update per
rollout batch, without minibatch steps or rollout reuse, and both workloads run with asynchronous partial rollouts and streaming
generation."

**2611.08453, App. I.** "On DAPO-Math-17K with a Qwen3-4B-Base student and an external Qwen3-8B teacher, we keep the default batch
size and train at $G{=}1$ for $8\times$ more optimizer steps, which matches the total number of rollouts in the $G{=}8$ run. This variant
improves the compute efficiency of OPD over the $G{=}8$ run (Figure 26). It takes longer in wall-clock time because it runs more
optimizer steps". **App. A:** "Uses FLOPs as the compute metric. Wall-clock and GPU-hours entangle the training objective with
implementation and hardware; FLOPs is the only implementation-invariant compute metric." **Limitation and future work:** "We encourage
future work claiming an OPD advantage over GRPO to report training FLOPs as an implementation-invariant measure, so that improvements
in objective, rollout scheduling, and infrastructure can be disentangled and directly comparable across papers." **Sec. 5.1:** "with
group size $G{=}1$ the same recovery costs only $0.2$ EFLOP (${\sim}$50${\times}$), matching the efficiency gains reported in the OPD
literature (Thinking Machines Lab, 2025)."

**Third, 2603.11137, App. G.** "Reopold improves the allocation and stability of teacher feedback but does not directly reduce the cost
of teacher inference; when teacher evaluation dominates wall-clock training time, the practical speedup may be limited." **Fourth, the
Thinking Machines post:** "Comparing compute costs across methods is nontrivial, as the ratio of training vs sampling vs log-prob
computation cost varies significantly depending on implementation. Below, we compute the cost in terms of FLOPs which penalizes methods
that can be effectively parallelized on GPUs."

## 4. Why has this not been done

The mechanism and the model were done (2608.29296, 2026-08-29, GRPO on fixed hardware). The pair's clock is unpublished because the
card paper chose FLOPs on principle (App. A) and wrote the wall-clock fact as a sentence, not a number (App. I); its authors hold the node
and the logs. The magnitude is unwritten because it is a coefficient of a published model. With the card paper's step counts,

$$\rho_{G=1} = \frac{k_{\mathrm{GRPO}}\, t_{\mathrm{GRPO}}(512)}{k_{\mathrm{OPD}}\, t_{\mathrm{OPD}}(64)} \approx \frac{200}{26} \cdot \frac{1}{1.1} \cdot r \approx 6.8\, r, \qquad r = \frac{t_{\mathrm{step}}(512)}{t_{\mathrm{step}}(64)} \le 8,$$

where $k$ is the optimizer-step count to 33.9% ($k_{\mathrm{OPD}} \approx 200 \times (0.2/10.1) \times 8 \times (10/12) \approx 26$ at
$G = 1$, $\approx 27$ at $G = 8$, because the recovery FLOPs are linear in $G$) and $1.1$ is the teacher forward's share of an OPD step
(REOPOLD: 8.2% at 8K tokens). The two-term Roofline, $t_{\mathrm{step}}(B_a) \approx (M_W + B_a\, S\, m_{\mathrm{KV}}) / W_{\mathrm{HBM}}$,
with $M_W = 8$ GB of bf16 weights, $B_a$ the active sequences per GPU (8 and 64 for node batches 64 and 512 under data parallelism),
$S$ the context in tokens, $m_{\mathrm{KV}} = 147{,}456$ bytes per token (2 vectors $\times$ 36 layers $\times$ 8 KV heads $\times$ 128
$\times$ 2 bytes, from the model's `config.json`) and $W_{\mathrm{HBM}} = 4.8$ TB/s, gives $r = 1.9, 3.2, 4.0, 4.6$ at
$S = 1{,}000, 3{,}000, 5{,}000, 7{,}168$. The self-GRPO OPD rollouts end at 3,741 tokens mean (Fig. 22), so the context averaged over a
response is about 2,000 and $r \approx 2.7$; GRPO's rollouts sit at 1,000–1,500 tokens for its first 55 steps, which lowers $\rho$ further.
Predicted $\rho_{G=1} \approx 15$–$25$; the published fixed-hardware measurements ($4\times$ batch: $2.93\times$ time for GRPO,
$1.74\times$ for PPO) sit in the same band, and the probe's step-0 stop rule ($r > 4$) sits inside it. Arithmetic is irrelevant at both
batches ($2 N_s B_a$ FLOPs per step is under 0.5 ms at $B_a = 64$ against 989 TFLOP/s): the step is memory-bound at both, and the bytes
grow with $B$ through the KV cache, which the headline's mechanism omits.
