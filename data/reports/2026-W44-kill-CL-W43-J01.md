Glossary (once). *Batch invariance*: a GPU kernel's property that one request's numbers do not depend on which other
requests share its batch; without it, the floating-point reduction order changes with batch size and the same prompt can
yield a different token. *Serving stack*: the inference engine (vLLM, SGLang, a provider's API) with its kernels, batching,
caching and routing. *First divergence*: the first token or turn at which two runs with identical inputs differ.
*Flip rate*: the share of repeated runs (or decode steps) whose output differs. *pass^k*: the probability that all $k$
runs of a task succeed. *Within-cell variance*: the spread between repeated runs of one (model, task) cell with every
setting fixed. *Replica floor*: the disagreement two exact re-executions produce when nothing changes.

## 3. Verdicts by clause

**Clause 1 — "is the serving stack's batch-dependent arithmetic, not the agent".** This is the benchmark owners' stated
protocol assumption, with the mechanism named: HAL (2602.16666 §4) pins temperature 0 precisely so that the residual is
"floating-point non-associativity, batch-size variation from concurrent server load, non-deterministic kernel scheduling";
2602.07150 §2.4.1: "modern LLM inference engines introduce various sources of non-determinism including floating-point
precision, parallelization, hardware-specific optimizations, and batching strategies." The claim's `current_belief` field
said the belief to test was "temperature-0 agent evaluations are reported as single runs or with a 'noise' of a point or
two, as if deterministic"; the fetched record says the opposite — the two owners who run replicates at temperature 0
already attribute the spread to the stack. Gate 2B: our expected reading equals the field's assumption. The genuine open
quantity is the *share*, and its surprise runs the other way: 2602.07150 names "tool execution or timing effects" as a
second source and does not separate them; 2603.09127 finds multi-round instability "not only a consequence of residual
platform-side stochasticity"; 2609.04198 finds batch-invariant self-hosting "helped only while the server was quiet";
Vosti (2609.38981) finds vLLM's batch-invariant mode and SGLang's deterministic mode "produce different logits under some
execution variations"; HAL's own tau-bench harness runs the gpt-4o user simulator with no temperature argument (C-W44-OWN-2's
finding, 2026-11-02). Each of these says the stack is *not* all of it.

**Clause 2 — "over multi-turn trajectories it is large enough to reorder leaderboards".** Occupied by 2602.07150 at
temperature 0 on SWE-bench Verified (ten runs × six configurations; first-divergence distributions by absolute and relative
token position; "deterministic sampling only delays divergence, but does not suppress it"; "longer trajectories exhibit
amplified variance"; SD 1.0–1.8 points at temperature 0 against the 2–3 point improvements the field reports). The
within-generation compounding rate is also measured (2609.31796: 2.1% at the first token, 25% by token 20 under a
bfloat16 batch-size change; 2605.30218: flips on 0.3–1.3% of decode steps). From the per-step flip rate the episode-level
expectation is computable at \$0: with a per-step flip probability $q$ over $n$ decision tokens with low margin, the
probability that a 40-action episode stays token-identical is $(1-q)^{n}$; at $q = 0.005$ (MarginGate's Llama-3.1-8B
figure) and $n = 2{,}000$ low-margin tokens per episode, $(1-q)^{n} = e^{-10} \approx 0$, so every pair diverges somewhere,
and the outcome-flip rate is then the fraction of divergences that change an action's success, which 2602.07150's
pass^k gaps (up to 24.9 points between pass@k and pass^k at temperature 0 for Devstral-2 on R2E-Gym) bound from above.
Nothing in this arithmetic is news.

**The premise number.** The claim's identification relied on temperature 0 being a low-variance arm whose residual is
the stack. 2602.07150 Table 1: "the variance never decreases and sometimes increases with temperature zero" (0.7% at
temperature 0.6 versus 1.2% at temperature 0 for Qwen3-32B on nano-agent; Devstral-2: 1.1% at 0.2 versus 1.6% at 0). On a
40-step coding benchmark the temperature-0 run spread is of the same size as the temperature-0.6 spread, so C-W44-07's
expected serving share $S$ of 0.3–0.7 sits below what the one published pair of arms shows ($S \approx 1$ on the pass@1
standard deviation, a related but not identical statistic). The surprise that would change a belief is $S$ well under 1
on a certified-static testbed, which is the inverted claim.

## 4. Why not done
