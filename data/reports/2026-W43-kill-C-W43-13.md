**Terms (one gloss each).** *Policy pair*: two policies evaluated on the same task in real and in sim. *Resolvable
pair*: a pair whose real success-rate gap exceeds its binomial standard error at the trial count used
(|p1−p2| ≥ 1.96·sqrt(p1(1−p1)/n + p2(1−p2)/n)); the real sign of such a pair is knowable. *Sign agreement*: the sim
orders the pair the way the real robot did. *MMRV* (mean maximum rank violation, SIMPLER 2024): for each policy, the
largest real-performance gap among the pairs the sim mis-orders, averaged over policies; a violation is weighted by the
real gap, never by the gap's uncertainty. *Chance-corrected agreement κ*: (A − 0.5)/(P̄ − 0.5), observed agreement A
against the agreement P̄ a perfect sim could reach when the real sign is itself a noisy draw. *Attenuation ceiling*: a
correlation between two noisy measurements cannot exceed sqrt(rel_real · rel_sim), the geometric mean of their
test-retest reliabilities (Spearman 1904, textbook). *Anytime-valid / sequential test*: a comparison that stays
correct whenever you stop adding trials.

## 9. Checklist lessons (2026-10-20)

- An incumbent metric can already answer the objection in weakened form: MMRV weights a violation by the real gap, not
  by the gap's standard error. Before calling a ruler new, write the incumbent's weighting and ours side by side and
  name the case where they diverge (0.08 at n = 5 vs 0.08 at n = 75).
- A mechanism can be in print as a reason to *omit* a metric (Kaedim App. B: "We do not report MMRV on this basis")
  without being in print as a ruler. Search the appendices of the smallest papers in the thread, not only the metric
  sections of the large ones, before writing "nobody has done this".
- Denominators inferred from decimal rates must pass two checks: the appendix protocol sentence and integer
  consistency across every cell. SIMPLER's eggplant task says 24 trials in Appendix B and its rates are consistent only
  with 20; the repo reconstructs trial vectors from rates the same way we do.
