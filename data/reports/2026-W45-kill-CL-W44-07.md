Glossary (each term once). **Serving stack**: the inference engine (vLLM, llama.cpp, a provider's API) with its kernels,
batching, caches and quantised weight formats. **Certified-deterministic arm**: a serving configuration shown by an exact
re-run (replica pass) to return the same tokens for the same request; a batch-invariant flag alone is not one (parent
report, lesson iii). **Channel**: one source of run-to-run difference (serving arithmetic; the environment's tools and
timing; a second sampled party such as a user simulator). **Control fork**: a continuation of the same model from a
rebuilt state, used to read what changes when nothing was meant to change. **Common random numbers**: giving every
replicate the same random draws for the environment so that replicates differ only through the channel under study.
**Infra error**: a trial that ends because the sandbox, not the agent, failed (an out-of-memory kill, a pod error).
**Serving share** $S$: the fraction of within-cell outcome variance at temperature 0 that a deterministic serving arm removes;
the claim says $S < 0.5$, the field (HAL 2602.16666 §4) assumes $S = 1$.

## 0. Verdict in one paragraph

**Killed: Gate 2B. The pre-registered surprise ($S < 0.5$) is contradicted by the one two-arm measurement in print on a
multi-turn tool-using benchmark, and by the magnitudes of the two channels measured separately; the surviving clause is a
protocol defect, not a claim.** The two-arm comparison the claim needs — the same agent loop, the same benchmark, the
environment rebuilt and verified, two serving configurations of one engine at temperature 0 — was run as an audit paragraph in
*The Replay Gap* (2608.08239, 2026-08-08, CMU) on SWE-bench Verified with mini-SWE-agent in the official Docker images: same-model
control forks on an AWQ-served 14B "remain near-identical to their base trajectories (edit distance 0.16–0.23; half never
diverge at any action), while the FP8-quantized 4B's controls diverge on 90–96% of forks (edit distance 0.49–0.67) under
identical decoding settings", with environment reconstruction verified at "99.99% return-code agreement over 11,702 replayed
actions" so that "environment reconstruction error is not a plausible source of the divergence we report". Changing only the
serving path moves the share of diverging runs from about one half to above nine tenths; the environment's command-level
channel on a Docker coding benchmark is bounded at the 0.01% level. The outcome level in that paper is vacuous (0–3% base rates,
"zero in 359 control forks"), so the magnitude comparison comes from two single-channel measurements: with the environment and
harness channel made deterministic by construction (single-shot, deterministic patch builder, official Docker grader), hosted
temperature-0 inference alone flips "6/70" per-instance outcomes between byte-identical runs (2607.09691, §5: "Temperature-0
API inference is not deterministic; ~9% of per-instance outcomes flip between identical runs"); with the model held fixed,
the environment's own outcome flips on Terminal-Bench 2.0 are the infra-error rate, "going from 5.8% at strict enforcement to
0.5% when uncapped", 2.1% at the 3× headroom the leaderboard's sandbox effectively grants (Anthropic engineering post,
2026-02-05). On the suites the claim names, the serving channel's flip rate (about 9 of 100 instances) is three to four times the
environment's (0.5 to 2 of 100 trials at leaderboard headroom; 6 of 100 only under strict enforcement), so the field's $S$ is
nearer 0.8 than 0.5 and HAL's attribution stands as the majority reading. The "second sampling party" clause is a different
object: a user simulator run at a non-zero or unpinned temperature is a sampled policy, not "the environment's tool and timing
nondeterminism", and its share is whatever the harness author left unpinned — a one-line protocol correction already held by
C-W44-OWN-2 (lab-parked), with the between-simulator spread in print at "up to 9 percentage points across different user LLMs"
(2601.17087). The exact certified decomposition has not been run because its expected answer is the field's stated belief and the
near-version is an audit paragraph in a routing paper: "why not done" is **no reason found**. Solo: killed. Lab (a benchmark
owner with replicate traces): killed as a paper, open as a protocol column (the same verdict the parent reached).

## 3. The two closest papers' own limitation text (verbatim, grep-verified)

**2608.08239, §6 Limitations:** "This is a deliberately controlled pilot: one scaffold, one benchmark family, one model
family (two quantized members), instances per run pair at temperature 0, and a 24 GB serving budget (28k context) that keeps
absolute resolution rates low (0–3%). [...] Quantization differences (FP8 vs AWQ) entangle model capability with serving
stack — our up/down comparisons are therefore between deployment configurations, not pure model scales. Prefix replay assumes
command-level environment determinism, which we verify rather than assume (99.99% return-code agreement over 11,702 replayed
actions; Appendix B)." §4.4: "Temperature-0 determinism is configuration-dependent: control forks of the AWQ-quantized 14B
remain near-identical to their base trajectories (edit distance 0.16–0.23; half never diverge at any action), while the
FP8-quantized 4B's controls diverge on 90–96% of forks (edit distance 0.49–0.67) under identical decoding settings — batched
FP8 serving is not a deterministic oracle, so even same-model replay carries irreducible noise on some stacks." §2: "Batching-
and kernel-induced nondeterminism at temperature 0 has been reported informally in the serving community; our same-model
control forks give it an experimental floor in the agentic setting, and show it differs sharply across quantization stacks."
What the limitation leaves open is "measuring mis-ranking in competent regimes, with unquantized pools and a third fork
position" — more routing, not the channel split; the residual divergence inside the AWQ arm (half the forks, edit distance
0.16–0.23) is never attributed between the AWQ stack's own batching and the environment, and that is the only unmeasured cell
this search found.

**2607.09691, §5 and §6:** "The follow-up re-ran the keep/drop arm under the byte-identical protocol (temperature 0, same
model alias, same prompts): it resolved [...] vs. the original [...], with a symmetric difference of 6 instances. Temperature-0
API inference is not deterministic; [~9%] of per-instance outcomes flip between identical runs. Any arm difference of this
order — including H2's 2-instance gap — is within run noise, a caution that applies to single-run SWE-bench comparisons
generally." Threats to validity: "Non-determinism — measured, not assumed. [~9%] flips of outcomes between identical runs
(§5); probe cells are single draws at [temperature 0]." "Single language (Python), single model family. Stated coverage gaps."
Operational note: "the first evaluation pass hit Docker Hub's anonymous image-pull rate limit; affected instances were detected
as environment errors (never counted as model failures) and re-run to completion under authenticated pulls." (The LaTeXML text
drops the numerals inside math; "6/70" and "~9%" are the paper's, the former verbatim in Appendix C.) Nothing here is left to
a future channel split: the paper treats the 9% as the API's and removes environment faults by re-running.

**Supporting, Anthropic 2026-02-05 (page):** "pass rates fluctuate with time of day, likely because API latency varies with
traffic patterns and incidents. We have not formally quantified this effect"; "the boundary between 'model capability' and
'infrastructure behavior' is blurrier than a single benchmark score suggests"; "leaderboard differences below 3 percentage
points deserve skepticism until the eval configuration is documented and matched." The timing channel is the one named and
unquantified source in the owners' own text, and it is a *serving*-side effect (API latency under load), not the environment's.

## 4. Why not done, and why now

The design has three parents, all old: the deterministic-arm, one-channel-at-a-time protocol (1809.05676, 2018), common random
numbers (simulation textbooks; 2512.24145 for LLM multi-agent simulators), and replica-stable serving (prefix reuse off at batch
size one, 2609.04748; batch-invariant kernels, 2025-09). The agent-benchmark replicate papers ran without it because the
expected answer is their stated assumption (HAL §4), and the one paper that ran the two-arm comparison did so as an audit of
its own control floor and got the assumed direction (2608.08239). Cost is small (the parent priced it at 15–25 GPU-hours).
**No reason found** — the `prompts/03_kill.md` flag — beyond the fact that the decomposition is an appendix whose
headline is already believed. The "why now" the claim offered (EnvOS's journaled digests, 2026-10-29; AgentCE-Bench's seedable
failure channel, 2026-04) enables a cleaner instrument, not a different answer.

## 5. Gate-by-gate verdict, with the position clause

- **Gate 0 (premise hygiene).** The claim's quantity $S$ is defined, but its denominator is protocol-dependent in two ways the
  record did not name: (i) the environment channel's size is a dial (tool-failure rate $r$, resource headroom, prefix reuse,
  user-simulator temperature) — at $r = 0$ and 3× headroom it is 0.5–2% of trials, at strict enforcement 5.8%, at $r = 0.20$
  whatever the designer injected — so "$S < 0.5$ on tool-using benchmarks" is not a property of the benchmarks (checklist
  2026-11-01: a variance-share headline must name the shipped levels); (ii) "the second sampling party" at a non-zero
  temperature is a sampled policy, and a party at temperature 0 has zero decision variance by construction (Gate 0's own
  sentence), so the clause is either a protocol bug (unpinned simulator) or folds back into the serving channel (pinned
  simulator). Fails as written.
- **Gate 1 (cheap to kill).** Passes: C-W44-07 at about \$300 and C-W45-04 at about 12 GPU-hours are under one GPU-week. But
  both pre-registered kills fire on the evidence already in print (section 6).
- **Gate 2 (pressure / ruler).** Route B fails at the pre-registered surprise: our expected result ($S < 0.5$) is contradicted
  by 2608.08239's serving-only swing (about 50% to 90–96% of forks) with the environment verified, and by the magnitudes
  (serving 9 of 100 instances, 2607.09691; environment 0.5–2 of 100 trials at leaderboard headroom, Anthropic). The field's

## 7. Lessons for `prompts/checklist.md`

- 2026-11-08 — An inverted residue inherits the parent's premise numbers and must be re-costed against the *other* channel's
  measured size before filing: CL-W44-07 was filed with the serving channel's numbers (parent report) and no number for the
  environment channel; the environment channel's size was in print as an infra-error rate (Anthropic 2026-02-05: 0.5–5.8% of
  trials by headroom) and the comparison (9% vs 0.5–2%) settled the share before any probe ran. Write both channels' fetched
  magnitudes side by side at `/claims` time for any "X is under half" headline. (CL-W44-07.)
- 2026-11-08 — A two-arm serving comparison can be an audit paragraph in a paper about something else: 2608.08239 is a routing
  paper whose §4.4 "Auditing the noise floor" runs the claim's design on SWE-bench. Search the neighbouring fork / replay /
  router literature's control-arm sections ("same-model control", "replay fidelity", "noise floor", "configuration-dependent")
  before calling a channel split unmeasured. (CL-W44-07.)
- 2026-11-08 — A channel whose magnitude the experimenter sets (tool-failure rate $r$, resource headroom, cache reuse,
  simulator temperature) cannot carry a share headline about "the benchmark": the share is a function of the dial. Name the
  shipped level in the headline or the claim fails Gate 0's protocol clause (third occurrence after 2026-11-01's W44 #4 and
  2610.07781's prompt-dependent sign). (C-W45-04.)
- 2026-11-08 — "Second sampling party" is not an environment channel: a sampled user simulator is a policy, and its share is
  whatever temperature the harness left unpinned (Gate 0: a party at temperature 0 has zero decision variance by construction).
  A claim that bundles it with "tool and timing nondeterminism" has two objects in one noun. (CL-W44-07.)
- 2026-11-08 — Common random numbers by stream position stop coupling once control flow diverges (2603.11084, 2026-03-11:
  event-keyed counter-based generators are the fix); any CRN arm on an agent loop must couple by event identifier, not by seed
  or stream offset, or its "coupled" cell is partly independent. (C-W45-04, which had written the caveat without the fix.)
