# Kill search — C-W43-15: convergence law of the capped contact solve along policy-visited states

Date: 2026-10-20. Candidate: `C-W43-15` in `data/candidates/2026-W43.jsonl` (L5, probe of claim `CL-W43-01`, from card
2610.17221). Checklist: `prompts/checklist.md` (positioning and density items run; new lessons in §9). Nothing below is
from memory: abstracts and density counts came from `export.arxiv.org/api/query` on 2026-10-20 (four 429 responses, each
retried after 30–60 s; no query was dropped); full texts were downloaded as PDFs from `arxiv.org/pdf/<id>` into
`scratchpad/ks15/dl/` (untrusted data), converted with `pdftotext -layout` and read in `scratchpad/ks15/txt/`; engine
sources were fetched from `raw.githubusercontent.com` and `mujoco.readthedocs.io`. Depth marks: [full] = whole text read
or grep-guided reading of method, results and limitations; [sections] = the sections named; [abstract-only] = API
abstract. Semantic Scholar skipped (`degraded: s2`, no key). No local conference-export index exists (`data/exports/`
absent). The complaint stream (GitHub issues of engine repos) is out of this session's repository scope and was not read.

**Terms (one gloss each).** *Constraint solver*: the iterative optimisation MuJoCo runs each step to find contact and
joint-limit forces (Newton, conjugate gradient or projected Gauss–Seidel). *Iteration cap* (`iterations` in the XML):
the maximum number of solver iterations per step; MuJoCo's default is 100, MuJoCo Playground ships 1 to 5 for its
MJX tasks. *Warm start*: initialising the solver from the previous step's forces, so one step's accuracy depends on
the previous step. *Contraction factor rho*: the ratio by which the solver's error shrinks per iteration when the
error falls geometrically, e(K) = a · rho^K. *Fidelity tag xi_hat*: 2610.17221's scalar that tells a surrogate how
wrong each training sample is, computable only when a convergence law e(K) is known. *Visited states*: the states a
trained policy actually passes through, as opposed to hand-chosen test scenes. *rho_L5*: the lane's exploit share, the
fraction of return earned on steps whose one-step re-simulation error exceeds a calibrated threshold kappa.
*Self-consistency* (2304.06372): how far a simulator's trajectory moves when only its own numerical settings change.

## 0. Verdict in one paragraph

**Survived-kill, in both positions, as the narrow measurement written out in §7.** No fetched paper re-steps the states
a trained policy visits under a sweep of the contact solver's iteration cap, and no fetched paper reports whether the
per-step error of a capped contact solve follows one contraction rate per task. The pieces around it are all in
print and each stops one step short: the shipped caps exist and are documented as a performance choice without an
accuracy check (MuJoCo Playground: Go1 `iterations="1"`, Leap hand `iterations="5"`, DM-Control XMLs "modified for
performance", §1.2); the only full-text benchmark of GPU simulators measures fidelity at a fixed time step at the
distribution level and names solver early termination as a noise source without sweeping it (GPUSimBench, §1.1); the
one paper that treats solver truncation as a training-time fidelity lever does so for linear solvers in PDE emulation
and lists "smoother relations between the achieved plateau ratio and the conducted iterations" as future work (PRDP,
§1.4); the one GPU-RL paper that lowers solver iterations bundles the cap with a coarser time step and a simplified
collision model into a single "simplified simulator" and never isolates it (MFPG-PPO, §1.5); the card paper refuses any
axis whose "convergence is non-monotonic in the outer iteration count" (§1.6); and a differentiable-contact paper
reports, for its own non-smooth Newton solve, that a fixed iteration budget returns a worse iterate than an earlier
one "by up to three orders of magnitude on a third of steps" on mesh terrain (Ostrich, §1.3), which is the first
published data point that per-step convergence of a contact solve is not geometric. The pre-window precedent is the
contact-models survey (2304.06372, §3), which measured iterations-to-convergence and self-consistency against the
stopping tolerance and the time step on three fixed robot scenes, not along a policy's states and not as a per-state
rate. The probe costs 10 GPU-hours and both outcomes are informative. The survival is of the "first to measure" kind:
the object is engine-side numerical analysis that an engine team or the Ostrich authors can run in a week, so the
race flag in §5 stands. One Gate 0 defect found and fixed in §6: the audit must carry the rollout's warm-start vector,
because MuJoCo's documented accuracy claim for low caps holds only under warm start.

## 1. Paper records (closest first)

### 1.1 arXiv 2607.13059 — GPUSimBench: Towards Scalable and Reliable GPU-Accelerated Simulators in Embodied AI (2026-07-06) [full]

- **Trick.** Run Isaac Lab, Genesis, ManiSkill, MJX and Playground on the same inclined-plane and ball-drop scenes at
  one time step, compare the simulated outcome distributions to real ones with an earth-mover distance, and measure how
  much identical worlds diverge across batch slots and across repeated runs.
- **Ships.** A benchmark protocol; a six-axis radar per simulator (throughput, parallelism, memory, physical alignment,
  parallel variability, run-to-run variability); four regimes of stochasticity.
- **Does not.** Vary any solver setting; measure per-step error against a converged reference; look at the states a
  policy visits. "We fix a unified simulation time step ∆t = 0.01 s across all simulators" (§IV, setup).
- **Verbatim, on the solver as a noise source (§V-B, Run-to-Run Variability):** "a primary source is the algorithmic
  path taken by iterative numerical solvers: small floating-point perturbations can change contact ordering, activation
  of constraints, projection decisions, and early-termination conditions, leading to different sequences of intermediate
  iterates even with identical nominal settings. In long-horizon contact-rich rollouts, these solver-path differences can
  compound into observable distribution shifts."
- **Verbatim limitations (§VI):** "This work has several limitations. First, GPUSimBench currently covers only a limited
  set of tasks and metrics, and the conclusions should be interpreted within this benchmark scope. Specifically, we
  evaluate two scalability scenes and one contact-rich real-world-matched experiment, all on a single hardware and
  software stack. As a result, the reported performance and variability may change across different GPUs, drivers, and
  simulator versions. In addition, the current benchmark does not yet cover deformable-body dynamics, fluid simulation,
  or the effects of perception and sensor rendering."
- **Difference.** The candidate sweeps the iteration cap per visited state and asks for a per-state rate; GPUSimBench
  holds the knobs fixed and reports distribution-level fidelity and non-determinism. Its run-to-run spread is the noise
  floor the candidate must report on repeated re-steps.
- **Code/data.** "We release this benchmark and evaluation protocol" (conclusion).

### 1.2 arXiv 2502.08844 — MuJoCo Playground (2025-02-12) [full; the record's named first check]

- **Trick.** A pip-installable stack of MJX physics, Madrona batch rendering and ready environments that trains
  locomotion and manipulation policies in minutes on one GPU and transfers them zero-shot.
- **Ships.** The environments and XMLs with per-task solver caps; the checkpoints the candidate rolls out.
- **Does not.** Sweep `iterations` against return or transfer anywhere in the paper. The only mention of solver
  iterations is Appendix A Table III, DM Control Suite: "Certain XMLs were modified for performance and are shown in the
  table" (A.1), and the caption "Where specified, XML modifications were made to the solver iterations, line search
  iterations, as well as contact custom parameters for MJX." The table lists `iterations=1, ls iterations=4` for
  ball-in-cup and cartpole, `iterations=4, ls iterations=8` for cheetah and hopper. The locomotion and manipulation
  appendices (B, C) state no solver setting.
- **Verbatim limitations (§VI):** "MuJoCo Playground inherits the limitations of MJX due to constraints imposed by JAX.
  First, just-in-time (JIT) compilation can be slow (1-3 minutes on Playground's tasks). Second, computation time
  related to contacts does not scale like the number of active contacts in the scene, but like the number of possible
  contacts in the scene. This is due to JAX's requirement of static shapes at compile time." No accuracy limitation is
  stated.
- **Shipped values (fetched 2026-10-20 from `google-deepmind/mujoco_playground`, branch main):**
  `_src/locomotion/go1/xmls/go1_mjx_feetonly.xml` line 4: `<option iterations="1" ls_iterations="5" timestep="0.004"
  integrator="Euler">`; `_src/manipulation/leap_hand/xmls/leap_rh_mjx.xml` line 4: `<option timestep="0.01"
  integrator="Euler" iterations="5" ls_iterations="8">`.
- **Difference.** Playground sets the caps; nobody in the paper measures what they cost. Not occupied.

### 1.3 arXiv 2609.08800 — Ostrich: Taking Large Strides Through Stiff Contact in Differentiable Dynamics (2026-09-08) [sections: abstract, §III solver, §IV-A, limitations]

- **Trick.** Solve hard contact with a non-smooth Newton iteration at time steps around 0.1 s and differentiate the
  converged residual through the implicit function theorem, so gradients cost constant memory per step.
- **Ships.** A GPU differentiable simulator; an accuracy-versus-time-step sweep of Ostrich, MuJoCo and Newton
  Semi-Implicit against real wheeled-robot trajectories (Fig. 3: "Brackets span each engine's usable plateau, where error
  stays within 2× of its sweep minimum (the model-error floor); past the edge, integration error takes over").
- **Does not.** Sweep an iteration cap; involve a policy; measure along RL-visited states.
- **Verbatim, on fixed-budget Newton (§III, Best-iterate backtracking):** "At large h the Newton iterates can overshoot,
  so instead of a line search we run a fixed budget and return the iterate with the smallest residual ∥r(s_k)∥₂ from
  index k_min onward; the floor k_min blocks early acceptance of under-converged states. On mesh terrain the final
  iterate overshoots an earlier, lower-residual one by up to three orders of magnitude on a third of steps."
- **Verbatim limitations (§V, "Limitations and future work"):** "The implicit Newton solve has a higher per-step cost
  than explicit integrators, so Ostrich's per-iteration advantage shrinks when small timesteps are not a bottleneck. ...
  Impacts are resolved inelastically (e=0); restitution is left to future work. All real-robot evidence comes from a
  single wheeled platform. Our adjoint fixes the active set within each step but lets it evolve between steps ...
  gradient flow through a contact's birth or death, which grasping needs but wheeled locomotion on persistent contact
  does not, remains open."
- **Difference.** Ostrich measures the time-step axis against reality for one scene and reports non-monotone residuals
  as an implementation note; the candidate measures the iteration axis per visited state and ties it to return. The
  "third of steps" sentence is the one published anchor for the candidate's expected bimodal result.
- **Code/data.** Not stated in the sections read.

### 1.4 arXiv 2502.19611 — PRDP: Progressively Refined Differentiable Physics (2025-02-26; pre-window) [sections: abstract, §6]

- **Trick.** Train a network through a deliberately under-converged iterative linear solver, raise the iteration count
  only when the training loss plateaus, and stop raising it once more iterations no longer improve the network.
- **Ships.** The schedule; results on inverse problems and Navier–Stokes emulation ("full accuracy of the network is
  achievable through physics significantly coarser than fully converged solvers").
- **Does not.** Touch contact, complementarity or a policy; the fidelity consumer is a regressor.
- **Verbatim limitations and outlook (§6):** "PRDP is limited to settings that involve iterative linear solvers. As such,
  it can not be used for purely explicit numerical solvers ... Our work opens up many interesting directions for future
  investigations, such as smoother relations between the achieved plateau ratio r and the conducted
  iterations/prescribed tolerance in the physics solver."
- **Difference.** PRDP asks how few iterations a training loop tolerates for a linear solver; the candidate asks what
  the error-per-iteration curve of a non-smooth contact solve looks like state by state. Not occupied.

### 1.5 arXiv 2610.02505 — Multi-Fidelity Policy Gradients Stabilize Data-Scarce Reinforcement Learning (2026-10-01) [sections: abstract, §5.1, §7]

- **Trick.** Use many cheap low-fidelity rollouts only as a control variate that cancels variance in the gradient from
  a few high-fidelity rollouts, so the estimator stays unbiased while the cheap data does the stabilising.
- **Ships.** MFPG-PPO in Isaac Lab (H1, ANYmal-D) and on a Franka arm; a budget-aware split of samples across fidelities.
- **Verbatim, the low-fidelity axis (§5.1):** "The LF simulation simplifies the robot collision model, coarsens the
  physics time step, reduces the number of physics solver iterations, and disables self-collisions."
- **Does not.** Isolate the solver cap; measure per-step error; measure exploitation under the converged engine.
- **Verbatim future work (§7):** "Three directions call for future research. First, re-synchronizing coupled LF rollouts
  to recorded HF states while uncoupled LF rollouts evolve freely can introduce bias ... Second, raising cross-fidelity
  correlation ... Third, real-to-sim tools that turn scene scans into interactive simulators ... could ease the
  construction of coupled LF simulators."
- **Difference.** The solver cap is one of four bundled knobs inside their "simplified simulator"; the candidate measures
  that knob alone. This paper matters more for the sibling C-W43-16 (fidelity mixing as a training recipe) than here.

### 1.6 arXiv 2610.17221 — Compute-optimal data scaling for neural surrogates via multi-fidelity training (the card) [full, carded 2026-10-19]

- **Trick.** Buy training data at a mix of solver accuracies, tag each sample with its accuracy, tune the mix.
- **Verbatim, the refused axis (App. G, "Solver error"):** "the SIMPLEC iteration in steady-state RANS does not admit a
  single, well-defined 'solver fidelity' parameter that exhibits the clean exponential convergence as in the other
  experiments ... The resulting convergence is non-monotonic in the outer iteration count and depends on multiple knobs
  that interact non-trivially. We therefore run all simulations to a fixed iteration count and study fidelity along the
  spatial discretization axis only."
- **Difference.** The candidate measures exactly the quantity the card declines to assume for a contact solver.

### 1.7 arXiv 2603.12185 — ComFree-Sim (2026-03-12) [sections: abstract, §V-A]

- **Trick.** Replace the per-step iterative contact solve with a closed-form impedance update in the friction cone, so
  contact cost scales linearly with contact count on a GPU.
- **Ships.** A Warp engine with a MuJoCo-compatible interface; a penetration-depth benchmark against MJWarp: "after 1000
  steps, we report the mean and standard deviation for MJWarp and multiple ComFree-Sim settings".
- **Does not.** Vary MJWarp's iteration cap; measure along policy states. Difference: a new solver benchmarked at
  default settings, not a dose-response of the incumbent's cap.

## 3. Pre-window precedents

- **2304.06372 — Contact Models in Robotics: a Comparative Analysis (2023; [sections: §VII-B self-consistency, §VII-C
  computational benchmarks]).** Trick: implement the main contact models and solvers (PGS, ADMM, Newton, staggered
  projections) in one C++ codebase and benchmark them on the same scenes for physical violations, self-consistency and
  cost. What it measured: "The accuracy of simulators can be affected by the numerical resolution induced by two
  'hyper-parameters': the value of the stopping criterion for the contact solver desired accuracy (ϵ_abs) and the
  time-step value (∆t). We measure their effect on the simulation quality when varying them independently"; and the
  "number of iterations required to converge (Fig. 16)" on Solo, Talos and an Allegro hand with a dropped ball, cold-
  and warm-started: "PGS approaches appear to be reasonably fast to reach mild accuracy (ϵ_abs = 10⁻⁵) while they
  eventually saturate before reaching high precision in complex scenarii". Difference: fixed perturbation scenes and a
  count-to-tolerance per solver, not the per-state error-versus-iteration curve along a trained policy's visits, and no
  return weighting. This is the closest precedent and the vocabulary the candidate should reuse.
- **2502.19611 — PRDP (Feb 2025).** See §1.4: solver truncation as a training-time fidelity lever for linear solvers.
- **2110.00541 — Validating Robotics Simulators on Real-World Impacts (Acosta, Yang, Posa, 2021) [abstract-only].**
  Trick: fit each simulator's contact parameters to real cube-toss and Cassie-landing data and compare; "simulators
  capture inelastic impacts well while failing to capture elastic impacts". The lane's real-impact reference for the
  third kill test (higher fidelity is not reality).
- **2305.17110 — IndustReal / SAPU (2023) [abstract-only].** Trick: "simulation-aware policy updates" that down-weight
  samples with interpenetration; the precedent that a numerical artifact changes what a policy learns.

## 4. Incumbents and shipped artifacts

- **MuJoCo XML reference, `option/iterations` (fetched 2026-10-20):** "Maximum number of iterations of the constraint
  solver. When the warmstart attribute of flag is enabled (which is the default), accurate results are obtained with
  fewer iterations; if the warmstarted solution already satisfies the tolerance, the CG and Newton solvers terminate
  with zero iterations. Larger and more complex systems with many interacting constraints require more iterations. Note
  that mjData.solver contains statistics about solver convergence, also shown in the profiler." Default 100;
  `tolerance` default 1e-8, "applied to the smaller of the cost improvement and the gradient norm" for CG and Newton.
- **MuJoCo XML reference, `flag/warmstart`:** "This feature should be disabled when evaluating the dynamics at a
  collection of states that do not form a trajectory - in which case warm starts make no sense". This is the Gate 0
  point in §6.
- **CPU MuJoCo `mjData.solver`:** per-iteration convergence statistics already exist on CPU; the candidate's law can be
  read from them on CPU for a converged reference and compared with MJX re-steps.
- **MuJoCo Warp `mujoco_warp/_src/types.py` (main, fetched 2026-10-20):** `Data.solver_niter: array("nworld", int)`
  ("number of solver iterations (nworld,)"), and a warning flag `ITERATIONS = 1 << 9` ("solver iteration limit
  reached"). So the production GPU engine records whether each world hit the cap but not the residual at the cap.
- **Playground XMLs:** Go1 `iterations="1" ls_iterations="5" timestep="0.004"`; Leap hand `iterations="5"
  ls_iterations="8" timestep="0.01"` (paths in §1.2).
- **GPUSimBench** (§1.1) is the shipped fidelity benchmark; it has no solver-setting axis.
- **RoboSimStudio** (2610.18835, Newton team) ships the knobs as editable; no measurement.

## 5. Why now, why not done

- Enabling pieces: MJX's functional step (2023) makes re-stepping 200k saved states at eight caps an embarrassingly
  parallel batch; Playground (Feb 2025) shipped per-task caps with public checkpoints, so the visited-state distribution
  of a cap-1 policy is free; GPUSimBench (Jul 2026) established that GPU-simulator fidelity is a measurable object and
  stopped at the distribution level; 2610.17221 (Oct 2026) supplied a consumer that needs the law and declined to assume
  it; MFPG-PPO (Oct 2026) put "reduces the number of physics solver iterations" into a training recipe without a
  per-step number.
- Why not done: the question sits between communities. Contact-solver authors measure convergence on fixed scenes
  (2304.06372) and do not train policies; RL users take the shipped cap as a performance setting ("modified for
  performance") and measure transfer, not numerics; GPU-simulator benchmarks measure engines against each other at
  fixed settings. Not "no reason found".
- Race flags: the Ostrich authors (already report non-monotone fixed-budget residuals and own an accuracy-sweep
  harness), the Newton/RoboSimStudio team (ship the knobs), and the DeepMind MuJoCo team (own `mjData.solver` and Warp's
  `solver_niter`). "They have the stack" is a guess; the re-open test is asking whether an iterations sweep along
  policy states is planned. Lane rate about one adjacent paper per two months against a five-day probe.

## 6. Killing experiment check

Restated: roll out the public Playground checkpoints (Go1 joystick at cap 1, Leap cube at cap 5, cheetah at cap 4, one
more) for 50 episodes each, about 200k visited (x_t, u_t); re-step every pair in MJX at caps {1, 2, 3, 5, 10, 20, 50,
100 with tolerance 1e-8 as reference} and at substeps h0/{1, 2, 4, 8}; record e_t(K) in the mass-weighted norm, the
active-contact count n_t and active-set changes; fit e_t(K) = a_t · rho_t^K per state; report the spread of rho_t, the
share of contact steps with R² < 0.9, the knee against n_t, and rho_L5 for the cap-1 Go1 policy against a cap-100
retrain. Cost: 10 GPU-hours, about $30, 5 days — under one GPU-week; both pre-registered kills stand as written.

Two corrections before it runs:
1. **Warm start is part of the state (Gate 0).** Under the shipped protocol each step is warm-started from the previous
   step's constraint forces, and MuJoCo's documented accuracy claim for low caps holds only then. Re-stepping a saved
   state cold changes e_t(K) at every K. The audit must save `qacc_warmstart` (the warm-start vector) with each visited
   state and re-step with it, and report the cold-start curve as a second arm. MuJoCo's own documentation says warm
   start "should be disabled when evaluating the dynamics at a collection of states that do not form a trajectory"; the
   candidate's states do form trajectories, and the record must say which protocol each number comes from.
2. **Noise floor.** GPUSimBench documents run-to-run and inter-environment divergence in GPU batches; the repeat-step
   spread at fixed K is the resolution floor for rho_t and must be reported per task before any R² is read.

## 7. Verdict

- **solo: survived-kill.** Public checkpoints, a free engine, 10 GPU-hours; nothing in the solo position is missing.
- **lab: survived-kill.** An engine team adds the Warp residual at the cap (one field beside `solver_niter`) and the
  production task set; the measurement is the same.
- **Narrowest surviving version.** A measurement, not a law: "On MuJoCo Playground's shipped tasks, re-stepped along the
  states their own policies visit with the rollout's warm start, the per-step error of the Newton contact solve against
  the converged step does / does not follow a single geometric rate per task; here is the distribution of per-state
  rates, its dependence on active contacts and contact-set changes, and the share of return earned on steps where the
  shipped cap's error exceeds the converged-trained policy's threshold." No claim about engines other than MJX, no
  claim about time steps beyond the substep arm, and no claim that the result transfers to PhysX or Newton until run
  there. The survival rests on nobody having measured this, with 2304.06372 having measured the fixed-scene version
  three years ago; that is a "first to measure" survival, and the report must say so.

## 8. Effect on the claim

`CL-W43-01` ("GPU physics engines trade physics for speed through solver iteration caps, and RL policies trained in
them exploit the difference") stays **open**. The first half is documented by shipped artifacts: caps of 1 and 5 against
a default of 100, set "for performance", with no accuracy check published by the engine or its users; the second half
(exploitation) is untested in every fetched source, and this probe is the first that can refute it on MJX (kill (b)

## 9. Checklist lessons (2026-10-20)

- Warm start is part of the state: a per-step re-simulation audit of an iterative contact solver must carry the
  rollout's warm-start vector (MuJoCo `qacc_warmstart`) or declare a cold start, because the engine's documented accuracy
  claim for low iteration caps ("accurate results are obtained with fewer iterations") holds only under warm start, and
  MuJoCo's own docs say warm start is meaningless on states that do not form a trajectory. The two protocols give
  different e_t(K); the record names which one each number uses.

  solver iterations, self-collisions) occupies the mechanism (fidelity mixing in training) without answering anything
  about any single knob; record it as a mechanism precedent and as a non-answer for the knob, and search for the
  bundled phrasing ("simplified simulator", "low-fidelity simulator") as well as the knob's name.
