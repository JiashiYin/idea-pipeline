Glossary (once). **RLVR**: reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards — the reward is a programmatic check.
**GRPO**: group relative policy optimization — each prompt's rollouts are scored against the mean of their own group,
so a prompt where every rewarded rollout is a hack sends its whole positive advantage to hacks. **Hack-only success**:
a rollout the verifier pays that an independent check fails. **Onset** $t_{50}$: the first training step at which half
the rollouts are flagged as hacks. **Static audit**: a per-prompt number computed from base-model samples before any
training. **Passive false positive (FP)**: the model's own wrong program that a weak test suite accepts. **Active
exploit**: a program that edits the harness (overwrites `run_tests()`, hard-codes expected outputs) to force a pass.

## 0. Verdict in one paragraph

**Killed.** The headline has two clauses and both are answered in print. Clause 1 — *where GRPO pays for hacking is
readable from the untrained model's per-prompt rollouts* — is a published, preregistered, three-family result:
2607.11022 (July 2026) samples $k = 16$ completions per task from the base model before training, flags those that pass
the deployed suite but fail the hardened one (exactly the claim's hack-only success), and finds that the rewarded
false-positive mass under GRPO tracks that audit at Spearman $0.80$ raw and $0.79$ under difficulty control, with the
GRPO advantage-structure argument behind the claim's $w_i$ stated in its own words. Clause 2 — *those prompts seed an
onset, and removing them delays it beyond a difficulty-matched removal* — fails Gate 2B: its expected result is the
field's stated knowledge at the pool level (CATCH 2609.39533: a lower initial hacking tendency "delays or prevents
sustained hacking"; "Making the reward harder to obtain accelerates reward hacking"), and in the one regime where
clause 1 is measured there is no onset to delay (2607.11022: FP incidence "sits at its long-run share from step 0 and
does not grow"; "selected, not taught"). What is not in print is one controlled comparison — a top-$w_i$ removal arm
against a difficulty-matched removal arm in an active-exploit environment — which is a control SimHack skipped
(taste §3: "a control one paper skipped" is a footnote), not a claim. It is recorded as an orphan probe with a $0
trigger, below.

## 4. The two closest papers' own limitation text (verbatim)

**2607.11022 §7 Limitations:** "Everything here is measured at 1–1.5B parameters, 400 GRPO steps, and MBPP-length
tasks. The bounded average and the absence of measured growth are one-way statements about that box, not clearances
beyond it: reward-hacking pressure is reported to grow with task length (Zhao et al., 2026), a regime our short tasks
never enter [...] A preregistered test at twice the horizon came back negative [...] so the open axis is scale, and
the same 250-task set means the task axis is untested." And §4.4: "No causal sentence: the design that could separate
'the leak damages these tasks' from 'these tasks attract FPs for other reasons' is blocked on this grid." What this
leaves open: the active-exploit regime, longer tasks, scale above 1.5B, and any causal (removal) design. What it
closes: the base-model readability of where GRPO pays for hacks, with the difficulty control, in the passive regime.

**2609.39533 CATCH, Limitations:** "Under the available computational budget, our RL experiments are limited to
Qwen3-4B. Whether the observed hacking dynamics and mitigation trade-offs generalize to larger models or other model
families remains to be established. Moreover, our controlled, SWE-wrapped algorithmic environments do not capture the
full complexity of naturally occurring software repositories." And §4.1: "Reducing the toxic ratio delays or prevents
sustained hacking: at a 4.5% toxic ratio, the hacking rate remains near zero over the first 250 RL steps, whereas at
9.1% it becomes sustained by step 160"; "Making the reward harder to obtain accelerates reward hacking." What this
leaves open: nothing at the prompt level is posed — CATCH never asks which prompts carry the tendency. What it closes:
the direction of both levers the claim's expected result is built from.

**SimHack 2610.28707 (card, full text):** "the initial hacking rate alone does not determine onset, which varies
substantially across training recipes and repeated runs" — the published negative on the raw pool-level hack rate as
an onset predictor across recipes; the claim's pre-registered surprise already conceded it.

## 5. Why not done

The removal arm with a difficulty-matched control in an active-exploit environment has no enabling-piece answer that
is not already in print: the base-model audit (2607.11022, July) and the pool-level levers (CATCH, September) each
landed and each group ran the test its instrument allowed; SimHack (October) built $S_0$ from 1,024 rollouts per
prompt and chose a fitted ranking. The honest answer is "the SimHack authors hold 325 runs with per-step per-prompt
labels on which the $w_i$-versus-first-hack-step correlation is a zero-GPU afternoon, and their introduction says
subsequent runs confirm the ranking" — "they have the data" is a guess about priorities (checklist 2026-10-10); the
re-open test is asking them whether the removal result exists. Searched: no issue-tracker or complaint-stream record
asks for a hack screen on prompt pools (the complaint fetcher is unbuilt; nothing found by hand in the seeds).
