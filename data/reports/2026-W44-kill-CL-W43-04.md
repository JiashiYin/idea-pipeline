Glossary used throughout. *Hazard*: the chance of failing in the next instant given survival so far. *Frailty*: a fixed
per-unit multiplier on the hazard that differs between units (here, between tasks), so that a pooled population shows a
falling hazard even when every unit's own hazard is flat. *Overdispersion*: more spread in a count than a binomial with one
success probability allows. *IRT* (item response theory): a logistic model with one latent ability per respondent (here,
per AI) and one latent difficulty per item (here, per task). *Random effect*: a per-task term drawn from a distribution,
which is what a frailty term is in IRT vocabulary. *Step clock*: time measured in agent turns or tool calls inside a run,
as opposed to the task's human-minutes label.

## 0. Verdict in one paragraph

**Killed — occupied (Gate 6), and the remaining half fails Gate 2B.** The claim has two halves. The first half — the
falling pooled hazard is between-task heterogeneity, identified from METR's repeated runs per task — is in print twice on
the exact dataset, in IRT vocabulary rather than survival vocabulary: Moss (LessWrong, 2026-02-13) fit a two-parameter
logistic IRT model in Stan with a per-task random difficulty ("Each task's difficulty has a mean that depends on log human
time, plus a random component to account for the fact that same-length tasks are not born equal"), found one standard
deviation of unexplained difficulty equal to a $4.7\times$ multiplier in human time, and derived the claim's own
`if_true` consequence — the 80% horizon read for a typical task versus averaged over tasks differs by an order of magnitude
(21.1 min vs 0.8 min for Claude 3.7 Sonnet); Nguyen and Fithian, 2610.12466 (UC Berkeley Statistics, 2026-10-08, NeurIPS
2026 workshop poster), fit an explanatory IRT model on 228 tasks and 26 AIs with a task random effect and a family random
effect whose variances are splines in log human time, plus a beta-binomial run-level correlation $\rho$ identified from
the repeated runs — the model is the probe's M2 with a normal frailty and a logistic link in place of a gamma frailty and a
Weibull link — and they report the probe's premise as a sentence: under run independence "we would predict that
$\sim 60\%$ of vectors $Y_{ij}$ were unanimous. However, it is actually 83% of them." The second half — "hold the task
fixed and the per-minute hazard is flat" — is not an observable on any horizon dataset, by the seed record's own
identifiability note and by 2610.12466's "Note that we ignore the time AIs take to complete tasks": human minutes is a
task label, no within-run failure moment is recorded, so a within-task hazard over human minutes is a modelling
convention, not a measurement. On the step clock, where a within-task hazard is observable, a flat per-step hazard is
the field's stated knowledge (2609.01660: "Task success follows a geometric law governed by a single per-step reliability
parameter"; 2609.19113: "Per-round failure rates are flat across the horizon") and the published counter-mechanism is a
rising one (2509.09677 self-conditioning: "the per-step accuracy of models degrades as the number of steps increases"), so
a flat within-task hazard is a service, not a surprise (Gate 2B). What is not in print is the one-paragraph translation —
Hamilton's Weibull shape $k \approx 0.6$ is what 2610.12466's fitted spread $s(x)$ produces from flat units — and that is a
comment under Ord's post, not a paper. Probe `X-2026-W42-S7F9` dies with the claim; probe `import:2026-W43-S7xF9` tests a
different claim (within-task step-clock hazard shape on tau-bench) and is detached as an orphan with three new neighbours
to answer before any run.

## 3. Verdict by component

- **"The falling hazard is between-task frailty" — killed, occupied.** 2610.12466 is the probe's M2 in IRT clothing. Map
  the symbols: the probe writes run $r$ of task $i$ succeeding with probability $\exp(-z_i (t_i/\theta)^{k_{\mathrm{cond}}})$ with
  $z_i \sim \mathrm{Gamma}$, mean 1, variance $\sigma^2$; 2610.12466 writes $\operatorname{logit}\tilde p_{ij} = \alpha_j - \beta\theta_i$ with
  $\theta_i = f(\log_2 T_i) + \xi_{F(i)} + \varepsilon_i$, $\varepsilon_i \sim \mathcal N(0, \sigma(\log_2 T_i)^2)$, $\xi_F \sim \mathcal N(0, \tau(\cdot)^2)$,
  and $p_j(t) = \mathbb E[(1 + \exp(-(\alpha_j - \beta\theta)))^{-1}]$ over $\theta \sim \mathcal N(f(\log_2 t), \sigma^2 + \tau^2)$. Both
  are a per-task multiplicative frailty on a within-task link, both are identified from the 4–8 repeated runs per task, and
  2610.12466 adds what the probe lacked: a frailty variance that varies with task length and a separate run-level
  correlation $\rho$. Their diagnostic ("the spread between curves reflects predictiveness", "SNR = 1" line on the
  time-to-difficulty plot) is the claim's ruler. Moss's typical-versus-marginal 80% horizon is the claim's `if_true`.
- **"Hold the task fixed and the per-minute hazard is flat" — killed, Gate 0 and Gate 2B.** On horizon data the within-task
  clock does not exist (seed record: "no within-run failure moment is observed ... 'hazard per minute' is a between-task
  construct in Ord's, Hamilton's and METR's fits alike"; 2610.12466: "Note that we ignore the time AIs take to complete
  tasks"), so $k_{\mathrm{cond}}$ is the shape of a link function, not a measured hazard; the pre-registered surprise
  "$k_{\mathrm{cond}} \in [0.8, 1.2]$" is a statement about which link fits, and Barry already reports that Weibull, logistic and
  LOESS "all did similarly well". On a step clock the flat-hazard sentence is the field's stated result twice over
  (2609.01660, 2609.19113) with a published rising-hazard mechanism against it (2509.09677), so the expected result equals
  the field's assumption: a service, not a paper (Gate 2B).
- **The hazard-vocabulary translation — not a claim.** Ord's reply and Hamilton's post never mention heterogeneity;
  2610.12466 never mentions Weibull, hazard or Hamilton. The sentence "Hamilton's $k \approx 0.6$ is the pooled shape that
  2610.12466's fitted spread $s(x)$ produces from units with $k = 1$, and the 99% horizon Ord calls undecidable between
  Weibull and log-logistic is decided by $\rho$ and $s(x)$, not by the link" is true, useful, and one paragraph long. It is
  a comment under Ord's post or a note to Nguyen and Fithian, and the pipeline records it as a footnote.

## 4. Why not done, race window, position

- **Why the hazard-vocabulary version was not written:** no reason beyond vocabulary — the statisticians did it in IRT words
  and the forecasters read it in survival words, and the two literatures do not cite each other (2610.12466 cites Moss,
  Barry, Kwa, shash42, Witkin, Lewis; not Ord or Hamilton). "No reason found" on a thorough search is itself a kill flag
  under `prompts/03_kill.md`.
- **Race window:** the horizon-statistics lane runs at roughly 15 arXiv papers and 5 notes per 12 months ($\approx 0.4$ per
  week), with a Berkeley statistics group holding the public-data refit and a NeurIPS slot; time to our first result was
  3 days. The window is irrelevant because the result exists; a translation note has a window of weeks before someone
  posts it in a comment thread.
- **Position clause.** *Solo:* killed — the free-data refit is published by two independent parties, one with a conference
  slot, and the dataset is frozen (pressure point #2: METR's tracker "no longer actively updated", last entry 2026-04-07).
  *Lab:* killed as a claim; what a lab with its own horizon suite can do is report $s(x)$ and $\rho$ beside its horizons —
  an application of 2610.12466's Model 2, not a result. A lab that records the within-run failure moment on a wall clock
  (not steps) would be the first to observe a within-task hazard over time; that is a new instrument and a new claim, not
  this one.

## 6. Checklist lessons (2026-11-01)

- Survival vocabulary (hazard, frailty, Weibull, half-life) returned zero occupants for a claim that two IRT papers occupy;
  the role words were "item response", "Rasch", "latent difficulty", "random effect", "overdispersion", "typical versus
  marginal". For any claim about heterogeneity across tasks or items, run the psychometric vocabulary beside the
  survival vocabulary. Second occurrence of the field's-textbook-answer lesson of 2026-10-18 (here the field's *next* step
  was taken by statisticians in a third vocabulary).
- Two of three occupants were research notes and a forum post cited by the arXiv paper's related-work section; the
  arXiv window undercounted this lane by a third. Read the closest paper's bibliography for non-arXiv incumbents before
  writing a density number.
- A within-unit hazard needs a within-unit clock. When the dataset's time axis is a unit label (human minutes per task),
  "conditional hazard shape" is the choice of link function, and the pre-registered surprise collapses to a model-selection
  statement the owner has already run (Barry 2026-03-20). Write the observable before the model.
