Glossary (each term once). **GRPO** — group relative policy optimization: $G$ rollouts of one prompt are scored and each is pushed
toward or away from the group mean. **Sibling** — one of the $G$ rollouts of a group. **Schedule** $\omega$ — the environment's
random draws for one episode (which tool call fails, what the simulated user says), keyed by event so two episodes that reach the
same event get the same draw. **Policy noise** $\xi$ — the agent's own sampling randomness. **Luck share** $\lambda$ — the fraction
of a prompt's reward variance explained by the schedule (defined below); an intraclass correlation. **Paired rollouts** — all
siblings of a group share one schedule. **Twin** — a counterfactual continuation that inherits the factual episode's downstream
sampling noise. **Anchor state** — a state several siblings reach, so their next actions can be compared. **Dial** — a quantity the
experimenter sets (a fault rate, a temperature) rather than a property of the environment. **User simulator** — a second LLM
playing the customer in $\tau$-bench-style environments.

## 0. Verdict in one paragraph

**Killed at Gate 0 (premise hygiene); Gates 2B and 4 fail behind it; Gate 6 is occupied for the instrument.** The claim's
quantity exists in print under the claim's own name and symbol: *Luck Is Not Skill* (2609.24144, 2026-09-21) writes, for a fixed
prompt, $\mathrm{Var}(R)=\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{env}}+\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{pol}}$ and calls
$\lambda=\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{env}}/(\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{env}}+\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{pol}})$ "the luck share, an intraclass
correlation", estimates it from "$K=8$ schedules times $M=8$ policy samples per schedule" on a GRPO run at $G=8$, and reports
"0.33 to 0.38" at step 0 and "0.44 to 0.53" at step 100 — in an environment whose "fault mixture was calibrated to produce
appreciable reward luck share" after "the first calibration measured a luck share of 0.03". So the one published reading of the
claim's ruler, taken in the one environment built to make luck visible, sits below one half for most of training, and the
unengineered reading was 0.03. The claim then fails Gate 0 three ways. (i) $\lambda$ as defined has no $G$ in it — the expected
within-group sample variance is $\mathrm{Var}(R)$ for every $G$ — so "at $G=8$" attaches to nothing; the paper names the
quantity that does depend on $G$, the group mean's effective sample size $G/(1+(G-1)\lambda)$, and it is not the claim's. (ii) In
user-simulator environments the "environment randomness" is a second sampled policy whose share is the harness's temperature
choice: $\tau$-bench calls the simulator with no temperature argument (W45 report, source read), TRACE pins "GPT-4.1 Mini
(temperature 0)" where $\lambda$ is zero by construction, and the one GRPO training paper on $\tau^{2}$-retail says "The simulated
user cannot be seeded" (2610.24294) — three protocols, three different $\lambda$ for the same environment. (iii) In flaky-tool
environments the fault rate is a dial ($p=0.25$ in the seed paper, "calibrated"), so the share is the designer's input (checklist
2026-11-08, third occurrence). The probe's own estimator, $\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{Var}(R\mid\text{actions})]$ by re-running the
environment "holding the agent's actions fixed", is not computable in an interactive environment past the first turn whose
reply differs — the paper conditions on the schedule $\omega$ for exactly this reason. Gate 2B fails because the probe's own
expected band for flaky tools (0.2–0.5) is the published number, and the headline's "more than half" is contradicted by it; the
nearest neighbours on the environments the field actually trains in say the opposite of luck-dominated: TWICE observes that "the
retriever, the corpus, and the grader are deterministic", ECPO gates on within-action noise on ALFWorld and WebShop (both
deterministic), and the checklist's 2026-10-08 entry already states that in deterministic sandboxes "differences between siblings
are decisions, never luck". Gate 4 fails because the `if_true` consequence — choose coupling and $G$ from a $\lambda$ table — is
the seed paper's own H1a diagnostic, and the paper states that "Reward luck share diagnoses reward contrasts; it is not a
validated predictor of learning gains." Solo and lab: killed. The residue is a reporting-gap pressure point (training papers do
not state the user simulator's temperature), not a claim.

## 3. The two closest papers' own text (verbatim, grep-verified)

**2609.24144, §4.2:** "For a fixed prompt, split the reward variance into the part explained by the schedule and the rest,
$\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{env}}=\mathrm{Var}_{\omega}(\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[R\mid\omega])$,
$\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{pol}}=\mathbb{E}_{\omega}(\mathrm{Var}_{\xi}[R\mid\omega])$,
$\mathrm{Var}(R)=\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{env}}+\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{pol}}$, and, when the total variance is positive, call
$\lambda=\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{env}}/(\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{env}}+\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{pol}})\in[0,1]$ the luck share, an intraclass
correlation. $\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{pol}}$ is everything the schedule does not explain, which includes the interaction between policy
and environment (the same fault can be survived or not depending on what the policy does), so it is not pure skill." Here $R$ is
the episode reward, $\omega$ the event-keyed schedule, $\xi$ the policy's sampling noise. On $G$: "it is not an effective sample size
for estimating the unconditional reward mean (there the exchangeable-correlation formula $G/(1+(G-1)\lambda)$ applies, and pairing
makes things worse)". **§5.2, Luck-share diagnostic:** "For a fixed policy and task, $K=8$ schedules times $M=8$ policy samples per
schedule give an $8\times 8$ table of rewards from which $\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{env}}$ and $\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{pol}}$ are estimated by
one-way random effects [...] Under the null the estimator has a clipping bias of about $0.03$ for $8\times 8$ tables, which is reported
rather than corrected." **§6.1:** "At step 0, reward luck share under tool faults at $p=0.25$ is 0.33 to 0.38 across the six C2 runs;
each task-bootstrap 95% interval has a lower bound above 0.20. At step 100 it is 0.44 to 0.53." **§7, limitations:** "The study covers
one 2B model, one synthetic environment, 100 training steps and three seeds per comparison. The fault mixture was calibrated to
produce appreciable reward luck share. Additional noise levels, outage-free training, blocking baselines, normalization ablations
and an external benchmark were registered as later tiers but were not run." **Deviations:** "the first calibration measured a luck
share of $0.03$ (prediction P12 failed) and exposed defects (faults keyed by call index, a tool rejecting numeric postal codes,
saturated diagnostic tasks)". **Practical implications:** "Pairing is available where a simulator, replay layer or grader exposes
controllable randomness. [...] Reward luck share diagnoses reward contrasts; it is not a validated predictor of learning gains."
Release (README, Repository section): `src/pairedrl/analysis/` holds "estimators, luck share, probe statistics, decision rules";
`registers/` holds "every number in the paper"; "Raw rollouts, trainer checkpoints and the probe's gradient evidence are not part
of the repository."

**2610.31544v1, abstract and §Limitations:** "We observe that in agentic search the retriever, the corpus, and the grader are
deterministic, so an episode is a deterministic function of the policy's decisions and its sampling noise." "Twice needs replayable
tools. Retrieval over a fixed corpus, sandboxed code, and simulators qualify; live web search and stateful APIs require caching, and
without it coupling weakens (§6.6), although credit stays unbiased. The gain depends on the twin correlation, which is lower at high
temperature and for long continuations, where divergence compounds". §6.6: "When retrieval is made nondeterministic by replacing each
passage with probability 0.3, $\bar{\rho}$ drops to 0.36". The "luck" in TWICE's title is the policy's own downstream sampling; the
paper's premise is that its environments carry none.

**Supporting, 2610.24294 (full):** "The simulated user cannot be seeded, so repeated evaluations of a policy differ; we compare
policies by task-paired differences". "Because the user simulator cannot be seeded, evaluations carry sampling noise, which we
address with task-paired standard errors and repeated evaluations". Retail was chosen "so that success reflects the agent's actions
rather than the simulated user's". **2606.05885 (full), §VarGate:** "Other anchors are dominated by within-action randomness: even
trajectories taking the same action may later diverge and obtain different returns." Weight
$\rho_{\mathrm{VG}}(s)=g_{s}\cdot B_{s}/(B_{s}+W_{s}+\epsilon)$, with $B_s$ the between-action and $W_s$ the within-action return
variance at anchor $s$ and $g_s$ a sample-size factor.

## 4. Gates, graded

- **Gate 0 (premise hygiene) — fails, three ways.** (i) *The quantity does not depend on $G$.* With independent schedules the
  within-group sample variance $\frac{1}{G-1}\sum_i (R_i-\bar R)^2$ has expectation $\mathrm{Var}(R)$ for every $G\ge 2$, and
  $\lambda$ is a ratio of population components; "at $G=8$" is attached to a quantity with no $G$ in it. The $G$-dependent objects
  (the group mean's effective sample size $G/(1+(G-1)\lambda)$; MaPP's composition noise; the probability a group is constant,
  "52 to 54% of paired groups" versus "about 13% of independent groups" in the seed paper's Table 10) are different claims and
  none is the headline's. (ii) *Whose temperature.* A user simulator is a sampled policy (checklist 2026-11-08, from CL-W44-07).
  $\tau$-bench calls it with no temperature argument so the provider default applies (W45 report, `envs/user.py` read); TRACE pins
  "GPT-4.1 Mini (temperature 0)" (W45 report), where the simulator's decision variance is zero by construction and $\lambda=0$;
  2610.24294 trains against gpt-oss-120b that "cannot be seeded". The same environment carries three different $\lambda$ under three
  shipped protocols; a quantity that moves with the harness author's unpinned setting is not "a property of the environment"
  (standards.md Gate 0, third bullet). (iii) *Flaky tools are a dial.* The one measured flaky-tool $\lambda$ is at $p=0.25$,
  a rate set so that "about three quarters of the episodes of the current policies meet at least one fault" and "calibrated to
  produce appreciable reward luck share"; the uncalibrated build gave 0.03. The headline's "flaky-tool environments" has no shipped
  level. A fourth defect is the estimator: the probe writes $\lambda=\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{Var}(R\mid\text{actions})]/\mathrm{Var}(R)$
  by re-running "the environment's randomness $K$ times holding the agent's actions fixed", but in an interactive environment the
  agent's second action is a function of the simulator's first reply, so "actions fixed" is undefined after the first divergent
  turn; the computable object conditions on the schedule $\omega$, which is the seed paper's $\lambda$, and the two orders of the law
  of total variance differ by the interaction term the paper names. The record's estimator does not estimate the record's quantity
  (taste.md §3, C-W43-15 lesson). Plain trick, for the record: *share one set of environment draws across a group and the siblings'
  differences are the agent's; the share removed is the luck share.* It is sayable, and it is the seed paper's sentence.
- **Gate 1 (cheap to kill) — passes on cost, fails on the $0 test and on informativeness.** P-W44-J01-a at 20–40 GPU-hours is under
  a GPU-week. A $0 version does not exist: a luck share needs the environment's draws re-sampled at a fixed schedule-or-action
  conditioning, and no released GRPO rollout set has that (released groups are $G$ independent siblings; tau2-bench's four-trial
  files and HAL's $K=5$ replicates re-sample both parties). The seed paper's own $\lambda$ tables are in its `registers/`, already
  published. A scorecarded version would cost about what the record says only after dropping four of its six environments: ALFWorld,
  WebArena and SWE-Gym-style sandboxes are deterministic or carry flakiness as an injected rate, and the tool-fault environment is
  the seed paper's own; what remains is one open-weights user simulator at a *stated* temperature with seeded batch-invariant
  inference (CL-W43-J01's piece) and MuJoCo at its shipped domain-randomization ranges — two cells, each with its temperature or
  range in the headline, roughly 10 GPU-hours and \$30–50. The pre-registered kill ("dead if $\lambda<0.25$ in every stochastic
  environment") fires or not as a function of the temperature chosen, so the negative teaches the setting, not the environment.
- **Gate 2 (pressure / ruler) — Route B fails at the pre-registered surprise.** The probe's own expected band for flaky-tool
  sandboxes, 0.2–0.5, is the published reading (0.33–0.53) in the environment calibrated to show it; the headline's "more than
  half" is contradicted by the same table for most of training and by the 0.03 of the uncalibrated build. For user simulators the
  record expects 0.4–0.7; the field's stated knowledge is not a number but a construction: TWICE's environments are deterministic,
  ECPO's are deterministic, GiGPO's are deterministic, and the one $\tau^{2}$ training paper picks the domain where the user
  "cannot complete the task for the agent" so that "success reflects the agent's actions". A ruler whose expected reading is either
  the one published number or a function of an unstated temperature is a service (standards.md Gate 2B).
- **Gate 3 (consumers).** Training teams would choose coupling from the table; benchmark owners would report $\lambda$. The seed
  paper's authors already hold the table's instrument and registered its "external benchmark" tier; one community at most.
- **Gate 4 (mechanism and consequence) — fails.** The `if_true` action, "choose coupling (TWICE / paired rollouts) and group size
  per environment from a table", is the seed paper's H1a diagnostic turned into a recipe, and the seed paper's own sentence
  disconnects the two: "Reward luck share diagnoses reward contrasts; it is not a validated predictor of learning gains" — with
  the evidence that pairing at $\lambda\approx 0.35$–$0.5$ "misses the registered learning-curve criterion". The consequence does
  not follow from the number by the owners' own finding (checklist 2026-11-15, third occurrence of "if_true equals the seed's fix").
- **Gate 5 (durability).** The ICC ruler is durable as an instrument; its readings are consumed by each harness's temperature and
  fault-rate choices, so a table is stale when a provider changes its default.
- **Gate 6 (ownership, density, race) — occupied for the instrument; the difference is breadth.** Fourteen direct neighbours in the
  window; the coupling line at six papers June–October 2026; the exact quantity, symbol, estimator and $G$ in print since 2026-09-21
  with an unrun registered tier naming an external benchmark. Position solo: a table that adds environments to another group's
  instrument, each cell a protocol choice — killed. Lab (a user-simulator benchmark owner): the owner can pin the simulator's
  temperature and report $\lambda$ as a protocol column in a day; that is a column, not a claim — killed as a paper.

## 5. Killing-experiment check and design requirements

P-W44-J01-a restated: six environments, $G\in\{4,8,16\}$, $K$ environment re-draws per trajectory prefix, one 7–8B agent, 20–40
GPU-hours, kill if $\lambda<0.25$ everywhere stochastic or if a table exists. Cost is under a GPU-week. The experiment as written
cannot run: (a) $G$ is not an argument of $\lambda$; (b) "holding the agent's actions fixed" while re-drawing the user's replies is
undefined past the first differing reply — the design must condition on an event-keyed schedule, which requires a seedable
simulator (open weights, fixed seed, batch-invariant serving) and a named temperature for both parties; (c) ALFWorld reads zero by
construction and is the control the record already names, WebArena's sites are deterministic containers, and a "flaky" sandbox
reads whatever rate is injected; (d) the half of the table that exists (tool faults at a stated $p$, $G=8$, $8\times 8$) is
published with its clipping bias stated. The kill criterion fires on the published number for the flaky-tool class and is
temperature-dependent for the user-simulator class. Both outcomes are predictable from print or from the setting; neither
teaches a belief. **Probe verdict, P-W44-J01-a: killed.**

Design requirements for any descendant (none is proposed): the headline names the agent's temperature, the simulator's model and

## 6. Why not done, and why now

The cross-environment table has not been published because its entries are not properties of environments: in the environments
the field trains in most (search over a fixed corpus, ALFWorld, WebShop, deterministic sandboxes) the share is zero by
construction, and where it is not zero it is a fault rate the designer set or a simulator temperature the harness left unpinned.
The one group that built the instrument registered the external-benchmark tier and did not run it, and wrote that the number
does not predict learning gains. That is "it is hard because the quantity is not a property of the thing", the Gate 0 flag, not
"no reason found". The record's "why now" — open-weights simulators and batch-invariant inference make the nested sampling exact
— is true and enables a cleaner reading of a quantity whose value is the protocol's; it does not change what the table would mean.
