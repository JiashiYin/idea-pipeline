**Terms, one gloss each.** *Solver budget / iteration cap* $K$: the maximum number of passes MuJoCo's constraint solver makes per
physics step to find contact forces; Playground ships $K = 1$ for Go1 and $K = 5$ for the Leap hand; the engine default is 100.
*Converged engine*: the same model stepped with the cap lifted so contact forces are solved to tolerance. *Transferable return*:
the return of a policy evaluated in the converged engine or in a second engine, not in its own training setting. *Dose-response
curve*: return plotted against a fidelity setting, borrowing pharmacology's name for effect against dose. *Fidelity schedule*: a
rule that changes a fidelity setting during training (coarse early, fine late), by analogy with a learning-rate schedule.
*Multi-fidelity RL (MFRL)*: training a policy with data from two or more simulators of different cost and accuracy. *LF / HF*: that
community's names for the cheap and the expensive simulator. *Control variate*: a cheap correlated quantity subtracted from an
expensive estimate to cut its variance without changing its mean. *Plateau criterion*: refine fidelity only when a validation or
return metric stops improving (PRDP, the multi-grid RL paper). *Cross-fidelity matrix*: train at every fidelity level, test at every
level; the diagonal is same-fidelity. *Time step* $h$: the physics integration interval; *decimation*: physics steps per policy
action. *Wall-clock matching*: giving every arm the same training time rather than the same step count. *Isolated knob*: one
engine setting changed with every other held fixed.

## 0. Verdict in one paragraph

**Killed (Gate 2B; Gate 4 fails with it).** The claim has two halves and the search finds each one already answered in the
neighbours' own words. (i) The *dose* half — "transferable return follows a dose-response curve in solver budget" — has, in the
fetched set, exactly two knob-isolated readings of return against solver iterations on policy rollouts in a rigid-body engine, and
both are flat inside noise: 2610.32277 Table 2 (solver iterations moved a cap-trained tracker's error by less than 0.006 rad;
"the first sim-to-sim gap we saw came from the harness, a missing floor and a wrong timestep, and not from the physics engines")
and SimuGuard D.2 (a frozen manipulation policy went from 74.4% to 86.7% success with more iterations, $p = 0.17$). This is the
evidence that killed CL-W44-08 two weeks ago, and this claim inherits it whole: a dose with no measured response is not a dose, and
the claim's own if_false sentence ("fixed budgets are right and the scheduler has no consumer") is the engine owners' stated
position (MJX tuning page, read in the W43 red team). (ii) The *schedule* half — "fidelity can be scheduled during training like a
learning rate" — is in print as a method three times on neighbouring fidelity axes: the adaptive multi-grid RL framework
(2207.03253, 2022: PPO "initially learned using computationally efficient low fidelity simulations ... Subsequently, the simulation
fidelity is increased in an adaptive manner", 61–76% compute saved, with a return-plateau criterion as the trigger), PRDP
(2502.19611, ICLR 2025: "The fidelity of iterative components is increased only if validation metrics plateau", on the
solver-iteration axis itself, for differentiable physics), and MFPG-PPO (2610.02505, 2026-10-01: an online per-iteration
allocation of a fixed sampling budget between a cheap and an expensive Isaac Lab configuration, "we adapt the allocation as the
policy changes", with LF pre-training as a routine first step). The design-optimisation branch of MFRL already treats "manual
fidelity scheduling" as a burden to be automated (2503.18229). And the measurement shape the claim proposes — a cross-fidelity
train–test matrix of PPO policies across fidelity levels with a per-task-class rule as the consequence — was published on
2026-09-09 for quadrotor wind-field fidelity (2610.06874) with the result "the matrix is remarkably flat ... No diagonal
(same-fidelity) advantage is visible" and the rule "fidelity investment should scale with platform wind sensitivity". So the
expected result on the claim's axis equals the field's stated knowledge (flat), the consequence equals the neighbours' published
recipe, and the unoccupied cell — the solver-iteration cap of a GPU rigid-body engine, scheduled during policy training — is empty
because every community positioned to fill it holds a flat reading for it. Verdict is the same from the solo and lab positions. What
survives is a watch item, not a claim: the physics time step is the one numerics knob with a published train-in / evaluate-in
effect (2610.32277: 2 ms for 5 ms moved error from 0.577 to 1.719 rad; MFPG-PPO's H1 LF bundle coarsens the step five-fold and
"LF-to-HF transfer is ... hard on H1"), and no fetched paper plots policy return against the step at fixed control period, which is
the footnote named in section 7.

## 5. Gate-by-gate verdict (position clause included)

Write $K$ for the solver iteration cap, $W$ for a fixed wall-clock budget, $\pi_K$ for the policy trained at cap $K$ under the full
protocol within $W$, and $J_{\mathrm{conv}}(\pi)$ for the return of policy $\pi$ in the converged engine. The claim's object is the
curve
$$R_a(K) = \mathbb{E}\big[J_{\mathrm{conv}}(\pi_K)\big] \quad\text{for task } a,$$
with the expectation over seeds and randomisation draws, and its shape clause is that two tasks $a, b$ of one class satisfy
$R_a(K)/R_a(K_{\max}) \approx R_b(K)/R_b(K_{\max})$ (pre-registered as a Spearman correlation above 0.8 between the normalised
curves). At matched wall-clock the number of training steps is $N(K) = W/(t_0 + K\,t_1)$, with $t_0$ the per-step cost outside the
solver and $t_1$ the cost of one solver iteration, so a schedule trades steps for accuracy and the comparison is the matched-$W$ one.

- **Gate 0 (premise hygiene):** the shipped values are fetched and dated (Playground XML $K = 1$ Go1, $K = 5$ Leap; Isaac Lab Franka
  MJWarp 20, pressure Q21); the conditioning set is written (seeds, randomisation draws, deterministic evaluation actions); the
  quantity is a property of (engine, task, algorithm). One defect: "a dose-response curve" presumes a monotone response, and the
  card paper that seeded the probe declines the solver axis exactly where convergence is non-monotone and multi-knob (its SIMPLEC
  reason), which is the contact-solver case (card, information asymmetry (a)). Passes on hygiene; the premise's evidence belongs to
  Gate 2.
- **Gate 1 (cheap to kill):** the named cheapest probe P-W43-01-2x2 is not a scorecarded record and rode on a probe killed with
  CL-W44-08; the scorecarded probe C-W43-16 (50 GPU-h, $150, 7 d) is under one GPU-week, so a probe under budget exists. "Both
  outcomes informative" is weak: the flat outcome is a number on the MJX tuning page. Pass on cost, weak on informativeness.
- **Gate 2 (pressure, route B — the ruler):** **fails (2B).** Three results side by side. *Expected, after this search*: $R_a(K)$ flat
  from the shipped cap to $K_{\max}$ on Go1 and Leap (the two isolated readings in print are inside noise; CL-W44-08 §5), so there is
  no shape to share and nothing for a schedule to exploit. *The field assumes*: the same — "Accurate solver forces are not so important
  in reinforcement learning" (MJX tuning page, W43 red team); the solver row flat in 2610.32277 Table 2; the engine owners ship $K = 1$
  and transfer to hardware. *Would change a belief*: a monotone, shared-shape response with a schedule beating fixed caps at matched
  $W$ — and that consequence is already the published recipe on neighbouring axes (rows 1–3), while the one published cross-fidelity
  matrix for PPO in robotics (row 4) came out flat with a per-platform rule. Expected equals assumed; the ruler is a documentation
  service.
- **Gate 3 (consumers):** engine teams (a default), Playground and Isaac Lab users (a recipe step), the lane's scheduler (a consumer).
  Three classes are nameable, but the actions change only on the positive result that Gate 2 says is not expected. Proper at best.
- **Gate 4 (mechanism and consequence):** **fails.** The if_true consequence — "Training recipes gain a fidelity schedule (coarse
  early, converged late)" — is 2207.03253's method, PRDP's method on the solver-iteration axis, and MFPG-PPO's LF pre-training step;
  the if_false consequence — "fixed budgets are right" — is the owners' current practice. The fork is between two published
