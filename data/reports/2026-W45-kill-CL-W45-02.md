Glossary (each term once). **Instruction-test mismatch** — the task text asks for one thing and the hidden tests grade
another. **Tighten** — repair by editing the instruction to say what the tests check. **Loosen** — repair by editing the
tests to accept any correct solution. **Repair convention** — which of the two a maintainer chooses. **Adopted repair** —
one repairer's edit copied by the other. **Clean divergence** — a task where one repairer changed only the instruction and
the other changed only the tests, so the two arms differ in one field. **Replicate interval** — the spread of a pass rate
from re-running the same cell (five trials in the probe). **Band contrast** — the gain of the two strongest models minus the
gain of the two middle ones. **DIF** (differential item functioning) — psychometrics' term for an item that is harder for one
group than another at equal ability; a repaired item is a changed item.

## 0. Verdict in one paragraph

**Killed.** Three failures, each on fetched text. (i) **Gate 0, the independence premise.** The two repairs are one repair
with two authors: the Terminal-Bench 2.1 README (in PR #53) says "Terminal-Bench 2.1 is a more verified iteration of
Terminal-Bench 2.0. 26 tasks were modified to fix bugs, modify timeouts or resources, or improve robustness to reward
hacking." and "many were taken directly from [Z.ai's Terminal-Bench 2.0 Verified changes]"; Z.ai's card says "We are
excited to announce our collaboration with the official Terminal-Bench team to release **Terminal-Bench 2.1** — the verified
version of Terminal-Bench, which fixes many of the issues identified in this review." and "jointly reviewing tasks,
validating fixes, and aligning on evaluation methodology". (ii) **Gate 1, the probe parks at its own step 0.** The file
diff of PR #53 against the Verified change log gives, of the 11 shared tasks: 4 where 2.1 adopted Verified's tightening
(adaptive-rejection-sampler, build-pmars, caffe-cifar-10's solver path, install-windows-3.11's socket path), 3 clean
divergences (hf-model-inference, train-fasttext, polyglot-c-py), 3 mixed (sam-cell-seg, install-windows-3.11's VGA half,
caffe-cifar-10's cmake test), 1 untouched by 2.1 (path-tracing) and 2 where 2.1 tightened differently or fixed the
environment instead (mteb-retrieve, protein-assembly). The probe's own rule — "parked at step 0 if fewer than four tasks have
a clean one-field divergence" — fires at 3; and of the 3, two have no headroom for any repair in the paper's own Table 7
(hf-model-inference 88.3% to 86.7%, train-fasttext 5.0% to 3.3%, pooled over 19 entries), so the loosen arm moves one task
(polyglot-c-py 5.0% to 80.0%). The pre-registered kill ("agree within the interval on at least five tasks") cannot be
satisfied or refuted on this material. (iii) **Gate 2B, the surprise is stated knowledge.** That the two conventions are not
interchangeable is asserted by both repairers and by a third maintainer team with the opposite recommendation: 2611.08417
App. A.3 ("stating the grader's expectation tells the agent part of the solution, while relaxing the grader leaves the task as
hard as its contributor intended"), Z.ai ("without adding explicit hints or solutions") and SWE-Bench Pro V2 (2610.24206,
Lessons (2): "weakening a test to fit an instruction changes what the benchmark measures"). That instruction tightening
lifts models inconsistently is measured in 2610.22760 ("their benefit is not consistent across models", three models, 68
SWE-bench Pro instances) and 2607.07593 (two models diverge under 17 problem-statement mutations); that test-strength
changes reorder agents is measured in SWE-ABS 2603.00520 (top agent to fifth) and 2604.01518 (top-10 drops of 4.2 to 9.0
points). What no one has done — matched arms of two conventions on the same tasks with a replicate floor and a band sign — is
the residue, and Terminal-Bench 2.0 cannot carry it (one movable clean task; 2.1 declared saturated by Z.ai: "We also believe
Terminal-Bench 2.1 is no longer suitable for measuring the performance of the latest models"). The residue lives on SWE-Bench
Pro, where two contemporaneous repairs with opposite conventions exist (Pro Verified 2609.08149, Pro V2 2610.24206) and V2
says it "release[s] all grades"; that is a new claim, filed below, not a re-open of this one.

## 3. The two closest papers' limitation and scope text (verbatim, grep-verified)

**2611.08417 (the card; no Limitations section — the scope sentences).** App. A.3: "Terminal-Bench 2.1 repairs these 11
tasks. It adopts Verified's repair on four tasks." ... "when an instruction and its test disagree, Verified edits the
instruction to state what the test checks, whereas Terminal-Bench 2.1 more often edits the test to accept any correct
solution; stating the grader's expectation tells the agent part of the solution, while relaxing the grader leaves the task as
hard as its contributor intended" ... "TB2.1 adds no harness-specific changes, so every agent is evaluated on the same task."
Sec. 4: "Agreement measures consistency rather than correctness." ... "these metrics measure retrospective agreement with the
release rather than independent defect discovery". Sec. 6.1: "The two weakest agents, which fall below 40% on TB 2.0
accuracy, both fall below the fit and lose accuracy on TB 2.1." ... "repairs that make hard tasks solvable matter little to
agents that rarely solve them". Sec. 8: "the broad ordering is stable under these repairs, but absolute performance estimates
and comparisons between nearby agents can depend on the benchmark version". The paper never says the two conventions were
compared on an agent; it says which it chose and why.

**2610.22760 Between Spoilers and Guesswork, Sec. 4 and Limitations.** "additional reference-derived descriptions can improve
resolution, but their benefit is not consistent across models." ... "These comparisons alone do not establish whether the
descriptions help by clarifying requirements or by supplying implementation guidance." ... "The effect of selection varies
across models and benchmarks even when hints contain only names." Limitations: "Budget constraints limited each reported
configuration to one trajectory per instance, making small differences sensitive to stochastic variation." ... "Small
benchmark subsets and a single agent harness limit generalizability". The tighten arm's model-dependence is in print without
a replicate floor; the paper names the floor as its own gap.

**Third, 2610.24206 SWE-Bench Pro V2 (Lessons and Limitations).** "Repair instructions, keep tests fixed, and validate repairs
blind: weakening a test to fit an instruction changes what the benchmark measures, and a blind re-implementation checks that
the edited instruction suffices." Sec. 2 on Pro Verified: "We never modify a hidden test to match an instruction, and we
discard tasks whose defect lies in the tests." ... "We validate each instruction repair with a blind human re-implementation,
while they rely on model trial runs." Sec. 3: "contradictions whose fix lies in the tests were discarded". Limitations: "The
protocol stops retrieval at test time, not memorization during training." Two maintainer teams now recommend opposite
conventions in print, and neither measured the other's.

## 4. Why not done, and why now

Not done on Terminal-Bench 2.0 because the material does not exist: the two repairs share authorship (Z.ai card; 2.1
README), 2.1 adopted Verified's tightening on four of the 11 tasks, only three tasks diverge cleanly, and of those two sit at
the ends of the scale in the paper's own Table 7 (88.3% and 5.0% pooled), leaving one task (polyglot-c-py, 5.0% to 80.0%) to
carry a two-convention contrast. Z.ai itself declared 2.1 unfit for current models. "Why has nobody compared two
independent repairs on outcomes" in general: until 2026-10-16 no benchmark had two independent repairs with opposite
conventions and released grades. The enabling piece is SWE-Bench Pro V2 (2610.24206, 2026-10-16: 480 of 731 tasks repaired or
retired, tests held fixed, "release all grades") beside SWE-Bench Pro Verified (2609.08149, 2026-09-08: 102 refined tasks,
test-side refinements per V2's description); their overlap is the first shipped two-convention pair, three weeks old. Scale
AI holds the V2 trial logs and has written the opposite lesson from Terminal-Bench's; they are the natural first mover and
have not been asked.
