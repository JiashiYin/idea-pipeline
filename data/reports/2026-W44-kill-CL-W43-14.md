# Kill search — CL-W43-14: single-run leaderboards and the run-to-run spread of cost, effort and harness effects

Date: 2026-11-02. Claim: `CL-W43-14` (L1, rank 2, champion candidate): "Agent leaderboards report cost, effort and

Glossary (once). *Cell*: one (model, harness, task) or (model, effort, task) combination on a grid. *Run-to-run
spread*: the variation between repeated runs of one cell with every setting fixed. *Task sampling*: the variation
that comes from which tasks are in the suite. *G-study*: a variance-components analysis that splits an observed
score into facets (task, run, harness, model and their interactions). *pass^k*: the probability that all $k$ runs of
a task succeed; *pass@k*: that at least one does. *ICC* (intraclass correlation): the share of total variance that
sits between units (tasks) rather than within them (runs). *Geometric standard deviation*: the multiplicative spread
of a log-normal quantity; $\times 1.34$ means one standard deviation is a factor of 1.34.

## 3. Verdicts by clause

**Harness clause** ("harness effects from single runs whose spread is larger than the differences ranked"). Occupied
at the mechanism level by 2609.33812 (agents at fixed model are harnesses; run SD $>$ between-pairing spread; tens to
a hundred runs per arm to resolve) and at the multi-task level by 2610.01618 (54% run-to-run across a configuration
grid), with the leaderboard-scale harness grids already replicated or intervalled: HarnessTax (3 attempts per task,
task bootstrap: harness has "little effect on task success rate"), 2609.11987 (task-bootstrap intervals include
zero), 2610.05778 (harness and its interaction about a quarter of outcome variance). Trial by Trace (2610.32285) is
one more single-run grid, and its "does not estimate within-cell repeatability" is a scope sentence, not an open
question.

**Cost clause.** The within-cell cost spread is measured: 2.2-fold median swing over three runs (2609.33812), up to
30$\times$ in tokens (2604.22750), geometric SD $\times 1.34$ at fifteen repeats (2608.25399). Written as a run share
of log-cost variance against the import S6xF1's own between-task figure (log-cost SD 1.4–1.6 per task on 2610.00917):
$$\mathrm{share}_{\mathrm{run}} = \frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{run}}^2}{\sigma_{\mathrm{run}}^2 + \sigma_{\mathrm{task}}^2},
\qquad \sigma_{\mathrm{run}} = \ln 1.34 = 0.29 \Rightarrow 3.7\%, \qquad
\sigma_{\mathrm{run}} = \frac{\ln 2.2}{1.69} = 0.47 \Rightarrow 8.9\%,$$
where $1.69$ is the expected range of three normal draws in units of $\sigma$. The claim's `if_false` reads "Run share
of cost variance under 5%: single-run tables are adequate"; the better-controlled estimate sits under it. For a
paired harness comparison over $T = 63$ tasks at one run per cell, the standard error of the mean log-cost difference
from run noise alone is $\sqrt{2}\,\sigma_{\mathrm{run}}/\sqrt{T} = 0.052$ to $0.084$, so the smallest resolvable cost
ratio is $e^{1.96 \times 0.052} = 1.11$ to $1.18$, under every harness cost effect in print (1.2–2.1$\times$ within a
model, 2609.33812 Table 13; 1.2–1.6$\times$ per solved task, 2609.11987; "up to 5x", HarnessTax; under a quarter of
the cost per task, 2610.00917). The one honest residual: the mean of dollars is heavy-tail dominated (the 30$\times$
tail), so the resolvable ratio on the dollar mean is wider than on the log mean — a replicate-count question for mean
cost that is a service to a board, not a paper.

**Effort clause.** On cost, effort moves the mean $\times 4.8$ against a $\times 1.34$ run spread (2608.25399): the
clause is a published negative. On accuracy, the field already reads the frontier effort response as flat
(Terminal-Bench 4.0 R03: three effort levels inside one interval at $n = 330$ trials; Haiku 5.5 card L04: under 2
points across five levels for three models; 2608.25399: no credible effect on solve rate where tasks sit at ceiling),
and HAL's "21 of 36 equal or lower" is the sign count a flat effect plus noise produces (expected 18 of 36). The
effort $\times$ task interaction on an agent benchmark is unmeasured, and its expected reading equals the field's
assumption: Gate 2B.

**The rival term.** L08 ("most of the uncertainty comes from tasks rather than run-to-run variance"), 2609.33044
("finite prompt sampling rather than the judge"), 2610.00651 (scaffold coverage, not tasks, not runs, bounds model
reliability) and 2610.05572 (99.93% of pairs cross-unit) all put the task or scaffold term first; the pressure #7
formula $\mathrm{Var}(\bar p) = \frac{1}{T}\big(\mathrm{Var}_t(p_t) + \mathbb{E}_t[p_t(1-p_t)]/m\big)$ says sharpening
drives the within-task term to zero. The claim needed to beat this rival to be a surprise; the fetched cost numbers
side with the rival.

## 6. Killing experiment check

The claim's cheapest probe, import S7xF3 step 0 (\$0, three days: the single-run band on Trial by Trace's 27
adjacent harness gaps), costs under one GPU-week and its result is predictable from rows 1–2 and §3: with run
flip rates of 20–40% on Agent-pass (C-W44-03's own expectation) and 95 tasks, the per-model resolvable gap is about
$2 \times 1.96 \sqrt{0.3 \cdot 0.7 / 95} \approx 18$ points, so most adjacent harness gaps (12–23 points) are
unresolved and the two largest survive — the same shape 2609.11987 and HarnessTax report. The experiment is cheap
and its outcome is not news; Gate 1 passes and Gate 2B fails.
