# Kill search — the meta-benchmark's concepts: proxies, exploitability, and exploitability as a trace-free predictor of transfer

Date: 2026-10-24. Claims: `CL-W43-J03` (fidelity scores do not predict transfer; budgeted worst-case violation does) and
`CL-W43-J04` (engines differ in exploitability more than in fidelity; speed reorders at fixed accuracy); the ruler's
functionals (energy-from-nothing, complementarity residual, metamorphic defects, Richardson convergence) assessed as
concepts. Jacob's null hypothesis under test: none of the framing or proxies is novel. Procedure: `prompts/03_kill.md`;
eight arXiv API queries (relevance order, all time), five web searches, ten abstracts fetched by id, two PDFs read
(GPUSimBench, GPUPhysBench, 2026-10-24). Semantic Scholar skipped (no key). Every neighbour below was fetched this
session; depth marked [abstract] or [page] or [full].

## 0. Verdict in one paragraph

**The proxies are not novel and were never the claim: they are standard verification and contact-mechanics criteria
(NCP violation is the solver literature's own test; metamorphic testing is routine for CPS and driving simulators;
Richardson is textbook), packaged dimensionlessly along policy-visited states — an instrument, recorded as such.
"Exploitability" is an occupied word with three literatures behind it (game theory's best-response value; MBRL's
model exploitation and the simulation lemma; the 2026 preference-reversal theory 2605.15960 and the adversarial
objective for learned simulators 2605.29032); what is not found is an oracle-free, conservation-law-rewarded,
budgeted adversarial measurement on engineered engines — a mechanism difference, not a domain difference, so
`CL-W43-J04` survives, weakly, and must define its term against those literatures. The fidelity axis is now occupied by
GAUGE 2608.05948 (Aug 2026): 22 real-grounded task families across rigid, cable, textile and volumetric materials, three
GPU engines scored by trajectory error — far stronger than GPUSimBench — so the ruler's validation layer uses GAUGE
rather than competing with it. Trace-free prediction of transfer has a direct precedent, RoboGauge 2602.00678 (Jan
2026): sim-to-sim robustness metrics that predict a *policy's* transferability without hardware; Kadian 2019
(1912.06321) is the single-artifact datum that an engine exploit (wall sliding) ruined sim-to-real predictivity. Not
found anywhere: a *simulator-level* exploitability number tested as a predictor of transfer across engines and
tasks, or any test of whether GAUGE-type fidelity predicts transfer. `CL-W43-J03` survives on that gap, redesigned as a
horse race of trace-free predictors (GAUGE fidelity, sim-to-sim robustness à la RoboGauge, Richardson error, $X$)
against public real outcomes; its power is bounded by tens of policy–sim–real triples. No hardware is needed for
either claim; persuasion comes from adversary calibration and prediction of public real outcomes, and the paper must
say the hardware confirmation is owed from a lab position.**

## 3. Verdicts by component

- **Proxies (energy, complementarity, metamorphic, Richardson): killed as claims, kept as instrument.** Standard
  criteria from V&V and contact mechanics; the packaging (dimensionless, along visited states, one profile per engine)
  is engineering, cited to 2304.06372, metamorphic-testing practice and Richardson.
- **Exploitability as a quantity (`CL-W43-J04`): survived-kill, weak.** The word and the concept are taken (best-response
  value in games; model exploitation and the simulation lemma in MBRL; preference reversal in 2605.15960; adversarial
  objective for learned simulators in 2605.29032). The unoccupied piece is the *oracle-free* reward (conservation-law
  violation needs no true model) applied by a budgeted, calibrated optimiser portfolio to *engineered* engines, reported
  as exploits per GPU-hour. Mechanism difference, so survival; weak because the field's existing exploit test (run in a
  second engine — PolySim, Humanoid-Gym, ROBOGATE's 97-point gap) already measures the whole-engine version. The
  fidelity side of the claim must use GAUGE's scores, not our own. Speed-at-fixed-accuracy has the 2015 CPU precedent and
  no GPU-era successor in the fetched set.
- **Exploitability as a trace-free predictor of transfer (`CL-W43-J03`): survived-kill, redesigned.** RoboGauge already
  predicts transfer without hardware from sim-to-sim robustness (policy-level); Kadian showed one exploit ruined
  predictivity. Nobody has asked whether a *simulator-level* number predicts predictivity, nor whether GAUGE-type
  fidelity does. Redesign: a horse race of trace-free predictors — GAUGE fidelity, sim-to-sim robustness (RoboGauge /
  PolySim style), Richardson error, $X(B)$ — against the public real outcomes (R2S-Eval, SimFoundry, Kaedim, SIMPLER,
  tens of triples). Pre-registered expectation unchanged ($X$ above fidelity); the honest register is "a first
  correlation table", not a definitive claim, until the triple count grows.
- **Real experiments: not required for either claim.** The real anchor is public: GAUGE's trajectories for fidelity, the
  paired policy tables for transfer. Persuasion: (i) the adversary rediscovers known exploits unprompted (calibration),
  (ii) $X$ predicts public real outcomes better than fidelity, (iii) sim-to-sim as the field's own secondary check. The
  hardware confirmation is owed from a lab position and the paper says so.

## 4. Why not done

An enabling piece landed: GAUGE (Aug 2026) made real-grounded fidelity a measured object across materials and three GPU
engines; RoboGauge (Jan 2026) made trace-free transfer prediction respectable; 2605.15960 and 2605.29032 (May 2026)
stated exploitability formally for learned models; minutes-scale GPU training (2025) made budgeted adversarial search
per engine affordable. The combination — an oracle-free adversary on engineered engines, raced against fidelity as a
predictor of transfer — sits between the simulator-benchmark, MBRL-theory and sim-to-real-evaluation communities, each
of which measures one piece.

## 6. Checklist lessons (2026-10-24)

- A proposed "novel metric" is searched first in the solver and software-testing literatures where it is a routine
  criterion (NCP violation, metamorphic relations); the novelty budget then goes to the measure, not the functional.
- An occupied word (exploitability) is defined against each literature that owns it before it is used in a headline.
- The fidelity axis of any simulator claim is checked against GAUGE 2608.05948 first; a fidelity measurement of our own
  is a follow-up unless it covers what GAUGE does not (policy-visited states, optimiser-chosen states).
- Web search found the two closest precedents (RoboGauge, Kadian 2019) that the arXiv API queries ranked low; both
  channels run in every kill search.
