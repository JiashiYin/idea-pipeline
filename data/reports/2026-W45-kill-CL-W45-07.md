Glossary (each term once): **design matrix** — the 0/1 table with one row per episode and one column per memory (plus a
column of ones, the *baseline* or intercept), a 1 where the memory was retrieved. **Estimable** (the card says
*determined*) — a linear combination of the unknown values that some average of outcomes estimates without bias; it holds
exactly when the combination's coefficient vector lies in the row space of the design matrix (Rao 1973, as the card
cites). **Kernel** — the directions in value space that change no episode's expected outcome; every estimable
combination is orthogonal to the whole kernel. **Level** — one memory's own value $\theta_{\{i\}}$. **Contrast** — a
difference $\theta_{\{i\}} - \theta_{\{j\}}$, or any combination whose coefficients sum to zero. **Same-size swap** —
replacing one retrieved memory by another at the same retrieval size $k$. **$F_{\mathrm{pair}}$** — the claim's
ruler: the fraction of pairwise contrasts that are estimable, among memories retrieved at least five times. **One-way
layout** — the textbook linear model $y = \mu + \alpha_{\text{group}} + \text{noise}$, in which the intercept $\mu$ is
confounded with the group effects. **Block** (the card's Definition A.5) — a connected component of the graph that joins
two memories when some episode retrieved both.

## 0. Verdict in one paragraph

**Killed.** Three things kill it, in order of cost. (i) The headline's one quantifier is impossible on the log it names.
The card's plain top-$k$ log is "300 questions retrieved over 24 passes" with a deterministic retriever, so it has at most
300 distinct design rows and the row space has dimension $r \le 300$; the estimable pairwise contrasts partition the
retrieved memories into classes with at most $r$ independent contrasts in all, so the number of estimable pairs is at most
$\binom{r+1}{2} = 45{,}150$. "At least one in five" then needs at most 672 memories ever retrieved, while 300 questions
retrieving 5 each with gold recall 0.93 (the card's number: "top-$k$ retrieves 0.93 ... of the gold memories") retrieve
well over a thousand distinct paragraphs; on a synthetic deterministic top-5 log with 1,173 to 1,500 retrieved memories
the bound is 0.04 to 0.07 and the sampled $F_{\mathrm{pair}}$ is 0.0000. The probe's own first kill clause
("$F_{\mathrm{pair}} < 0.20$ under plain top-$k$ while LEDGER's log gives above 0.60") therefore fires before any reader
call: LEDGER's side is at least $0.797^2 = 0.635$ by the card's own fraction, since two determined levels make a
determined contrast. Gate 0 fails (a premise number was not counted) and Gate 1's kill fires at step 0. (ii) The
qualitative clause — levels are not estimable but contrasts are, and a fixed-size decision reads only contrasts — is the
textbook statement for a rank-deficient one-way design (Scheffé 1959, Searle 1971, Rao 1973 §4a; the connectedness theorem for
block designs, book-level, not fetched), it is the card paper's own first-page sentence ("the history fixes only the differences between values"),
it is the card's own H.1 reading of slate off-policy evaluation ("sets the undetermined part aside, because it does not
change the value of a policy"), it is Swaminathan et al. 2017's Lemma 2 (the all-ones vector is in the null space and the
policy value is unchanged by projecting it out), and it is the unbiased-learning-to-rank identifiability result of
2309.15560 ("identifiable up to a scaling transformation, which is sufficient for pairwise ranking objectives", with a
graph-connectivity condition that is the same object as the card's blocks). The surprise equals the field's stated
knowledge: Gate 2B fails. (iii) The decision clause — "every decision a store makes is a same-size swap" and "ranking is
all a store does" — is occupied from the causal side by Causal Memory Policy 2610.02070, whose Proposition 1 defines memory
utility by exactly these interventions ("so that $|R|$ remains fixed across both arms") and whose main negative is the
claim's retention clause reversed: "Identification does not deliver a retention policy" and "none improves on retaining

## 1. Mathematics check (the brief's question): is "contrasts are estimable although levels are not" textbook?

Yes. In the one-way layout with $g$ groups, $y_{ij} = \mu + \alpha_i + e_{ij}$, the design matrix $[\mathbf{1} \mid
\mathbf{Z}]$ has the intercept column equal to the sum of the group-indicator columns, so its rank is $g$, not $g+1$; a
combination $\lambda_0 \mu + \sum_i \lambda_i \alpha_i$ is estimable iff $\lambda_0 = \sum_i \lambda_i$ (Searle, *Linear
Models*, 1971, ch. 5; Scheffé, *The Analysis of Variance*, 1959, §1.4 and ch. 3; Rao 1973 §4a.2, the citation the card
itself uses — the two books are not fetched, the statement is standard; Rao is quoted through the card: "a combination
$\mathbf{c}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\beta}$ is estimable when some linear function of $\mathbf{r}$ has expectation
$\mathbf{c}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\beta}$ for every $\boldsymbol{\beta}$, which holds exactly when $\mathbf{c}$ lies in the row
space of $\mathbf{A}$"). Hence no single $\alpha_i$ is estimable and every contrast ($\sum_i \lambda_i = 0$) is. The card's
fixed-size design at resolution $d = 1$ is this layout with $k$ ones per row instead of one: each row of $\mathbf{X}_1 =
[\mathbf{1}_T \mid \mathbf{X}]$ has intercept 1 and exactly $k$ ones, so $\mathbf{1}_T = \frac{1}{k}\mathbf{X}\mathbf{1}_n$,
which is Theorem 1's kernel vector $\mathbf{h}_\emptyset = (-k, 1, \dots, 1)$ written as a column dependence. The
one-way layout's estimability condition becomes: $(c_0, \mathbf{c})$ is orthogonal to $\mathbf{h}_\emptyset$ iff $k\,c_0 =
\sum_i c_i$; for a pure contrast $c_0 = 0$ and $\sum_i c_i = 0$, so every contrast passes this one test. In block-design
language (textbook, not fetched; Bailey and Cameron 1111.3768 [abstract]: optimality "can be calculated from the concurrence graph of
the design"), episodes are blocks of size $k$ and memories are treatments, and all elementary treatment contrasts are
estimable iff the design is connected — the card's Definition A.5 (blocks as components of the co-retrieval graph) is the
same object, and 2309.15560's "identifiability graph" is the same object for click logs.

The claim's "two-line identity" is a corollary of Theorem 1's own proof, not an addition to it. Theorem 1 shows
$\mathbf{x}_S^{\top}\mathbf{h}_B = 0$ for every set $S$ with $|S| = k$ (the proof never uses that $S$ was observed): for
$B \subseteq S$ the sum $\sum_{j \notin B}\mathbf{1}[B \cup \{j\} \subseteq S] = k - |B|$ cancels the second term; for $B
\not\subseteq S$ both terms are zero. So $(\mathbf{x}_{S'} - \mathbf{x}_S)^{\top}\mathbf{h}_B = 0 - 0$ for any two size-$k$
sets. Checked numerically on random sets at $d = 1$ and $d = 2$ (maximum absolute inner product $0.0$). What the identity
does **not** say is the claim's load-bearing step: that the kernel of a *real* top-$k$ log is the family
$\{\mathbf{h}_B\}$. At $d = 1$ that family is one direction; the kernel of $\mathbf{X}_1$ on the card's log has dimension at
least $n + 1 - r \ge 2{,}972 - 300 = 2{,}672$ (and at least $N + 1 - 300$ among the $N$ retrieved memories), and the other
directions do void swaps — the card says so for aliasing ("the invisible change $\mathbf{e}_{\{i\}} - \mathbf{e}_{\{j\}}$
raises $\varphi_i$ and lowers $\varphi_j$ by the same amount") and for nesting (Lemma B.2's $(-1, \mathbf{1}_C/k_1)$, which
is orthogonal to swaps inside $C$ but not to swaps between $C$ and its complement). **Novelty, if any, is the
application; and the application is already in the card's own text (the "differences" sentence; the slate remark) and in
CMP's estimand.** Gate 2B is graded accordingly in section 6.

The rank bound used in the verdict, written out. Let $\mathcal{R}$ be the row space of $\mathbf{X}_1$ restricted to the
$N$ memories retrieved at least five times, $r = \dim \mathcal{R} \le$ (number of distinct retrieved sets) $\le 300$.
Define $i \sim j$ when $(0, \mathbf{e}_i - \mathbf{e}_j) \in \mathcal{R}$; this is an equivalence relation (sums of
estimable contrasts are estimable). The contrasts inside a class of size $m_c$ span a space of dimension $m_c - 1$, and
classes have disjoint supports, so $\sum_c (m_c - 1) \le r$. The number of estimable pairs is $\sum_c \binom{m_c}{2} \le
\binom{r+1}{2}$ (convexity: one class), hence
$$F_{\mathrm{pair}} \;\le\; \frac{\binom{r+1}{2}}{\binom{N}{2}} \;\le\; \frac{45{,}150}{\binom{N}{2}},$$
which is below $0.20$ for every $N \ge 673$. Under a deterministic retriever the 24 passes repeat the same 300 rows, and
the "retrieved at least five times" filter is vacuous (every retrieved memory has frequency a multiple of 24). The
synthetic check (300 question-specific 5-sets with 0%, 10%, 30% of slots replaced by another question's paragraph; 24
passes): $N = 1{,}500 / 1{,}361 / 1{,}173$, $\operatorname{rank}\mathbf{X}_1 = 300$ in all three, bound $0.040 / 0.049 /
0.066$, sampled $F_{\mathrm{pair}}$ over 4,000 random pairs $= 0.0000$ in all three. A pairwise contrast needs two
retrieved sets differing in exactly one element (or a chain of such), which a relevance retriever over question-specific
paragraphs almost never produces.

## 5. Why not done, and why now

## 8. Checklist lessons (2026-11-09)

- A headline quantifier about a *fraction determined* on a named log is bounded by the log's number of distinct design
  rows before any data is read: estimable pairwise contrasts number at most $\binom{r+1}{2}$ for row-space dimension $r$,
  and $r \le$ the number of distinct retrieved sets ($\le$ distinct queries under a deterministic retriever; repeated passes
  add nothing). Count the rows before writing the quantifier. (CL-W45-07: 300 rows versus "one in five" among $>1{,}000$
  memories.)
- "Levels not estimable, contrasts estimable" is the one-way layout (Scheffé 1959; Searle 1971; Rao 1973 §4a), connectedness
  for block designs (textbook; Bailey–Cameron 1111.3768 fetched), and "identifiable up to a transformation that the ranking
  objective does not see" (2309.15560 for click logs; Swaminathan 2017 Lemma 2 for slates). Any claim whose mechanism is
  this sentence has its novelty in the application only, and the application's own paper usually states it on page one
  (the card: "the history fixes only the differences between values"). Grep the seed paper for "difference", "contrast",
  "policy value" before grading Gate 2B.
- A kernel vector that a theorem *names* is not the kernel a *log* has: $\mathbf{h}_\emptyset$ is one direction of a kernel
  of dimension $\ge n + 1 - r$; an orthogonality statement about the named vector says nothing about the rest. Compute the
  kernel dimension from $n$ and $r$ before arguing from one named direction.
- The arXiv API's phrase and OR handling is unreliable for statistics vocabulary (Q5 = 0 on a known occupant; Q6, Q9, Q10
  returned 361–540 off-target hits); for textbook statements, fetch the known paper by `id_list` and cite the book, and
  record the defective counts as defects, not densities.
