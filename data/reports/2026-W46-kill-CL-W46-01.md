Glossary (each term once). **Failure attribution**: naming the agent and the step a human judged responsible for a failed
multi-agent run. **Decisive step**: the earliest step whose correction would have reversed the failure, as the corpus defines it.
**Harness**: the orchestration code that runs the agents, relays tool results and prints the final answer. **Provider error path**:
an HTTP error returned by the model API (here Azure OpenAI's content filter, "Error code: 400") that ends the loop. **Fallback
answer**: a "FINAL ANSWER" line the harness prints after a crash, with no agent having produced it. **Terminal message**: the last
record of a released trajectory. **Wilson interval**: a 95% confidence interval for a proportion that stays inside $[0,1]$ at small
counts. **Mode label**: a MAST failure-mode code (one of 14), assigned to a whole trace and naming no agent.

## 0. Verdict in one paragraph

**Killed: the pre-registered kill fired on free data inside the kill search (Gate 1 clause met in the negative), with Gate 0, Gate 2B
and Gate 4 failing behind it. A footnote survives, not a claim.** The headline's quantity was computed on every run it names.
Who&When: 26 of 58 hand-crafted runs carry the provider's content-filter error and 25 end on a harness-printed FINAL ANSWER
(the seed paper's 26/58 and 25 reproduced exactly); 0 of 126 algorithm-generated runs do (one tool library's own HTTP 400 is quoted
in a tool result — the false-positive class). LongRCA Bench: **0 of 1,140** released trajectories end on a provider error (Wilson
upper bound 0.34%); the harness is already a named speaker in its schema (`Computer_terminal`, "Run finished: <reason>", the last
record in 972 of 1,140 runs), and its construction already excludes "Infrastructure, smoke-test, and debug runs" (2608.15242 sec.
3.2). MAST's human-labelled split is 19 flat-text traces with mode labels and **no responsible-agent label** at all (HF README schema;
tracker issue #7: "the dataset only contains 19 instances"), so the headline names a corpus that does not carry the label it
conditions on. Pooled over the corpora that do: $h = 26/1{,}324 = 0.020$, Wilson $[0.013, 0.029]$ — under the claim's own kill line
(pooled $h < 0.05$, upper bound under 0.08, LongRCA under 0.05). The mechanism also fails on the one corpus where the crash exists:
the human label's decisive step precedes the crash in 19 of 26 runs (median 21.5 messages earlier; labelled step median 8 against a
run length of 32.5), so "the human label landed on whoever spoke last" is false at step level (6 of 26). The recommendation the claim
would deliver is already in print in the seed paper's own words — "Attribution benchmarks should mark such runs, and evaluations of
agents should report harness-terminated runs separately from agent errors" (2611.13254 sec. 5) — which is the Gate 4 defect the
2026-11-08 lesson names (if_true equals the seed's fix). And the "open-items list" the scorecard quotes ("the same harness-fault
scan on MAST-Data, LongRCA or TRAVERSE") is not in the paper: it is the card's `occupies` inference, written into the claim as a
quotation. What survives is one sentence for the Who&When maintainers and a comment on 2611.13254: the content-filter runs are a
Who&When hand-crafted artefact (one framework, one provider, 2025), the two larger corpora are clean, and the 26 runs' labels sit on
the web surfer at twice the base rate (20 of 26 against 13 of 32) but on steps well before the crash.

## 3. The two closest papers' limitation and scope text (verbatim, grep-verified)

**2611.13254, sec. 4.6 and sec. 5.** "A deterministic scan of all raw Who&When trajectories finds this crash in 26 of 58
hand-crafted runs (45%), 25 of which end with such a fallback answer, and in 0 of 126 algorithm-generated runs. For the 25 fallback
runs the corpus attributes the failure to the orchestrator in 4 cases and to another agent (mostly the web surfer) in 21." ...
"Attribution methods evaluated on this split are therefore partly scored on runs whose final answer came from the harness, not from
any agent." **Sec. 5, "Harness faults belong in the denominator":** "Almost half of one benchmark split ends with the harness, not an
agent, producing the answer that gets scored. Attribution benchmarks should mark such runs, and evaluations of agents should report
harness-terminated runs separately from agent errors; otherwise a method that learns to blame the web surfer for a provider's HTTP 400
will look good." **Sec. 5.1:** "the harness-fault result is independent of annotation." **Sec. 6:** "The protocol, the records and the
scripts are included so the analysis can be repeated, extended to new corpora, and checked against human annotation." The strings
"harness-fault scan", "MAST-Data", "unscanned" and "contaminated split" occur zero times in the paper.

**2608.15242 LongRCA, sec. 3.2–3.3 and App. D.** "Failures arise from ordinary execution rather than injected errors.
Infrastructure, smoke-test, and debug runs are excluded because they are not completed task attempts." ... "Normalization preserves
original content, roles, and temporal order with stable 0-based indices". **App. D, Table 14:** "history — Ordered trajectory
records containing step, name, role, and content; includes the task instruction". **Sec. 3.3:** "Of 1,200 reviewed trajectories, 60
were excluded, leaving 1,140 human-annotated failed executions." ... "role agreement is 66.1%, step exact agreement is 40.3%".

**Third, 2610.17747, Limitations:** "Finally, we score only against the released annotations, so a judge that blames a different but
also valid step or agent counts as wrong." **Fourth, 2603.25001, sec. 1:** "The Who&When dataset and subsequent works implicitly assume
that the oracle of failure-inducing steps is deterministic".

## 4. Why has this not been done

The one-corpus scan was done on 2026-11-09 and its generalisation needed no technique: the released parquet files carry per-record
roles, and the whole census ran here in under three hours at \$0. The reason nobody published the other two numbers is that they are
zero and empty: LongRCA's construction excluded infrastructure runs and its 2026 generators (MiniMax-M2.5, Kimi-K2.5, Qwen3.5-Plus)
do not sit behind Azure's content filter, and MAST's human split has no agent labels to contaminate. "No reason found" for the
positive claim; a clean reason for its absence. The enabling piece the claim named (LongRCA v4, 2026-08-15) is real but enables a
negative.

## 5. Gate-by-gate verdict (claim level), with the position clause

- **Gate 0 (premise hygiene): fails in three places.** (i) The headline conditions on "runs carrying a responsible-agent label"
  and names MAST's annotated traces; MAD's human split carries mode labels only (schema: `round, mas_name, benchmark_name, trace_id,
  trace, annotations`), so one of the three named corpora has no members in the object. (ii) The scorecard's target value quotes an
  "open-items list" ("the same harness-fault scan on MAST-Data, LongRCA or TRAVERSE; and the effect of the 45% contaminated split")
  that is not in the paper; it is the card's `occupies` inference (third occurrence of the 2026-10-25 fabricated-quote lesson). (iii)
  The probe's gloss of a harness-terminated run includes "a max-turns notice" and any message "produced by the orchestration code";
  on LongRCA 972 of 1,140 runs end on the harness's "Run finished" record by protocol, so $z_i$ as glossed is 1 by construction on
  three sources and measures a record format, not a fault. The seed paper's object is narrower (provider error plus fallback answer)
  and that is what was counted here.
- **Gate 1 (cheap to kill): passes, and the kill fired.** C-W46-01's steps 0–1 ran inside this search: pooled $h = 0.020$,
  $[0.013, 0.029]$; LongRCA $0/1{,}140$; the clause "pooled $h < 0.05$, upper bound under 0.08, LongRCA and MAST under 0.05" is met.
- **Gate 2 (pressure): route A thin, route B fails.** The strain is one meta-paper's section; no leaderboard, tracker or disclosure
  asks for the census (the Who&When repository has zero issues; MAST's tracker asks about annotation blocks, not terminations). The
  pre-registered surprise ("$h \geq 0.10$ pooled, the field assumes 0") is the seed paper's thesis generalised, and on data it is
  the field's assumption that holds outside one split.
- **Gate 3 (consumers): passes as written**; for two of three classes the action is "nothing changes" once the large corpora are clean.
- **Gate 4 (mechanism and consequence): fails.** The mechanism "an annotator assigns the agent who spoke last before the
  termination" is contradicted on the 26 runs: the labelled step equals the crash index in 6, precedes it in 19 (median 21.5 messages;
  labelled step median 8 of 32.5); the annotators' reasons describe earlier retrieval failures ("WebSurfer's inability to reliably
  access the requested documents"). The if_true action (mark such runs; report harness-terminated runs separately) is the seed paper's
  sec. 5 recommendation verbatim, so the result cannot fork the consequence (checklist 2026-11-08, CL-W45-01).
- **Gate 5 (durability): passes in form** and is already LongRCA's practice.
- **Gate 6 (ownership, density, race): fails.** 2611.13254 owns the mechanism, the number, the reading and the fix; LongRCA owns
  the corpus-side rule and the speaker field; MP-Bench and 2610.17747 own "Who&When's numbers are partly a benchmark artefact" with
  their own defects; 2610.17418 owns the infrastructure category. **Position clause. Solo:** killed — the data are public and the
  answer is negative. **Lab:** killed — a corpus owner adds a `termination` field in an afternoon, and LongRCA already has one.

## 6. Consequence for the probes, and the residue

- **C-W46-01 (the census): killed — it was run.** Steps 0–1 completed on all 1,324 labelled runs; the kill clause fired. Step 2
  (two readers, kappa) is unnecessary at $0/1{,}140$ and $26/58$ reproduced exactly.
- **C-W46-02 (the Who&When re-score): killed as a probe of this claim; its premise is false at step level.** The 26 crash runs' labels
  are not "the last speaker" (6 of 26 at the crash step), so holding them out does not test recency learning. What remains computable
  is a footnote: agent-level accuracy on the 32 clean hand-crafted runs for any method that releases per-run predictions, and the
  observation that the web surfer is labelled in 20 of 26 crash runs against 13 of 32 clean ones.
- **Residue (footnote, not a claim):** "Who&When's hand-crafted split carries the provider's content-filter crash in 26 of 58 runs
  and a harness speaker label (`Orchestrator (termination condition)`) on 31 of 58; LongRCA Bench carries 0 of 1,140 provider-error
  terminations and already records the harness as `Computer_terminal` with a termination reason; MAST's human split carries no
  agent labels. The contamination is one framework's 2025 artefact and the attribution field's larger corpora are clean of it." Its
  home is a comment to the authors of 2611.13254 (whose paper says the scan "can be repeated, extended to new corpora") and a note
  to the Who&When maintainers. Cost: done; zero further.
