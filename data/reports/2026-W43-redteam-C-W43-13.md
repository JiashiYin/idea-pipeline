**Terms (one gloss each).** *Policy pair*: two policies evaluated on the same task in real and in sim. *Resolvable pair*:
the real success-rate gap exceeds its binomial standard error at the trial count used (the record's Wald test). *Sign
agreement A*: the sim orders the pair as the real robot did. *True sign T*: the order of the two policies' true success
probabilities; *q_r* = P(real sign = T), *q_s* = P(sim sign = T). *Plug-in ceiling P̄*: the record's mean of Φ(|p1−p2|/SE)
over pairs, the observed-gap estimate of q_r. *κ* (the record's): (A − ½)/(P̄ − ½). *Attenuation ceiling*: a correlation
between two noisy measures cannot exceed the geometric mean of their reliabilities (Spearman 1904). *Noise ceiling*
(neuroscience RSA): the same bound estimated from repeated measurements of the criterion. *Winner's curse*: pairs
selected for a large observed gap have, on average, a smaller true gap. *Paired design*: both policies run on the same
fixed initial positions, so their real noise is shared. *Cluster bootstrap*: resampling tasks, not pairs, because pairs
within a task share policies.

### T-O1 — the split's unresolvable half is uninformative about the simulator by construction, and both kill thresholds are produced by a perfect simulator (OPEN → kill)
- **Objection.** Model: true success rates θ_i; real estimate p_i ~ Bin(n, θ_i)/n; sim estimate s_i ~ Bin(m, φ_i)/m; for a
  pair, T = sign(θ_i − θ_j), R = sign(p_i − p_j), S = sign(s_i − s_j); q_r = P(R = T), q_s = P(S = T). With R and S
  independent given the pair,

  A = P(R = S) = q_r·q_s + (1 − q_r)(1 − q_s) = ½ + 2(q_r − ½)(q_s − ½).

  A is symmetric in q_r and q_s: a low A on a subset is equally explained by a bad sim or an unreliable real sign, and a
  high A needs both arms right about T. On pairs selected as unresolvable (observed |p_i − p_j| < 1.96·SE), q_r is small by
  selection, so A says nothing about q_s. Simulation (`theory_sim.py`, 2,000 cohort replicates, seed 43): a sim that knows
  T exactly (q_s = 1), scored against SIMPLER's REAL_PERF taken as truth with the Appendix-B denominators, gets
  A = 0.895 on observed-unresolvable pairs (0.864 with m = 80 sim trials, YUBI's count) — the record's kill line of 0.9
  is where a perfect simulator lands. On a same-family cohort (k = 5, θ ~ U[0.45, 0.70], 7 tasks) a perfect sim gets
  A_unres = 0.69 at n = 20 and 0.57 at n = 5 — the record's "expected 0.5–0.75" is the perfect-sim prediction. On the
  *resolvable* side, a perfect sim with m = 25 sim trials scores A_res = 0.79 and with n = 5, m = 20 scores 0.65 (winner's
  curse plus finite-m sim noise), so the record's "< 0.8 on resolvable pairs means the simulator is broken" is false at
  the sim trial counts the papers use.
- **Evidence.** [argument]; `rt13/theory_sim_out.txt` §1–2: "[SIMPLER thetas, Appendix-B n, sim knows T] unr: A(perfect
  sim)=0.895"; "[narrow, n=25, sim m=25 (SimFoundry)] res: A(perfect sim)=0.790 … unr: 0.628"; "[narrow, n=5, sim m=20
  (2606.10366)] res: A(perfect sim)=0.653".
- **Generator's answer (round 1).** The split was meant as a reporting convention, not an estimator of q_s; the identity is
  correct. **Round 2.** A reporting convention whose two pre-registered outcomes are both the model's own prediction has
  no kill result and no consequence (Gates 1 and 4). **Status: open.**

### T-O2 — P̄ is observed power, biased +0.29 at a true null; κ is not identified (conceded)
- **Objection.** P̄ substitutes the observed gap for the true gap: it is the post-hoc power of the sign test, a function of
  the pair's z-score. Exact enumeration at n = 20, θ_1 = 0.5: at true gap d = 0, P(R = T | no tie) = 0.500 but
  E[P̄] = 0.788 (bias +0.29); at d = 0.05, +0.16; at d = 0.10, +0.05; crossing zero near d = 0.13; −0.05 at d = 0.20–0.30.
  So the plug-in overstates the real sign's reliability exactly at near-ties and understates it at mid gaps. Per pair,
  (A − ½)/(q_r − ½) = 2q_s − 1, so κ is a sim property only with the true q_r per pair; with the plug-in a perfect
  simulator yields κ = 1.13 on SIMPLER's cohort and 0.67 / 0.45 / 0.22 on narrow cohorts at n = 20 / 25 / 5. The Wald SE is
  0 when both rates are 0 or 1 (11 such SIMPLER cells), so Φ(|d|/0) = 1 is assigned by fiat. q_r is identified only from
  repeated real evaluations or a hierarchical model across pairs; the record has neither.
- **Evidence.** [argument]; `theory_sim_out.txt` §3: "true gap d=0.00: P(real sign = true sign | no tie)=0.500 E[plug-in
  Pbar | no tie]=0.788 -> plug-in bias +0.288".
- **Generator's answer.** Conceded: κ and P̄ are struck from any deliverable. **Status: conceded.**

## 7. Checklist lessons (2026-10-20, red team)

- A split-by-resolvability agreement statistic is symmetric in the proxy's and the criterion's reliabilities
  (A = ½ + 2(q_r − ½)(q_s − ½)); before pre-registering a kill on "agreement on unresolvable pairs", compute what a
  perfect proxy scores under the record's own noise model — here it was the kill line itself.
- A plug-in ceiling built from observed gaps is observed power: biased upward at true near-ties (+0.29 at d = 0, n = 20)
  and downward at mid gaps; a chance-corrected agreement that divides by it is not identified without repeats of the
  criterion or a hierarchical model across pairs.
- A reliability estimate from one realization of k ≤ 6 cells has an interval covering negative values a third of the
  time; do not promise an attenuation-corrected correlation at cohort sizes where it overshoots 1 in most draws.
- Pairs share policies: any agreement contrast over policy pairs needs a task-cluster bootstrap or a within-task
  permutation, not a two-proportion interval (SIMPLER's split: [−0.01, +0.44]).
- Before costing a "re-score published tables" probe, open each table: SimFoundry's design is paired on fixed grid
  positions (the two-sample binomial is wrong), PolaRiS and Kaedim grade progress rubrics (binomial resolvability does
  not apply), 2606.10366 publishes rankings, not rates. Zero-cost probes still need the one-file check (2026-10-10).
- When a kill search lists a statistics paper as an incumbent for a neighbouring deliverable (Snyder: ranking), open its
  supplement before claiming the adjacent deliverable (its Table II is the sample-size table).
- Red teams of zero-cost probes should run the probe: the first two tables scored decided this verdict in an hour.
