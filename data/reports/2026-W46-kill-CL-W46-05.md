Glossary (each term once). **GRPO** — group relative policy optimization: several sampled answers to one prompt are scored and each is
pushed toward or away from the group mean. **Checkpoint** — the saved weights at one training step. **Greedy decode** — one deterministic
output per input, the most probable token at every step (temperature $T = 0$). **Solved set $Q_i$** — the validation problems whose greedy
decode at checkpoint $i$ is correct. **Union-versus-best gap $G$** — problems some checkpoint's greedy decode solved and the best
checkpoint's did not, $G = \bigcup_i Q_i \setminus Q_{\mathrm{best}}$. **Cell** — one (checkpoint, problem) pair. **$p_{ix}$** — the
probability that a sampled decode of checkpoint $i$ solves problem $x$ at the RL temperature; $\hat p_{ix}$ its estimate from $m$ draws.
**Coin-flip cell** — $0.2 < p_{\mathrm{best},x} < 0.8$. **Strict drop** — $p_{ix} \geq 0.8$ at some $i$ and $p_{\mathrm{best},x} \leq 0.2$.
**Argmax flip** — the greedy output changes between checkpoints while $p_{ix}$ does not materially move. **Zero-variance group** — a GRPO
rollout group that is all-correct or all-wrong, so it gives no gradient. **Pass@k** — the share of problems solved by at least one of $k$
samples. **Split-generation test** — split one checkpoint's samples into two halves and use the cross-half covariance of per-problem
residuals to measure real per-problem dispersion free of binomial draw noise. **Sharpening** — post-training pushing per-task success
probabilities toward 0 or 1.

## 0. Verdict in one paragraph

**Survived-kill, weak (Gate 2B holds on magnitude only; Gate 6 holds with a race flag).** No fetched source splits a greedy checkpoint
gap into probability drops, coin-flip cells and argmax flips, reports a same-checkpoint draw floor beside a forgetting score, or states
that most of a union-versus-best gap is single-decode noise. The two occupants read the greedy table as lost capability: Temporal
Sampling (2505.20196) chose greedy "To minimize variability arising from different sampling methods during evaluation" (sec. 2.2) and
reads the result as "the model oscillates between correct and incorrect answers across checkpoints" (sec. 2.3, Figure 3); Agentic-OPD
(2611.12799) uses "greedy decoding for all checkpoint evaluations" (sec. 2) and concedes "measuring how much of the historical task
coverage can be reliably recovered" only as a "useful next step" (sec. 5; card, full text). But the direction of the claim is stated,
without a share, by the nearest replicate-aware paper in the same agent setting: Recycling Zero-Variance Queries (2606.10709, GRPO on an
LLM search agent, Qwen3-1.7B/4B) writes that "Evaluating a query with a finite rollout group therefore yields a stochastic observation of
whether the query is zero-variance under the current policy" (sec. 3.3), that "roughly 20% of unique queries have flipped from
zero-variance to signal-bearing" over training (sec. 1), that flips come "from the too-easy tail as it drifts" (sec. 1), and that a
noisier group size produces more flips "consistent with more queries being temporarily assigned to zero-variance regions due to sampling
noise" (sec. 4.4) — on training queries with $K = 4$ or $8$ rollouts, not on a greedy validation table, and with no split of drift from
noise. The identification device the probe needs also exists in print: 2609.22547 (pass@k crossovers) separates real per-prompt
probability change from binomial noise with a split-generation residual covariance whose "within-model base placebo stays near zero"
(sec. 4.3), applied to base-versus-RL pairs on math, with "multi-turn agentic or tool-use settings" left "open" (Limitations). So the
surprise that survives is the magnitude — at least half of $G$ coin flips, at most a fifth strict drops — and the three-way split with a
draw floor; the residue is a claim, not a footnote, but a weak one: the Temporal Sampling authors hold 64 samples on 8 checkpoints and
can compute the split in a day, and the probes as written misclassify settled cells into the coin-flip band at $m = 8$ (sec. 5 below).

## 3. The closest papers' limitation and protocol text (verbatim, grep-verified)

**2606.10709, sec. 3.3.** "Evaluating a query with a finite rollout group therefore yields a stochastic observation of whether the query is
zero-variance under the current policy. Observing a zero-variance group at step $t$ does not imply that the query is intrinsically
uninformative, nor that it will remain so under future policies." ... "a previously all-correct query may re-enter the signal-bearing region
as the policy drifts." **Sec. 1:** "By the time of pool saturation, roughly 20% of unique queries have flipped from zero-variance to
signal-bearing. Queries re-enter the signal-bearing region from the too-hard tail as the policy improves, and from the too-easy tail as it
drifts." **Sec. 4.4:** "Compared to $K=8$, the noisier $K=4$ setting produces approximately 17% more too-hard rescues and 23% more
too-easy rescues, consistent with more queries being temporarily assigned to zero-variance regions due to sampling noise, and later
re-entering the effective set after resampling." **Limitations:** "We study the Qwen3 model family at 1.7B and 4B parameters, and the
recycling dynamics we report are observed at this scale. The balance between too-easy and too-hard rescues may shift at larger scales or
with other model families". Nothing in the paper decodes greedily, builds a validation table, or divides the 20% into noise and drift.

**2609.22547, sec. 4.3.** "Intuitively, if a single curve $q = T(p)$ governed all prompts, the residuals $r_{A,i}$ and $r_{B,i}$ on disjoint
generation halves would reflect only independent binomial sampling noise around that curve and their product would average to zero; a
positive cross-product $S > 0$ directly measures reproducible prompt-specific departure from the curve." ... "every analysis rejects both
single-curve nulls at the bootstrap floor ($p \leq .0025$), whereas the within-model base placebo stays near zero". **Limitations:**
"evaluating large-$k$ crossovers in multi-turn agentic or tool-use settings remains open. Finally, our controlled GRPO and SFT runs use
100–132 updates on a 1.5B base model (17 to 30 times fewer steps than DeepScaleR or ProRL); extending controlled $k = 4096$ evaluations
across longer training horizons and larger base scales is a natural next step."

**2505.20196, sec. 2.2 and App. B.** "To minimize variability arising from different sampling methods during evaluation, we employ greedy
sampling following [48]." (also sec. 2.3: "To minimize variability caused by random fluctuations in model performance from diverse
sampling, we employed greedy sampling".) "Questions exhibits alternating 'Improve' and 'Forget' events frequently during training, which
means the model oscillates between correct and incorrect answers across checkpoints." App. B names only domains ("primarily concentrated
on mathematical reasoning tasks") and training frameworks ("GRPO and SFT frameworks ... we have not empirically validated this
hypothesis"); no sentence concerns the decoding draw.

**2610.01509, sec. 9.** "our analysis remains observational rather than interventional" ... "we did not compare how different types of
post-training sharpen the policy." Its sec. 3: "post-training bimodalizes the per-task success rate distribution to extrema."

## 4. Why has this not been done

Enabling pieces with dates: Agentic-OPD (2026-11-09) made the greedy gap a training target, so the ruler now moves compute; the
split-generation placebo statistic (2609.22547, 2026-09-18) gives a ready estimator of real per-prompt dispersion against binomial
noise; the public Search-R1 GRPO checkpoint (`PeterJinGo/SearchR1-nq_hotpotqa_train-qwen2.5-3b-it-em-grpo`, Hub API 2026-11-16: 3
safetensors shards, config, tokenizer; last modified 2025-03-12) removes training from step 0. Why nobody has run the split: the
owners of the data built methods on the gap (Temporal Sampling's sampler, Agentic-OPD's scheduler), and the replicate-aware papers
(2606.10709, 2609.22547) asked different questions (training efficiency; base-versus-RL crossovers). It is also mildly hard: the gap set is
defined at $T = 0$ and the probability at $T > 0$, so the identity must carry an argmax term, which is why the three-way split rather than
a two-way one. Not "no reason found".

## 5. Killing experiment check

Cheapest probe, C-W46-14 (about 8 GPU-h, \$20, 3 days): the public 3B Search-R1 checkpoint on 500 HotpotQA validation problems, greedy
under three batch compositions ($\delta_0$), eight draws at $T = 1.0$ ($\hat p_x$, coin-flip share $f$, $\rho_1$), and the stationary
reference $\sum_x (1 - \hat p_x)(1 - (1 - \hat p_x)^{n-1})$ for $n = 100$. Kill: $f < 0.12$ with upper bound below 0.17 and $\delta_0 <
1\%$. Then C-W46-13 (about 150 GPU-h): one 300-step run, 30 greedy checkpoints, $m = 8$ draws on 10 checkpoints, the gap split. Both under
one GPU-week; the checkpoint exists (Hub listing fetched). Gate 1 holds.

What survival requires of the design (hints for the design red team):

1. **Write the misclassification matrix before the share.** With $m = 8$ and thresholds 0.2 / 0.8, a settled cell with $p = 0.1$ lands in
   the coin-flip band when $X \geq 2$ successes, probability $1 - 0.9^8 - 8 \cdot 0.1 \cdot 0.9^7 = 0.187$; at $p = 0.15$ it is 0.343. So
   under the Sharpening Tax reading (gap cells near $p \approx 0.1$) about a fifth of true strict drops are labelled coin flips by
   construction, biasing the kill clause "strict drops $\geq 0.5$" downward. Fix: a two-stage rule with $m = 16$ on every gap cell whose
   first eight draws are not unanimous, and shares reported after inverting the binomial misclassification matrix (or as posterior band
   probabilities), with the raw shares beside them.
2. **Use the split-generation placebo as the noise floor.** Split each cell's draws into halves; the cross-half residual covariance over
   problems (2609.22547 eq. 9–10) estimates real checkpoint-by-problem dispersion, and two halves of the same checkpoint are the
   placebo. This replaces the hand-made "subtract $\hat p(1 - \hat p)/m$" correction and is a published statistic.
3. **Cost the free training-side table first.** GRPO already scores $G$ rollouts per training problem per step (Agentic-OPD eq. 10;
   2606.10709 Figure 1 reads exactly this table). Logging it costs nothing and gives the training-set analogue of the three-way split
   before the validation decodes; the claim remains about validation cells, but a training-side split that contradicts it is an early kill.
4. **Pre-register against 2606.10709's number**: about 20% of pool queries flipped status under $K = 8$ groups on a 1.7B search agent,
   with 17–23% more flips at $K = 4$. The claim's draw-facet share must exceed what group-size noise alone implies, or the result restates
   that paper.
5. **Transfer caveat for C-W46-14**: the public checkpoint is trained on NQ plus HotpotQA and is a final checkpoint of a different run than
   Agentic-OPD's HotpotQA-only preliminary; $f$ measured there bounds the claim's gap only through the record's arithmetic ($f \geq 0.31 q$),
   which should be stated as a transfer assumption in the probe.

## 6. Verdict

**Survived-kill, weak.** No occupant: nobody splits a greedy checkpoint gap by a draw floor or states that most of it is single-decode
noise (Gate 6 holds; density about one relevant paper a month; race flag: the Temporal Sampling authors hold the data). Gate 2B holds on
the magnitude and the split only: the direction — finite-sample solved status is stochastic and flips are partly noise — is written in the
same agent setting by 2606.10709, and the same-checkpoint placebo is a published statistic (2609.22547), so the claim adds a number and a
decomposition, not a new mechanism. Gate 4 holds: the if_true action (report $\rho_1$ and $m$; select teachers on $\hat p$) differs from
both seed papers' own fixes (a temporal sampler; a set-cover scheduler). Gate 0 flag carried: the shares are protocol-dependent through
$m$ and the thresholds (hint 1). Position: solo viable (public checkpoint, under one GPU-week); lab is the owners' afternoon.

Probe verdicts: **C-W46-14 survived-kill** (run as written, adding the split-half placebo and an $m = 16$ sensitivity row on the band
edges); **C-W46-13 redesign-owed** (hints 1–4 before the design red team; a probe written before its claim's kill search is a sketch,
rule 17).
