Glossary (each term once). **HAL** — the Holistic Agent Leaderboard (Princeton), whose public trace archive is the data behind seed
paper 2611.21515. **Run** — one executed (model, scaffold, settings) configuration on one benchmark; **cell** — one run on one task.
**Frontier step** — two adjacent runs on a benchmark's accuracy-cost Pareto frontier ordered by cost; 2611.21515 counts 35 steps over
seven benchmarks. **Token quadruple** $(I, R, W, O)$ — per model call: inclusive input tokens, cache-read tokens, cache-write tokens,
output tokens, priced at $p_I, p_R, p_W, p_O$. **Prefix cache** — the provider keeps the processed prefix of a prompt so a later request
repeating it pays the cached price $c$ (a fraction of the input price; 0.1 at Anthropic, 0.5 at OpenAI for the models here) for those
tokens. **Hit share** $h$ — cache-read tokens over input tokens for a run. **Append-only bound** $\bar h$ — the hit share a token-exact
cache would have given a scaffold that only appends to its prompt, $\bar h = \sum_t \min(P_t, P_{t-1}) / \sum_t P_t$ with $P_t$ the
prompt tokens of call $t$. **Uncertainty budget** (GUM, the ISO Guide to the Expression of Uncertainty in Measurement) — a table of every
input that moves a measured value, each with a standard uncertainty, evaluated from repeated observations (Type A) or from an assumed
distribution over what is known (Type B), summed in quadrature, $u_c^2 = \sum_i (\partial f / \partial x_i)^2 u^2(x_i)$, and reported as
$U = k\,u_c$ with coverage factor $k = 2$. **Blunder** — in metrology, a gross error to find and correct, never a budget row. **Compatible**
— two results whose difference is under $k$ times the standard uncertainty of that difference. **litellm** — the translation library
HAL's generalist scaffold calls providers through. **Weave** — the tracing library HAL logs calls with; a traced call is a *span* with
`started_at`, `ended_at`, a `parent_id` and a `summary.usage` block.

## 0. Verdict in one paragraph

**Killed, Gate 0 (premise), with the claim's own `if_false` branch realized at $0 on the archive it names.** The claim's dominant row,
"the serving cache state at execution", was written as a reproducibility interval centred on production hit rates (0.90–0.98, from
TraceLab's 95.6% in Claude Code sessions). On HAL's SWE-bench Verified Mini archives the realized hit share is not a draw: it is 0.000
on the Anthropic run (1,415 calls, `cache_read_input_tokens = 0` throughout; the scaffold never sets a cache breakpoint), 0.414 on the
GPT-5 run and 0.596 on the o3-mini run (OpenAI's automatic caching, fixed by prompt length and the 1,024-token minimum), with the
append-only bound at 0.83–0.86 on every one. The cache state on HAL is a property of the scaffold's request construction, a constant to
declare and re-price (which is exactly probe C-W47-01's measurement), not an interval that survives replication. The accounting-rule
row is not a bias of 4–58% either: every OpenAI-path and Together-path call in the archive is logged twice (a `litellm.completion`
parent span and an `openai.chat.completions.create` child span with the same `chatcmpl` id), HAL's `total_usage` and published
`total_cost` sum both, and the published cost is **2.00× the deduplicated bill on 3 of 3 such archives** (o3-mini $8.09 vs $4.05;
DeepSeek-R1 $4.16 vs $2.08; GPT-5 $45.09 vs $22.54 cache-priced) and **1.00× on the Anthropic archive** ($76.81), so the row is a
provider-path blunder of $\ln 2 = 0.69$ that does not cancel across the frontier and that 2611.21515's audit (median published-to-
reconstructed ratio 1.00) cannot see, because a reconstruction summed from the same spans carries the same factor. Once the blunder is
corrected and the cache condition declared, what remains of the budget is the agent's draw (Type A, 5–9% on a 48-task total by the
import's own numbers, 4–9% of log-cost variance by the killed CL-W43-14 record) plus an automatic-cache residual bounded at about 0.04
at $c = 0.5$ and exactly zero where no breakpoint was set: $u_{c,r} \approx 0.07$–$0.09 < 0.10$, Type A share above 0.6, resolvable
cost ratio at $k = 2$ about $e^{2 \cdot \sqrt{2} \cdot 0.07} \approx 1.22$ against a geometric-mean frontier step of 1.50. Both
pre-registered kill lines fire, and the field's stated knowledge already covers the survivor: the replicate row (CL-W43-14, 2026-11-02)
and "the harness decides how the prompt cache is used, so it picks the rate on the price sheet" (2609.28919). The residue is an audit
finding of real value to HAL and to 2611.21515's authors — a factor-2 cross-provider bias in the SWE-bench Mini cost column — filed as
a complaint, not a claim. All three probes are killed with the claim; probe C-W47-01's step 0 ran here and confirmed its expected
reading on the first Anthropic archive ($h = 0$, $\bar h = 0.86$, re-priced ratio 2.55), which is why it is a correction and not a paper.

## 4. Gates

**Gate 0 — premise hygiene: fails.** Three premise defects, each settled on fetched data.

(a) *The cache-state row is not a draw on this archive.* The import wrote it as a Type B reproducibility interval, hit rate 0.90–0.98,
centred on TraceLab's 95.6% — a production Claude Code number ("a number's verb is part of the premise", checklist 2026-11-08). On the
four decrypted SWE-bench Mini archives the realized hit share is a scaffold constant: the Anthropic run (claude-3-7-sonnet, HAL
Generalist, 1,415 calls, 21.16M prompt tokens) has `cache_read_input_tokens = 0` and `cache_creation_input_tokens = 0` on every call —
no `cache_control` breakpoint was ever sent, so the published $76.81 is the flat-price invoice to the cent ($21{,}156{,}837 \times 3 +
889{,}021 \times 15$ per million). The two OpenAI-path runs have $h = 0.596$ (o3-mini) and $h = 0.414$ (GPT-5) from OpenAI's automatic
caching, whose hits are set by prompt length (1,024-token minimum, 128-token increments) and the scaffold's prompt structure; the
append-only bound is $\bar h = 0.83$–$0.86$ on all three. A quantity that is zero by construction for one provider path and a function
of prompt lengths for another is a protocol constant to declare, not an uncertainty to budget (standards.md Gate 0: "a property of the
thing, not of the protocol").

(b) *The accounting-rule row is a blunder of factor two, not a bias of 4–58%.* Every OpenAI-path and Together-path model call in the
archive appears twice in `raw_logging_results`: a `litellm.completion` span and, inside it (child `parent_id` = parent `id`, same
`trace_id`, same `chatcmpl-...` response id, the child alone carrying `request_id`), an `openai.chat.completions.create` span, both with
the same `summary.usage`. HAL's `total_usage` equals the sum over both: o3-mini run 390 spans for 195 calls, `prompt_tokens` 1,743,150
against 871,575 deduplicated; DeepSeek-R1-via-Together 294 spans for 147 calls, 452,360 against 226,180; GPT-5 1,572 spans for 786
calls, 26,836,914 against 13,418,457. The published `total_cost` is priced on the doubled totals: **$8.09 vs $4.05 deduplicated at HAL's
own flat sheet (o3-mini); $4.16 vs $2.08 (DeepSeek-R1); $45.09 vs $22.54 cache-priced (GPT-5, Aug–Nov 2025, after PRs #109/#113/#114)**.
The Anthropic-path run has one span per call and a ratio of 1.00. So on HAL's SWE-bench Mini column the OpenAI and Together runs are
overstated by exactly 2.00 relative to the Anthropic runs. This is not in 2611.21515's audit: its median ratio of 1.00 and its regression
recovery of "exact input/output rates for 14 model identifiers" are both what a reconstruction summed from the same spans produces
(inference from the method text; the artifact URL is not in the paper). In the GUM's own category this is a blunder — found, corrected,
and removed — and it is the largest term by far: $\ln 2 = 0.69$ against the import's cache row $u = 0.12$ and draw row $0.05$–$0.09$.

(c) *The run date is not one date.* The Claude 3.7 archive's spans run from 2025-08-04 to 2025-11-09 (config `date` 2025-11-09;
`run_id` timestamp 2025-08-04), so "the price sheet in force on the run date" is a per-call quantity the archive holds in `started_at`:
identifiable, re-priceable, not an uncertainty.

The other premise numbers hold as fetched. What fails is the claim that the budget has a dominant Type B row that "no number of replicates
shrinks": on HAL the Type B rows are blunders and declared constants.

**Gate 1 — cheap to kill: passes, and the $0 part ran.** The archive has the fields both probes needed (per-call cache fields and

**Gate 2 — Route B, pre-registered surprise: fails (2B).** The three results were written side by side: expected — the cache row
dominates and at least 12 of 35 steps are compatible on cost; field assumes — cost exact, uncertainty on accuracy; belief-changing — Type

gross-error audit". After deduplication and re-pricing at a declared cache condition, the budget per 48-task run is the draw (relative

residual for OpenAI runs, bounded by $|\partial \ln(\text{input bill})/\partial h| \cdot \delta h = \frac{1-c}{1-(1-c)h}\,\delta h =
\frac{0.5}{0.70} \times 0.05 \approx 0.036$ on the input share at $c = 0.5$, $h = 0.6$, $\delta h = 0.05$, so under 0.03 on the total; and
exactly zero for Anthropic runs that never requested the cache. Then $u_{c,r} \approx 0.07$–$0.09 < 0.10$, the Type A share exceeds 0.6,
