# Kill search (concept level, first pass) — CL-W45-J01 — 2026-11-03

**Headline under test.** Only one or two numbers per scene are worth measuring, and which ones can be computed in
simulation before any robot time: the value of calibrating each physical parameter under a material prior is
concentrated in task-specific parameters, and for most tabletop tasks it is below the cost of measuring, so a
material-prior database plus randomization is the right default and targeted calibration the exception.

**Born from.** Jacob and a colleague, 2026-11-03: collaborators report that per-scene physical-parameter calibration by a
VLM-driven real robot is "heavily adopted lately" in real-to-sim data-synthesis pipelines (Astra-era 3D modelling);
their prior is that a database of material-parameter confidence intervals plus domain randomization suffices.

**Level.** Claim (concept), abstract-level, operator-run in the live session; the full texts of 2606.22062, 2006.01952
and 2302.04903 are owed before probe design (rule 17). Semantic Scholar skipped (no key); GitHub complaint stream
skipped; no conference-export index.

## 0. Verdict in one paragraph

**Survived at concept level, with Gate 2B flagged as the red team's first objection.** The *concept* "identify the
parameters the task is sensitive to, not all of them" is in print since RSS 2020 (Liang–Saxena–Kroemer 2006.01952:
task-oriented exploration, relevance implicit in a regret objective) and in the task-driven adaptation line of
Princeton IRoM (AdaptSim 2302.04903: adapt the parameter *distribution* to task performance, "matching dynamics ... may
not lead to policies that perform well"). The *budget* question (measure vs randomize) was asked in June 2026 on a
two-parameter pendulum (2606.22062) and answered "measure what you can", leaving the per-parameter question open in its
own words. **Nobody in the fetched set computes or publishes the per-parameter value of measuring under a material prior,
the concentration of that value across parameters, or a decision rule with the measurement cost on the other side** —
the arXiv abstract conjunction (Sobol OR variance-based sensitivity OR value of information OR EVPI) AND (sim-to-real OR
domain randomization OR system identification) AND robot returns **0** (2026-11-03). The agentic pipelines that spend
robot time on calibration choose *what* to calibrate by a VLM's semantics (2601.08454: "the minimal subset of missing
physical parameters required for the simulation task") with no check that the choice is right. The surprise is
two-sided and pre-registerable: the collaborators' prior (prior + DR suffices) for most tabletop tasks, and the
Phys2Real/ForceTwin class (CoM, mechanisms, static friction in joints) where one parameter carries the gap.

## 3. Limitation text in the neighbours' own words

- 2606.22062: "sim-to-real pipelines should first measure the parameters they can and reserve randomization for the
  uncertainty that remains" and "structural model mismatch remains the setting where randomization breadth may become
  more valuable" — the per-parameter and the structural-mismatch questions, both left open.
- 2610.19479: existing approaches rely on "uniform generation, offline retrieval, or manual parameter alignment by domain
  experts" — the alignment step is named as the manual cost.
- 2302.04903: "attempting to match the dynamics between simulation and reality across all states and tasks may be
  infeasible and may not lead to policies that perform well in reality for a specific task".
- NVIDIA developer blog, "5 Steps to Create SimReady Assets with Frontier AI Models" (fetched 2026-11-03): friction
  "assumed static friction of 0.8, dynamic friction of 0.6"; "these parameters were not calibrated against physical
  YuMi measurements"; "did not establish agreement with real-world robot dynamics".
- Moonlake, "Evaluating 3D simulation-ready assets" (vendor blog, fetched): the 22 asset metrics are "only a proxy for
  whether an asset improves robot learning"; correlation with training outcomes is future work.

## 4. Why has this not been done

Enabling pieces landed this year: material priors now exist as artefacts (SimReady Central with authored physics,
PhysX-Mobility's physical annotations, VLM-inferred parameter distributions in Phys2Real and DEXTERA), so "prior plus
randomization" is a concrete arm rather than a hand-set range; parameter-conditioned policies (RMA, Phys2Real) make the
value of knowing a parameter a *conditioning* experiment instead of a retraining per parameter; and the agentic
real-to-sim wave (75 papers in 13 weeks) made the choice of what to calibrate a live cost. The question sits between
communities: system-identification people optimise identifiability, randomization people widen ranges, and the
pipeline builders let a VLM pick. Not a "no reason" case.

## 7. Killing experiment (checked against the definition)

Quantity defined first. Scene parameters $\theta = (\theta_1, \dots, \theta_d)$ with material prior $p(\theta)$; a policy
$\pi$; success $J(\pi, \theta)$ when deployed in a world with parameters $\theta$. The value of measuring parameter $k$
exactly is the expected value of perfect information,

$$\mathrm{EVPI}_k = \mathbb{E}_{\theta_k}\Big[\max_\pi \mathbb{E}_{\theta_{-k} \mid \theta_k} J(\pi, \theta)\Big] - \max_\pi \mathbb{E}_{\theta} J(\pi, \theta),$$

where $\theta_{-k}$ is every parameter but $k$. Calibrate $k$ only if $\mathrm{EVPI}_k$ exceeds its measurement cost
$c_k$ in robot time. The cheap proxy is the Sobol index of the randomized policy's success,

$$S_k = \frac{\mathrm{Var}_{\theta_k}\big(\mathbb{E}[J(\pi_{\mathrm{DR}}, \theta) \mid \theta_k]\big)}{\mathrm{Var}_{\theta}\, J(\pi_{\mathrm{DR}}, \theta)},$$

with $\pi_{\mathrm{DR}}$ the policy trained under the prior; a parameter with $S_k \approx 0$ cannot be worth measuring.

Estimator: one policy per task conditioned on a masked parameter vector, $\pi_u(a \mid s, \hat\theta)$, trained under the
prior with random masks (each masked entry replaced by its prior mean $\bar\theta_j$), so every conditioning pattern
is in-distribution. Then $\widehat{\mathrm{EVPI}}_k = J_k - J_0$ with $J_k$ the success when only $\theta_k$ is revealed
and $J_0$ when nothing is; $J_{\mathrm{all}}$ when everything is. Concentration $= \max_k \widehat{\mathrm{EVPI}}_k /
(J_{\mathrm{all}} - J_0)$. Second arm (budget-matched): at identification budgets $B \in \{0, 2, 5, 10, 20\}$ rollouts
on the hidden twin, compare prior + DR, full calibration of all $d$, and targeted calibration of the top-value
parameters only.

Kill criteria (pre-registered): dead if the top parameter carries under 35% of total value in most tasks (no
concentration, nothing to target); dead if targeted calibration beats neither arm at any budget on any task (the rule
has no content); dead as a headline if a per-parameter value rule is found in print at full-text reading of
2606.22062, 2006.01952, 2302.04903 and the Phys2Real related work. Expected (judgment): total value under 5 success
points on pick-and-place and peg insertion, 15–40 points on CoM-offset pushing and spring-loaded drawers, with the top
parameter above 60% of the total in at least five of six tasks. Cost: 60–100 GPU-h, $100–300, 3–4 weeks; under one
GPU-week. Degraded: s2 skipped; full texts owed.
