Glossary (each term once): **prefill** — the parallel pass over a request's prompt tokens, compute-bound; **decode** — generating
output tokens one step at a time, memory-bound; **PD disaggregation** — prefill and decode on separate GPU pools, with the KV cache
(the stored attention state) shipped between them; **role switching** — flipping a running engine between the prefill role and the
decode role without reloading weights; **P/D ratio** — the count of prefill instances to decode instances; **rollout** — the
generation stage of RL training; **staleness** — how many policy versions behind a trajectory may run in asynchronous RL;
**backlog-threshold rule** — switch a role when an observed queue or occupancy crosses a level; **hysteresis** — two thresholds, one
to engage and a lower one to release, plus a cool-down, so noise near the level does not cause churn; **demand forecaster** — a rule
that predicts the next horizon's prefill tokens and decode admissions and sizes the roles to the prediction; **hindsight oracle** —
the switching schedule a dynamic program picks with the whole arrival-and-length record known, the best any policy could have done;
**headroom** $H = T_{\mathrm{static}}/T_{\mathrm{oracle}} - 1$, with $T_{\mathrm{static}}$ the rollout time under the best static
P/D split and $T_{\mathrm{oracle}}$ the time under the oracle schedule; **capture ratio** $\rho_{\mathrm{arm}} = (T_{\mathrm{static}}
- T_{\mathrm{arm}})/(T_{\mathrm{static}} - T_{\mathrm{oracle}})$, the share of the oracle's gain a policy realises; **WAPE** —
weighted absolute percentage error, the sum of absolute forecast errors over the sum of measured values; **SLO** — a latency target
(time to first token, time per output token); **goodput** — requests served inside their SLOs per unit time.

## 0. Verdict in one paragraph

**Survived-kill (Gate 6 holds on the measurement; Gate 2B holds against the RL-side occupant), proper grade, with two design
flags that make C-W43-04 a redesign-owed sketch.** No fetched paper compares a threshold rule against a hindsight-optimal switching
schedule on recorded agent traces, and none reports a capture ratio: Prorsa (2610.16592, 2026-10-12) owns the lifecycle forecaster
and compares it only to a static split chosen with whole-task hindsight ("The static baseline uses the optimal prefill-to-decode
instance ratios of 4:4, 5:3, and 3:5 for the math, coding agent, and general agent tasks, respectively", §5.4) — its text has zero
occurrences of "oracle" or "hindsight"; FluidPD (2610.06917, 2026-10-02) ships the observation-only two-threshold rule with
hysteresis and cool-down in serving (FluidRole, §IV-C2) and its "oracle" is the best static ratio per window ("We define the oracle
P:D ratio for each window as the static ratio with the highest overall SLO attainment", §III-A), not a switching schedule; TokenScale
(2512.03416, 2025-12-03) compares provisioned to hindsight-required instance counts by correlation only, on single-turn serving
("This work focuses on single-turn workloads", §4.6); PEARL (2609.35158) switches per rollout step from a fitted makespan model with
hysteresis thresholds and no oracle. No RL rollout engine ships a demand-driven role switch other than Prorsa's forecaster: SGLang's
Q4 2026 roadmap (issue 43699) lists "Runtime prefill↔decode role switching" as done (PR 28403) and names the policy as open — "a
router-driven policy that flips roles on load instead of an operator call" — which is the claim's consumer asking for its answer in
print. The surprise is not the RL occupant's thesis: Prorsa writes "This level of accuracy is sufficient to guide role switching"
(§5.5) and makes the forecaster its contribution. Two flags. (i) **Tautology risk in the contrast.** Prorsa's forecast demand $W_P$
"includes all queued and in-flight prefill requests at $t$, and new requests predicted to arrive within the horizon" (§4.2): the
forecaster is a backlog rule plus a ten-second arrival increment, so the two arms differ only by that increment and the admission
forecast $\hat K_D$; the probe must isolate the increment (Prorsa's rule with the arrival forecast zeroed) or the equality is near
construction. (ii) **The mechanism's sign is contested by the occupant.** The claim says headroom shrinks with concurrency because
many renewal processes superpose into a smooth demand ratio; Prorsa attributes its 1.0–1.44x to decode KV-cache capacity that
tightens with concurrency and asynchrony ("Longer trajectory lifetimes increase request concurrency, and asynchronous rollout further
amplifies this effect through sample over-dispatch, placing greater pressure on decode KV-cache capacity and limiting throughput",
§5.4) — a mean drift the law of large numbers does not remove. What survives is a claim, not a footnote: the first oracle-normalised
capture ratio for role switching, with $H$ reported against concurrency and staleness separately in the KV-bound and compute-bound
regimes. The orphan C-W45-11 (session admission) was not searched here and stays an orphan.

## 4. Why has this not been done

An enabling piece landed and is dated twice over. The mechanism is in the engine: SGLang's runtime role switch shipped as an operator
call (PR 28403, marked done in the Q2 and Q4 2026 roadmaps), and the maintainers list the load-driven policy as unbuilt. The forecaster
is in print with its error but without its cost (Prorsa, 2026-10-12), and the observation rule is in print in serving without a
forecaster arm (FluidPD, 2026-10-02). The oracle is nobody's because each group's incentive was to ship a controller, and the import
field's result (ABCS 2021: a non-predictive policy is constant-competitive, predictions buy $1+\varepsilon$) has not been instantiated
for the LLM role switch. Replay inputs are public (SWE-agent `.traj` with `execution_time`; Frontier with PDD since 2026-08). "No
reason found" does not apply; the reason is that the question belongs to no one of the three groups that could ask it.

## 5. Killing experiment check

Restated: trace-driven replay of agentic rollout at 256–4096 concurrent trajectories, synchronous and asynchronous (staleness 2 and
8), four arms on one trace — best static split, Prorsa's rule, a FluidRole-style two-threshold hysteresis rule on prefill backlog and
decode KV occupancy, and the hindsight oracle (a dynamic program over prefill-instance count per 10-s slot with a 5-s switch cost) —
reporting $H$ and $\rho_{\mathrm{arm}}$ per workload x concurrency x staleness. Cost as filed: 16 GPU-hours calibration (or Frontier's
constants), about \$60, 7 days. **Under one GPU-week: confirmed.** Corrections from this search, owed before any red team (rule 17):
(a) Frontier's README lists PDD and parallelism reconfiguration for RL rollouts, not P/D role flips with a switch cost; budget the flip
and the cancel-and-resend cost (Prorsa App. C) into the simulator or the 300-line fallback — add three days. (b) Split the forecaster arm
in two: Prorsa's rule as written, and Prorsa's rule with the predicted-arrival term and $\hat K_D$ set to zero (a backlog rule with
Prorsa's sizing); only the gap between these two is the value of forecasting, and the FluidRole-style rule is a third, separately
tuned arm whose thresholds are swept, not fixed. (c) Report $H$ in two regimes by construction — KV-bound (coding, trajectories near
Prorsa's 846-s lifetime, $n_D$ set by $C_{KV}$) and compute-bound (general agent, short lifetimes) — because the occupant's stated
lever is capacity drift, which grows with concurrency; the pre-registered expectation "$H$ falls as $1/\sqrt{\text{concurrency}}$"
applies to the demand-ratio variance only and must be stated as such. (d) The kills_if stands: at $\ge 1024$ asynchronous
trajectories in two of three workloads, $H \ge 15\%$ with the forecaster capturing $\ge 0.8H$ and the hysteresis rule $\le 0.5H$ —
dead. Pre-registered non-kill: $H < 5\%$ everywhere including synchronous — switching is a dead lever at this scale, and the digest
says so. Add a second pre-registered reading: if $H$ rises with concurrency in the KV-bound regime, the claim's mechanism is wrong
and Prorsa's is right, which is a result, not a kill of the measurement.
