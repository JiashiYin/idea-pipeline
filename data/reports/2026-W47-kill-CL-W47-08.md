**Terms, one gloss each.** *Success checker $c$*: the hand-written function a simulated benchmark evaluates on the simulator state
to record an episode as a success; RoboTwin 2.0 ends the episode at the first state on which it returns 1. *Verified checker $c'$*:
the seed paper's repaired checker for the same task (physical state in place of commanded values; only conditions the instruction
states; conditions must hold one second). *Phantom success*: an episode $c$ accepts and $c'$ rejects. *Phantom share*
$\mathrm{Phan}(\pi) = |\{e : c_e = 1, c'_e = 0\}| / |\{e : c_e = 1\}|$ over a policy's episodes. *Imitation initialisation $\pi_0$*:
the supervised-fine-tuned (SFT) checkpoint before any RL. *Checker-trained policy $\pi_c$*: a policy whose RL reward was the verdict
of $c$. *Phantom share of the gain*
$$\phi = 1 - \frac{\mathrm{SR}_{c'}(\pi_c) - \mathrm{SR}_{c'}(\pi_0)}{\mathrm{SR}_c(\pi_c) - \mathrm{SR}_c(\pi_0)},$$
with $\mathrm{SR}_k(\pi)$ the success rate of $\pi$ under checker $k$ on paired seeds: the fraction of the gain a checker-trained
policy reports under $c$ that disappears under $c'$. *2x2 interaction $X$*: with $\pi_{c'}$ trained against $c'$ from the same
$\pi_0$, $X = [\mathrm{SR}_{c'}(\pi_{c'}) - \mathrm{SR}_{c'}(\pi_c)] - [\mathrm{SR}_c(\pi_{c'}) - \mathrm{SR}_c(\pi_c)]$, what the
Verified checker takes from the checker-trained policy beyond what it takes from any policy. *GRPO*: group relative policy
optimisation, an RL update that scores several rollouts of one scene against their group mean. *RFT*: rejection-sampling
fine-tuning, behaviour cloning on the rollouts the checker accepted. *Criteria Exposure (CE)*: 2611.08318's name for letting a
self-improving agent read the benchmark's success-criteria code. *Shove-cheat*: Prism-GRPO's name for a RoboTwin rollout that pushes
the pot toward the can instead of lifting the can, and is marked a success. *Pushcut*: SimpleVLA-RL's name for the same class of
behaviour (pushing in place of grasp-move-place), presented there as emergence. *NRFR*: 2607.09492's "Newly Rewarded Failure Rate",
the failure rate among samples whose proxy reward improved over the SFT baseline.

## 5. Killing-experiment check, the $0 arithmetic, and what a design would need

**Cost.** Step 2a as written: about 20 GPU-h, $40, three days on the released click-bell and move-can-pot pairs, once $c'$ is public
(or with a hand-written contact-and-persistence condition for click bell). Under one GPU-week. Step 2b (RFT arms, 60–90 GPU-h) is
not needed: the pairs exist.

**The $0 bound from published numbers (the W46 lesson).** Write $g = \mathrm{SR}_c(\pi_c) - \mathrm{SR}_c(\pi_0)$ for the gain under the
released checker and $h = \mathrm{Phan}(\pi_c)$ for the checker-trained policy's phantom share. If $\mathrm{Phan}(\pi_0) = 0$ (the SFT
policy imitates demonstrations that all pass $c'$; the seed's 1,233 expert episodes do), then the phantom successes of $\pi_c$ number
$h \cdot \mathrm{SR}_c(\pi_c)$ and
$$\phi = \frac{h\,\mathrm{SR}_c(\pi_c)}{g}.$$
On Move Can Pot, SimpleVLA-RL Table 4 gives $\mathrm{SR}_c(\pi_0) = 28.1$, $\mathrm{SR}_c(\pi_c) = 61.2$, $g = 33.1$; Prism-GRPO's
binary-GRPO shove share from the same released SFT checkpoint is $h \in [0.007, 0.070]$ over five seeds, so
$$\phi \in \Big[\frac{0.007 \times 61.2}{33.1},\ \frac{0.070 \times 61.2}{33.1}\Big] = [0.013,\ 0.129].$$
The claim's line ($\phi \ge 0.25$) is not reached at any seed; the probe's kill line ($\phi < 0.10$) is crossed at every seed but the
worst. For RL-ZVP ($h$ up to 0.200) the top of the range is 0.37 — the claim's quantity is reachable by a confidence-weighted
variant on one task, which is Prism-GRPO's own finding and not a released checkpoint. Two caveats, both against the claim: the shove
is a phantom under the instruction ("requires the arm to lift the can") but is **not** one of SimAudit's 61 defects, so $c'$ as
described would score it a success and the probe's $\phi$ on Move Can Pot would read near zero; and on click bell, the one
phantom-direction defect task with a released pair, the checker-trained policy's real-robot success rose 30% → 60% (SimpleVLA-RL
Table 6), which a policy that stops 2 cm above the bell cannot produce, so the sim gain on that task is largely not phantom.

**What a redesign would have to add to be more than a row** (recorded for the lane, not to revive the claim): (1) a second scorer
that catches the exploits RL actually finds — Prism-GRPO's displacement rule and SimAudit's $c'$ are disjoint, so $c'$ would first be
audited with RL rollouts (SimAudit's pipeline run on checker-trained policies: their appendix, not ours); (2) the NRFR decomposition
(new failures versus inherited) in place of $\phi$; (3) the checker commit pinned to the training run; (4) pre-registration against
the two in-print values (2 of 89; 0.7–7.0%), so the result is a third point on a curve, which is what it would be.

## 6. Why has this not been done, and why now

The two-oracle score is the code-RL community's standard practice (held-out tests: SpecBench, KernelBench-Verified, EvilGenie) and
reached robot benchmarks from both sides this autumn: the RL side measured the hack on RoboTwin because it changed a method's ranking
(Prism-GRPO: RL-ZVP's raw success beat Prism's until calibrated); the agent side ran the 2x2 because exposure to criteria code is how
self-improving agents are built (2611.08318). SimAudit arrived third, with the checkers but no training arm. So "why not done" is: it
is done in the parts that mattered to the people with the checkpoints, and the remaining conjunction (SimAudit's $c'$ × SimpleVLA-RL's
released RL pairs) waits on a release with no URL yet. The complaint stream agrees: RoboTwin's tracker holds the checker complaints —
