**Glossary (each term once).** *Score*: Genie Sim's task metric, the mean over episodes and scored stages of the stage credit, in
$[0, 1]$. *Real-to-sim discrepancy*: the README's "less than 10%" and the paper's $\mathrm{MAE} = 0.0405$ between simulated and real
Scores over 22 (robot, task) pairs. *Physics step* $h$: the interval at which the engine integrates the rigid-body equations;
`physics_step` in the release is its reciprocal in Hz. *Solver iterations*: passes of PhysX's contact solver per step (position /
velocity). *Contact offset*: the distance at which PhysX begins generating contacts before surfaces touch. *Repeat floor*: the Score
difference between two runs at identical settings and layouts; the smallest difference that means anything. *One-at-a-time*: change
one setting from the shipped value, hold the rest. *Validity table*: a published table of simulated against real scores with a
correlation or error summary. *Engine default*: the value PhysX applies when a USD sets none.

## 0. Verdict in one paragraph

**Survived-kill, weak; Gate 0 fails as worded and the headline is rewritten before any probe design; Gate 2B holds narrowly; both
probes are redesign-owed or parked.** The exact shape — a real-validated evaluation benchmark re-scored under its own physics step
and contact offset, the shift read against the published validity number — is in no paper: nine queries in the words of the
sim-to-sim, V&V and benchmark communities return zero on it, and the concurrent-twin query on the seed paper's own phrase returns
seven validity papers (REALM, PolaRiS, VISER, X2Real family) none of which varies a numerics setting. Two premises are false in the
release's own text. (i) "Unpublished": the shipped benchmark configuration schema sets `physics_step: int = 120` (so $h = 8.33$ ms),
`rendering_step: int = 60`, `enable_gpu_dynamics: bool = False` (CPU PhysX) and documents the override `--app.physics_step 240`
(`params.py`); the paper's task layer ("Simulation evaluation uses a fixed Genie Sim experimental branch", sec. II-C) and the README
name none of this, so the honest sentence is "stated nowhere a reader of the validity claim looks, shipped as a default of 120 Hz".
Solver iterations and contact offsets are set in neither the robot USD (`G2_omnipicker/robot.usda`: `PhysxRigidBodyAPI` on 56 bodies,
`enabledSelfCollisions = 0`, no `solverPositionIterationCount`, no `contactOffset`) nor the task config, so engine defaults apply —
that half of "unpublished" stands. (ii) "No validity table reports the numerics": the smallest validity paper of the autumn does.
Kaedim's 2610.00731 (2026-09-30) Table 2 lists "physics PhysX 240 Hz; continuous collision detection on; contact offset 6 mm; 64
position and 16 velocity solver iterations" as settings held constant, and its configuration gate rejects any run whose "solver step
count" drifted. The register the claim's surprise lives in — "one simulator setting moves a shipped benchmark's success by about ten
points" — is occupied three times with other knobs: SimuGuard's collision constant (89% to 98% on RoboTwin), 2609.37771's object
masses (a 26-point swing of a baseline against an accelerated variant on RoboTwin), Kaedim's authored-versus-default physics and
assets (mean score error 17.54 to 6.97 points against real). The numerics knob is the one nobody has turned on an evaluation
benchmark, and the two data on it point in opposite directions — 2610.32277's time step decisive for sim-trained trackers; Manda
Robotics' four-engine study, "identical success scores hide centimetre-scale tracking differences" and "force peaks are not
timestep-converged" while "mean hold COM lift changes from 68.6 to 66.5 mm" — so the expected result is not the field's statement
either way. What survives is a claim one level narrower than filed: on the released benchmark at its shipped 120 Hz CPU-PhysX default,
refining the step (240 Hz, 1 kHz) or doubling the contact offset moves at least 3 of 8 contact-rich Scores by more than the claimed
10 points, with solver iterations as the flat control. Weak because a positive result is the fourth "a setting moves a board by ten
points" datum and the engine owners' and the four-engine study's prior is that coarse outcomes are step-robust; the solo position
survives on cost (50–130 GPU-h on a public release with baseline weights); the lab position (AgiBot, the 22 policies and the real
column) is the one that prints the row on the board.

## 3. The two closest papers' limitation text (verbatim, grep-verified)

**2610.00731, sec. 6 Discussion:** "there are five limitations to this study. First, the authored and default reconstructions differ
in object geometry, texture, metric scale, collision geometry, mass, friction, restitution and scene at once, so the current design
attributes the reduction to the pipeline as a whole and cannot say how much comes from the objects, how much from the scene, or which
object property matters most. As an extension of this work, we would change one factor at a time from the default reconstruction,
geometry, scale, physics or scene, and measure the change in score error after each." ... "Third, simulated and real trials are graded
by different graders, which affects both reconstructions equally." ... "Fourth, at five tasks by two policies the study is small for
this area". **Sec. 4, Table 2** (settings held constant): "physics PhysX 240 Hz; continuous collision detection on; contact offset 6
mm; 64 position and 16 velocity solver iterations"; "Nine properties must match: step budget, action chunk length, top camera height,
placement version and its per-policy key, physical rig, gripper collision filter, joint clipping and solver step count."

**2609.37771, abstract and sec. 6:** "design limitations, where success criteria and simulation settings do not fully capture how
acceleration affects task execution" ... "Simulation can be idealized: unrealistic object masses let the same actions move an object
differently in simulation and reality, and action smoothing that differs from real robots hides the jitter that acceleration adds to
policy outputs." ... "Control-frequency limits and differences in low-level motor-control implementations may explain the discrepancy
between simulation and real execution." No sentence names the physics step or the solver.

**Third, Manda Robotics (2026-10-05), scope section:** "Unless stated otherwise, each configuration is one matched trial. Repeats and
half-timestep runs are sensitivity checks, not confidence intervals, success-rate estimates or convergence studies." ... "Native-drive
portability, learned-policy transfer and real-world accuracy require separate tests with matched hardware measurements. We take up
learned-policy transfer and real-world accuracy in the next two posts of this series." **Fourth, 2611.15020 sec. II-C:** "Simulation
evaluation uses a fixed Genie Sim experimental branch." **App. D.3:** "Native integration uses CPU TGS, 32/1 solver iterations and a 1
ms step".

## 4. Why has this not been done, and why now

Enabling pieces, dated: Genie Sim v3.5.0 (2026-11-02) is the first real-validated benchmark whose physics step is a CLI override
(`--app.physics_step`) on a public image with baseline weights; the validity number arrived nine days later (2611.15020, 2026-11-11);
the one-at-a-time method is in print for RL trackers (2610.32277, 2026-10-23) and the one-parameter rerun tooling for benchmarks
(SimuGuard, 2026-10-30). Why unrun: the validity community calibrates assets, visuals and controllers and either leaves the numerics at
engine defaults (PolaRiS) or prints and freezes them (Kaedim); the engine-comparison community measures numerics on scripted scenes and
reads "coarse outcomes agree"; the sim-to-sim community measured the time step on sim-trained trackers and has no reason to run
real-trained VLAs. It is also hard in one honest way: the paper's 22 policies are unreleased, so the sweep reads a rebuilt or baseline
policy, not the published table. "No reason found" does not apply; the race is against Kaedim's announced one-at-a-time extension
(physical parameters) and Manda's announced policy-transfer posts (engines), neither of which names the step or the offset.

## 5. Killing experiment check

C-W46-18 as filed: one-at-a-time sweep of $h$, position iterations, contact offset and GPU/CPU on 8 contact-rich released tasks, 100
fixed-layout episodes per cell, repeat floor measured; kill if fewer than 2 of 8 tasks move by more than $\max(f_t, 1.96\,\mathrm{SE},
0.04)$ under every knob and none by more than 0.10. Cost 50–130 GPU-h, under one GPU-week: passes Gate 1. This search did most of step
0, and it changes the design: (a) $\theta_0$ is 120 Hz ($h = 8.33$ ms), CPU PhysX, engine-default iterations and offsets — the shipped
value is the coarsest point of the filed sweep set $\{1, 4, 8.3\}$ ms, so the sweep is a refinement (240 Hz, 500 Hz, 1 kHz) and the
question is convergence of the shipped Score, the lane's verification leg proper; (b) the control cadence must be held at its shipped
period in seconds while `physics_step` changes, or the decimation moves with the step and the arm confounds control with physics —
verify how the benchmark derives the policy-step period before the first run; (c) `pause_physics_during_inference = False` (objects
drift under gravity during the inference round-trip) and `enable_rate_limit` are protocol knobs larger than any numerics knob by
construction and are held fixed and reported; (d) the headline's "unpublished" becomes "shipped as a default the paper and README do
not name" and the current_belief sentence "no table reports them" is replaced by Kaedim Table 2; (e) the if_true consequence is the
sensitivity row, since the setting line is already Kaedim's practice. Survival of the claim requires that the per-task paired shift
beats a measured floor on the shipped CPU path (Manda: PhysX CPU repeat-identical on three repeats; GPU arm not), so the floor table
is cheap and the kill line is well-defined. C-W46-20 (engine swap): the fetched docs assign one runtime per task config ("direct Isaac
evaluation, managed Newton wok evaluation, and native MuJoCo Rubik execution"; `runtime.py` reads `runtime` or `benchmark.backend`
from the task YAML) and the deformable tasks "reuse one physics, CPU MuJoCo and synchronized render session" of their own — fewer than
three released tasks run on two runtimes without new assets, which is the probe's own step-0 parking trigger.
