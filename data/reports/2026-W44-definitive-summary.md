### Does a definitive comparison exist? No. The two closest and what each lacks

- **2610.31778** Benchmarks Are Surprisingly Compaction-Friendly — has: One scaffold (OpenCode), five compaction strategies implemented as hooks, four frontier models, three long-horizon benchmarks (PaperBench 3 papers, ProgramBench 2 tasks, PostTrainBench 2 models x 3 targets), thresholds 160K-380K, two runs per task-configuration, bootstrap CIs. — lacks: No published recipe (its five arms are self-summary, tool-output pruning, filesystem offload, reasoning-block discard, small-model summary); no matched dose (thresholds 'are not comparable across models because compaction frequency depends on how fast each model's context grows', its own words); no matched compute (accuracy conditional on a valid submission, no prefill or wall-clock in the result); no random-deletion or placebo control; closed models only, two runs per cell, three to six tasks per benchmark; its own limitation text: 'four models, three benchmarks and a few tasks per each benchmark due to cost and compute constraints. We fix one scaffold'.
- **2609.32961** Beyond Token Savings: A Systematic Study of Context Compression in LLM Agents — has: Twelve harness primitives decomposed into primitive x trigger x depth, mini-swe-agent, three open-weight models, 100 SWE-bench Verified issues (three repos) and 40 Terminal-Bench 1.0 tasks, three runs per cell, about 35,000 runs, measured latency and estimated billed cost, task-level gains and losses. — lacks: No published recipe (no ACON, SJC, FOCUS, ReFold, CADOC, StateComp, AutoCompact); not a forced regime (thresholds at the 5th to 25th percentile of uncompacted peak context, so most trajectories compact fewer than twice on SWE-bench); dose differs by policy (step-triggered arms compact after every step); billed cost assumed at a 10:1 cached ratio rather than measured; no random-deletion or placebo control; no pass-rate bands; models no larger than 35B-A3B; its related-work section positions it against serving characterizations, not against the recipe literature.
- **2610.31710** Compact-Bench (third-closest) — has: Paired full-history versus summary-only continuations from one repository state across 14 summarizer backbones and 5 production prompts; recurring-detail retention on 146 real sessions; 224 production boundaries. — lacks: Only the summarizer backbone and prompt vary (no deletion, stub, learned-trigger or every-turn arms); 35 small repairs with an eight-step continuation; a measurement of one compaction event, not of a run under a forced window; no cost axis.

### Spec (every number next to its assumption)

- **Harness.** One open harness with a pluggable compaction hook: the OpenCode fork released with 2610.31778 (its five strategies are already hooks) or mini-swe-agent as in 2609.32961; every recipe is implemented as a replacement for the hook and nothing else changes (tools, prompts, retry logic). Models served locally with vLLM, prefix caching on, so uncached prefill is measured, not estimated.
- **Regime / forced_window.** A hard context cap $W = 64\,\text{K}$ tokens for every arm (assumption: small enough that the tasks below compact at least twice; 2609.32961 saw fewer than 2 compactions per SWE-bench run at calibrated thresholds, which is why its comparison is not forced).
- **Regime / task_selection.** Only tasks whose uncompacted trajectory reaches peak context $\geq 2.5\,W$ in a pilot at a 1M window (assumption: about 40% of SWE-bench Verified and 60% of Terminal-Bench 2.0 tasks qualify for a 35B model; the pilot sets the number).
- **Regime / benchmarks.** SWE-bench Verified long-trajectory subset (target 150 tasks), Terminal-Bench 2.0 (target 60 tasks), and one non-compaction-friendly set where the constraint is not on disk: the CONPACT long-horizon research scenario (2608.11242) with injected side constraints (target 100 sessions). 2610.31778's own text: current benchmarks are compaction-friendly because progress stays on disk; a definitive table needs one set where it does not.
- **Regime / model_band.** Open weights 27B to 35B active-or-total (Qwen3.5-35B-A3B and Qwen3.8-27B, both used by the closest papers), with one frontier API model on a 30-task subset as a transfer check (not compute-matched; reported separately).
- **Matched compute.** Per task a fixed budget of uncached prefill tokens $P$ and model calls $C$ (assumption: $P = 3\,\text{M}$, $C = 80$; set from the full-context pilot's 75th percentile). A run that exhausts either is scored as failed. Wall-clock and GPU-seconds are logged per run on the same engine so cost is measured, not derived from a price list (2609.32961 assumed a 10:1 cached price ratio; this spec measures prefill).
- **Matched dose.** Every arm compacts at the same boundaries (the forced window cap) to the same retained budget $r\,W$ with $r \in \{0.15, 0.25, 0.40\}$ (assumption: 0.25 is the common scaffold default; the other two bracket it). Dose is the retained-token fraction $r$ and the number of boundaries $n_b$. Learned triggers (AutoCompact, StateComp) and every-turn recipes (CCM) get a second arm with a free trigger but the same total retained tokens over the run, so a gain that comes from compacting more often or less deeply is separated from a gain in what is kept.
- **Controls.** (1) Random deletion at the same $r$ (2610.27301's control), sentence-level and word-level. (2) Placebo summary: a summary of the same length written for a different task from the same benchmark, inserted at the same boundary (the wrong-task placebo for any claim that content matters). (3) Shuffled summary: the arm's own summary with its entries permuted (tests order and provenance claims such as SJC's). (4) No-compaction reference at a 1M window on the same tasks (the ceiling that defines damage). (5) Pass-rate bands: tasks binned by the no-compaction reference's pass rate over 3 seeds into $[0, 0.2)$, $[0.2, 0.8)$, $[0.8, 1]$; every number reported by band. (6) Entropy-only version: keep the $r\,W$ tokens with the highest next-token entropy under the policy (the content-free selector that several recipes may collapse to).
- **Metrics.** Damage fraction $D = 1 - s_{\text{arm}} / s_{\text{ref}}$ where $s$ is the pass rate over 3 seeds and the reference is the 1M-window run (paired by task). Paired damage and rescue from the same pre-boundary state (Compact-Bench protocol): continue once from the full history and once from the compacted context. Side-constraint retention and compliance (CONPACT protocol) on the research-scenario set. Uncached prefill tokens, calls, GPU-seconds per run; prefix-cache hit rate.
- **The single plot.** Damage $D$ against dose $r$ at the forced window, one curve per recipe with its 95% task-bootstrap band, the random-deletion curve drawn as the floor and the TRC curve as the cheap incumbent, one panel per benchmark (the third panel is the side-constraint set). A recipe whose curve lies inside the random-deletion band on a panel is dead on that panel.
- **Pre-registered expectation.** Judgment probability 0.55. On SWE-bench and Terminal-Bench the summarization family (self-summary, ACON, SJC, FOCUS, transparent-compact, CliffCompaction) collapses to one band within 3 points of each other at every $r$; TRC and ReFold sit on or above that band at lower uncached prefill; random deletion is within 5 points of the summarization band at $r = 0.25$ on SWE-bench (the compaction-friendly case) and 15 or more points below it on the side-constraint set; the learned triggers' published gains shrink below 3 points once total retained tokens are matched (their gain was dose, not policy).
- **What the field assumes.** That a model-written summary beats mechanical deletion and that a better summarizer or a learned trigger is the lever. 2609.32961 already reports TRC matching full-context success at 0.57x tokens on SWE-bench while summarization loses 11 points; 2610.31778 reports no strategy beating default self-summarization; 2610.27301 reports no current-question sentence selector beating random deletion on later questions.
- **What would change.** If the expected result holds: every compaction paper reports damage-versus-dose with the random-deletion floor; harness defaults move to TRC-plus-reversible-stubs with summaries only for the side-constraint layer; cost teams stop pricing compaction by tokens saved. If a recipe beats random deletion by more than 10 points on every panel at matched dose and compute: that recipe becomes the default and the question moves to why its selection rule works.
- **Cost.** 3500 GPU-hours. Main grid: 16 arms (13 recipes + 3 controls) x 310 tasks x 3 seeds x 2 models = 29,760 runs at $r = 0.25$. Dose sweep: 7 arms x 2 extra $r$ values x 100 tasks x 3 seeds x 2 models = 8,400 runs. Reference and pilot: 310 tasks x 3 seeds x 2 models at a 1M window = 1,860 runs. Learned arms: fine-tuning AutoCompact and StateComp routers on the chosen harness: 300 GPU-hours (assumption: their papers' recipes at 27B scale). Paired-continuation and CONPACT scoring: 150 GPU-hours. Assumptions: 0.08 GPU-hours per agent run: a 35B-A3B or 27B model on H100s under vLLM with prefix caching, 60 to 80 calls, mean prompt 40K tokens, 30K decode tokens; the pilot measures this and rescales the grid. 40,000 runs x 0.08 = 3,200 GPU-hours, plus 450 for learned arms and scoring; the range is 2,000 to 7,000 if the per-run cost is off by 2x.
- **Consumers.** Every compaction and context-management paper (Gate 3 community-wide): report damage-versus-dose against the random-deletion floor and TRC on the shared harness, or the comparison is not accepted. Harness builders (OpenCode, OpenHands, Claude Code, Codex, Pi): the default trigger, retained fraction and primitive. Serving and cost teams: whether compaction is priced by uncached prefill rather than tokens saved (2610.34763 shows the sign can flip). RL trainers putting compaction in the loop (CompactionRL 2607.05378, KV-streams 2609.35750, FoldAct): which primitive to train against. Benchmark owners: a side-constraint track becomes part of agent benchmarks because the compaction-friendly ones cannot separate recipes.
- **Why no one has run it.** Each recipe paper evaluates on its own harness, benchmark and model against self-summarization only, with the recipe's own trigger (dose unmatched). The two systematic studies used harness primitives, not published recipes: implementing a recipe in someone else's harness is weeks of work per recipe and 2609.32961 needed 35,000 runs for 12 primitives. Frontier labs run the comparison on their own models (2610.31778 cites DeepSeek-V3.2 section 4.4 and Kimi K2.5 section 5.2) and do not release the harness. The random-deletion control is three weeks old (2610.27301, 2026-10-19), and the forced-regime design needs tasks long enough to compact twice, which are the expensive ones. No single author of a recipe is paid to show it ties random deletion.
- **Enabling piece.** Three pieces landed in five weeks: the paired-continuation protocol with released data (Compact-Bench, 2026-10-22), the random-deletion-at-matched-budget control (2610.27301, 2026-10-19), and an open harness with five compaction hooks and code (2610.31778, 2026-10-22). Before October 2026 there was no shared instrument that isolates one compaction event.
- **Publication rate.** 4.25 per week — Unique arXiv API hits in the last 8 weeks (2026-09-07 to 2026-11-01) whose title names compaction, context compression, context management, context folding or memory compaction for LLM agents: 34 papers / 8 weeks; 11 in the two weeks to 2026-10-28. The 12-month keyword count is 96 but is truncated by the 250-result cap on two queries (earliest row 2026-06-21), so the 8-week rate is the honest number.
- **Kill probe (solo-feasible).** Pilot of the forced regime only: 2 recipes (self-summary, TRC) plus random deletion at $r = 0.25$ on 60 qualifying SWE-bench tasks, 3 seeds, one 27B model, under the $64\,\text{K}$ cap, with the paired-continuation readout. Cost 60 GPU-hours, 7 days. Dead if: Random deletion is more than 10 points below both recipes on the paired readout with non-overlapping bootstrap bands in the middle pass-rate band: the floor is not a floor, the field's assumption holds, and the comparison is a service rather than a paper (the spec still stands as a service spec).
- **Claim attached.** CL-W43-05. **Reopen trigger.** lab compute of >= 3500 GPU-hours and a shared harness (one open scaffold with a pluggable compaction hook and the paired-continuation instrument)

### Does a definitive comparison exist? No. The two closest and what each lacks

- **2605.27881** Retrieval, Reward, and Training Protocols: What Matters in Training Search Agents? — has: Four credit strategies (Search-R1 outcome-only, GiGPO, IGPO, Tree-GRPO) re-implemented on one GRPO objective in one verl codebase; three base models; 9,000 training instances; five QA test sets; a process evaluation of search behaviour; released checkpoints. — lacks: Horizon capped at 4 tool-call turns, so it is not long-horizon; no critic-based arm (no PPO, no learned value); compute not matched (Tree-GRPO's branch rollouts and GiGPO's grouping are charged nothing; batch size fixed, not tokens); no shuffled or placebo control; no pass-rate bands; search QA only (exact match); its own limitation: 'Due to resource constraints, we cannot cover all existing credit assignment algorithms' and conclusions 'limited to local corpus-based search environments'.
- **2603.21972** Demystifying Reinforcement Learning for Long-Horizon Tool-Using Agents: A Comprehensive Recipe — has: Five axes (reward shaping, model scale 1.5B/3B/7B, data composition, algorithm, environment stability) on TravelPlanner with GRPO, group 8, 1K queries; GPU-hours per algorithm reported on 8 or 16 A100s; seven takeaways including that algorithm effects reverse with scale. — lacks: The algorithm axis is exploration (GRPO versus DAPO versus ARPO), not credit: no step-level estimator and no critic in the table; one environment; GPU-hours reported but not matched (ARPO's branching is allowed to cost more); no control arms; reward shaping compared as staged versus dense, which changes dose and credit together.
- **2605.00347** Odysseus (third-closest) — has: Seven variants (GRPO outcome and process, each with and without positive-advantage filtering, turn-level Reinforce++, PPO with a CNN critic, PPO with a turn-level CNN critic and filtering) on one Super Mario Land level with Qwen3-VL-8B at a fixed 0.38M training samples, at least two runs each. — lacks: One game level, dense game reward, a VLM; no group-size incumbent; no shuffled or placebo control; sample-matched but not token- or wall-clock-matched; no published step-credit recipe beyond GRPO variants.

### Spec (every number next to its assumption)

- **Harness.** One RL stack (verl, in which 2605.27881 re-implemented four credit methods and in which GiGPO's lineage lives), one rollout engine (vLLM), one environment interface. Every recipe is a plug-in advantage estimator or critic on the same actor loss with the same clipping, the same KL setting and the same sampling temperature; the authors' released code is used to check each re-implementation reproduces the published number on its own benchmark before it enters the table.
- **Regime / environments.** Three, chosen for horizon: ALFWorld (the lane's consensus benchmark, 10 to 30 turns), WebShop (5 to 15 turns), and one long-horizon executable one with forkable state so counterfactual recipes can run: AppWorld or a Terminal-Bench training split (30 to 100 turns). Assumption: the long one is the only place the field claims critics are needed (Odysseus, 2606.22164).
- **Regime / forced_horizon.** Minimum horizon enforced by task selection: tasks whose shortest known solution has at least 8 turns on ALFWorld and 20 turns on the long environment (assumption: this drops about a third of ALFWorld).
- **Regime / model_band.** Qwen3-8B (2605.27881's default) and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (the GiGPO and ECPO lineage); one size each, so the table is not confounded by scale (2603.21972 found algorithm effects reverse between 1.5B and 7B).
- **Regime / decision_density.** Each environment's decision density $\rho$ (2606.22164) is measured once from counterfactual branching on 200 trajectories and reported as the x-coordinate of the plot.
- **Matched compute.** Per training run a fixed budget of sampled tokens $T$ and environment steps $E$, counting branches, sibling rollouts, critic forward and backward passes and judge calls (assumption: $T$ set to what flat GRPO with group 8 uses for 300 optimizer steps; branching recipes therefore see fewer root prompts). Wall-clock on the same hardware is logged as the second axis. A recipe that needs an external judge model is charged its tokens.
- **Matched dose.** Dose is the number of tokens with non-zero advantage entering the optimizer and the number of optimizer steps. Recipes that zero out routine steps (SCOPE, ProVer, CRR at selected steps) reduce the dose; each such recipe gets a second arm in which the removed steps keep the flat GRPO advantage, so a gain from targeting is separated from a gain from dropping examples (checklist, Comparisons: 'is the comparison measuring dose or credit?').
- **Controls.** (1) Flat GRPO with group 8 (production default) and group 32 at the same token budget $T$ (strongest cheap incumbent: more siblings for free credit). (2) Shuffled credit: the recipe's own per-step weights permuted across steps within each trajectory, marginal-matched (same mean and spread), the control of CL-W43-06. (3) Placebo critic: the recipe's critic trained on returns from a different environment's trajectories, same architecture and budget. (4) Entropy-only credit: step weight proportional to the policy's mean token entropy on that step, the content-free targeting rule. (5) Pass-rate bands: tasks binned by the initial policy's pass rate over 16 samples into $[0, 0.2)$, $[0.2, 0.8)$, $[0.8, 1]$; every gain reported by band. (6) First-two-thirds split: credit quality (correlation with counterfactual return difference) reported separately for the first two thirds and the last third of each trajectory.
- **Metrics.** Success rate at the matched budget $T$ and at 0.5 $T$ and 2 $T$ (the curve, not one point), 3 seeds. Credit quality: Spearman correlation between the recipe's step advantage and the counterfactual return difference from 4 branch rollouts at 200 held-out decision points, by trajectory third. Stability: fraction of runs whose success drops more than 10 points from its peak before the budget ends. Cost: GPU-hours and wall-clock per run, critic memory.
- **The single plot.** Gain over compute-matched flat GRPO (group 8) in success points, on the y-axis, against the environment's decision density $\rho$ on the x-axis, one marker per recipe per environment with its 3-seed band, the group-32 incumbent drawn as a horizontal line per environment and each recipe's shuffled-credit control drawn as a hollow marker. A recipe whose filled marker is inside its hollow marker's band is dead at that $\rho$.

At matched compute, the large flat group absorbs at least half of every critic-free recipe's published gain on ALFWorld and WebShop; the critic-free hindsight recipes do not beat their shuffled control in the first two thirds of trajectories (CL-W43-06's parked claim); the stabilized critics (EasyPPO, Odysseus-style turn critic, SCALE) and the counterfactual recipes (CRR, TWICE) keep a gain of 5 or more points only on the long environment where $\rho$ is lowest; on the high pass-rate band no recipe beats flat GRPO.
- **What the field assumes.** That step-level credit beats trajectory-level credit on long horizons generally; 2605.27881 already found 'the simplest outcome-based approach achieves competitive or superior performance in most settings' and that process credit 'can over-correct', at a 4-turn horizon; 2603.21972 found exploration variants help only at 1.5B; Odysseus found critic-free methods fail at 100 turns.
- **What would change.** If the expected result holds: every credit paper reports against group-32 flat GRPO at matched tokens and against its shuffled control, by band; training teams spend the critic's compute on group size except on low-$\rho$ environments. If a critic-free recipe beats its shuffled control and group-32 on all three environments by more than 5 points: that estimator becomes the default and the critic question is closed for this horizon band.

RL training teams (Narasimhan route, any RL group): whether to pay for a critic and on which environments. Environment builders: decision density $\rho$ as a reported property of an agent environment. Theorists: the $\rho^{-1/2}$ prediction of 2606.22164 tested across recipes rather than in a toy.
- **Why no one has run it.** Each recipe paper compares to flat GRPO at its own group size on ALFWorld and WebShop with 1.5B to 7B models and no compute matching; branching and counterfactual recipes double rollouts and report the gain per optimizer step. ### Does a definitive comparison exist? No. The two closest and what each lacks

- **2607.13059** GPUSimBench: Towards Scalable and Reliable GPU-Accelerated Simulators in Embodied AI — has: Seven GPU engines (ManiSkill, Genesis, Madrona, MuJoCo Warp, MJX, MuJoCo Playground, Isaac Lab) on one workstation: throughput and memory on a cube array and a Franka scene, an inclined-plane collision test against a real apparatus (earth-mover distance per engine), and batch-position versus run-to-run variability types; a decision guide naming Isaac Lab the balanced default. — lacks: Every engine at its shipped settings with one unified time step of 0.01 s and no sweep of solver iterations, time step or contact softness; no policy trained, so no return and no transfer; one contact-rich fidelity test; its own limitation: 'two scalability scenes and one contact-rich real-world-matched experiment, all on a single hardware and software stack ... the reported performance and variability may change across different GPUs, drivers, and simulator versions'.
- **2608.05948** GAUGE: A Measurement-Grounded Benchmark for Physical Fidelity in Simulation Engines and Video World Models — has: Isaac Sim 6.0, Genesis 1.12 and Newton 1.3 on 14 motion-capture-grounded tasks (rigid bodies, textiles, volumetric deformables) with calibrated material parameters, trajectory error per engine, 180 Hz simulation, and a parallel video-world-model track. — lacks: Engines at defaults by design ('all other settings remain at their defaults without task-specific fine-tuning'); no solver-budget or time-step axis; no policy, so fidelity is never tied to return or to throughput; its limitations section names material ranges and fluids, not solver settings; three engines, not MJX or Warp.
- **2510.01708** PolySim (third-closest) — has: One humanoid tracking policy trained across IsaacGym, IsaacSim and Genesis with a router that harmonises friction, actuator gains, gravity and integration step; 14 ASAP motions; MuJoCo as the unseen test engine; single-engine and mixed arms compared on success and tracking error. — lacks: Engines at harmonised defaults, no fidelity knob swept; the comparison is engine mixing versus single engine, not setting versus setting; compute per arm not matched (mixed arms run more engines); one task family; no audit of what the policies exploit.

### Spec (every number next to its assumption)

- **Harness.** One task specification in MJCF for three contact-rich tasks, loaded unchanged into MJX, MuJoCo Warp, Newton (MuJoCo solver and Kamino), Genesis and Isaac Lab (MJCF import), with PolySim's simulator-router idea (2510.01708: friction, actuator gains, gravity and integration step harmonised) extended to five engines; one PPO implementation (rsl_rl-style, one code path, engine behind a step() interface); one evaluation reference per task: the same engine's converged setting (iterations x8, time step /4, hard contact) plus one cross-engine reference, so 'transferable return' is defined without a robot and then validated on one task against GAUGE's real rigid-body trajectories.
- **Regime / tasks.** Quadruped rough-terrain locomotion (Go2 or ANYmal), in-hand cube reorientation (Allegro or LEAP), peg-in-hole insertion (Franka); assumption: one task per class where the lane's literature claims exploitation (SAPU for insertion, GPUSimBench's inclined plane for contact).
- **Regime / knob_grid.** Solver iterations $k \in \{k_0/2, k_0, 2k_0, 4k_0, 8k_0\}$ where $k_0$ is each engine's shipped cap; time step $h \in \{h_0, h_0/2, h_0/4\}$; contact softness $\sigma \in \{\sigma_0, \text{hard}\}$ where 'hard' is the engine's stiffest stable setting; full grid on the iteration axis at $(h_0, \sigma_0)$ and one-at-a-time on the other two (assumption: 5 + 2 + 1 = 8 settings per engine per task, with the converged corner as the ninth).
- **Regime / model_band.** One PPO policy architecture and hyperparameter set per task across engines (assumption: the Isaac Lab defaults for that task, frozen before the first run).
- **Regime / forced_regime.** Every arm trains to the same wall-clock GPU budget (compute-matched) and, in a second arm, to the same environment-step count (dose-matched), because a higher iteration cap lowers throughput and the two arms separate 'fidelity costs samples' from 'fidelity costs time'.
- **Matched compute.** GPU-hours per training run fixed at $B$ (assumption: $B = 2$ GPU-hours on one H100 for locomotion at 4096 environments, the field's typical budget; $B = 4$ for manipulation). Throughput (steps per second) is logged per setting so the frontier can be re-read at any budget.
- **Matched dose.** Environment steps fixed at $N$ (assumption: $N$ = the steps the default setting reaches in $B$); higher-fidelity arms then take up to 8x longer. Both arms are reported; the plot uses the compute-matched arm.
- **Controls.** (1) Shuffled budget: per-environment iteration count drawn at random each step with the same mean as $k_0$ (tests whether the mean solver budget or the cap is what matters; also the systems lever's premise). (2) Placebo: friction and mass domain randomization at $k_0$ (the field's standard answer to the reality gap), same compute. (3) PolySim-style engine mixing at defaults, same compute (the published cross-engine recipe as an arm). (4) Exploit audit: per-step physics residual $d_t = \lVert F_{\text{ref}}(x_t, u_t) - x_{t+1} \rVert$ on the states each trained policy visits, with $F_{\text{ref}}$ the converged step of the same engine, and the exploit share $\rho_{\text{ex}} = \sum_t r_t \mathbf{1}[d_t > \kappa] / \sum_t r_t$ (themes.yaml L5 instrument; $\kappa$ calibrated on converged-trained policies). (5) Real validation on one task: the trained policies' contact trajectories scored against GAUGE's rigid-body motion-capture data (2608.05948) so 'converged reference' is checked against reality at least once. (6) Seeds: 3 per cell; pass-rate bands replaced by task-difficulty bands (terrain level, cube goal angle, hole clearance).
- **Metrics.** In-engine return $R_{\text{train}}$ (what the paper would report). Reference return $R_{\text{ref}}$: the same policy evaluated under the converged setting of its own engine and under one other engine's converged setting. Transfer loss $\Delta = 1 - R_{\text{ref}} / R_{\text{train}}$ and the exploit share $\rho_{\text{ex}}$. Throughput in environment steps per second per setting; GPU-hours to reach 90% of the setting's final return.
- **The single plot.** Reference return $R_{\text{ref}}$ against training GPU-hours, one curve per (engine, iteration cap) at the shipped time step and contact setting, one panel per task, with each engine's shipped default marked by a filled point; the upper envelope is the throughput-versus-verified-fidelity frontier the lane names, and a default that sits below the envelope at its own budget is a shipped setting that trades physics for speed.
