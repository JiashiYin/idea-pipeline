Glossary (each term once): **base policy** $\pi_{\mathrm{B}}$ — the model before RL (here Qwen3-4B); **RL policy** $\pi_{\mathrm{RL}}$ —
the same model after GRPO on FrozenLake. **Continuation policy** — the policy that plays out the rest of the episode after the action
being graded. **$Q^{\pi}(h, a)$** — the success probability of taking action $a$ at history $h$ and following policy $\pi$ afterwards;
**$V^{\pi}(h)$** — its average over $\pi$'s own actions; **$Q^{*}$** — the same with an optimal continuation (what 2611.06483 computes by
dynamic programming). **Advantage** $A^{\pi}(h, a) = Q^{\pi}(h, a) - V^{\pi}(h)$ — how much better $a$ is than $\pi$'s own choice, judged by
$\pi$'s continuation. **Performance difference lemma** (Kakade and Langford 2002) — the identity that the total gain of one policy over
another equals the sum, over the states the first policy visits, of its actions' advantages judged by the second policy:
$$J(\pi_{\mathrm{RL}}) - J(\pi_{\mathrm{B}}) = \sum_{t=1}^{T_{\max}} \mathbb{E}_{h_t \sim d_t^{\pi_{\mathrm{RL}}},\ a_t \sim \pi_{\mathrm{RL}}(\cdot \mid h_t)} \bigl[ A^{\pi_{\mathrm{B}}}(h_t, a_t) \bigr],$$
with $J(\pi)$ the success probability of $\pi$, $d_t^{\pi}$ the distribution of histories at turn $t$ under $\pi$, $T_{\max}$ the turn cap,
discount 1, deterministic environment. **Prover policy** — Setlur et al.'s name (2410.08146) for the continuation policy a process reward
is measured under. **Matched state** — one history from which both policies sample an action, so the comparison is of actions, not of
where each policy went. **State visitation / state weights** — how often each policy reaches each history. **Win share** — the fraction
of matched states on which one policy's mean action value exceeds the other's. **Process reward** — a per-step score used as a training
signal. **Ordinary state** — any state not in 2611.06483's injected-error pool.

## 0. Verdict in one paragraph

**Killed (Gate 2B; Gate 6 for the why-now).** The claim has two parts: (i) step quality depends on the continuation policy and on which
states are weighted, so a half-and-half pool graded by a perfect player is one ruler among several; (ii) under the base policy's own
continuation on RL-visited ordinary states, the RL action wins more than half. Part (i) is the field's stated knowledge in the process-reward
community: Rewarding Progress (Setlur et al., 2410.08146, 2024-10-10) builds its theory on exactly this — "this progress should be measured
under a prover policy distinct from the base policy" — states the performance difference lemma verbatim as its Lemma F.1, and writes the
claim's mechanism sentence two years early: a capable prover's $Q^{\mu}$ "before and after this irrelevant step will be identical since
$\mu$ can succeed from either step. This means that $\mu$ fails to distinguish steps, resulting in $A^{\mu} \approx 0$ in most cases." The
state-weight half of the lemma is stated for LLM RL in 2609.39634's abstract ("estimate policy improvement under the behavioral policy's
state-visitation distribution rather than the improved policy's own"). Part (ii) is not a surprise against 2611.06483 either: the paper's
authors chose the symmetric pool *in order to remove* visitation ("To isolate action quality from state visitation bias, we construct a
state evaluation pool by sampling intermediate states equally from base and RL trajectories"), and their reading of the gain is already
"long-horizon execution and error recovery" and "long-horizon resilience" — which is what "RL's actions are better under the base's
continuation on the states RL steers into" says in other words; their Figure 6 already shows the matched-state gap "gradually narrows and
reverses" with depth. So the expected result (RL wins 60–70% of RL-visited ordinary states under $Q^{\pi_{\mathrm{B}}}$) would not change
the authors' belief or anyone's ruler; it relabels the same mechanism. The claim's `if_true` also carries a Gate 0 defect: the
$R_{\mathrm{single}}$ decline is measured on "a fixed pool of 512 states from base model trajectories", where there is no visitation shift
by construction, so "a visitation shift, not a loss of step quality" cannot be its reading. The claim's why-now ("the identity is from 2002
and unused by any of them") is false for the nearest community (PAV uses it), so Gate 6 reads "no reason found" for the one thing nobody has
done — tabulating the two rulers on one agent checkpoint — and the people with the checkpoints are the only ones who would want it. What
survives is a footnote-sized residue on one environment: a one-quantifier test of the paper's "concentrates in error recovery" by the
lemma's own decomposition over the RL policy's natural (non-injected) trajectories. No neighbour is "none — occupied" on the exact two-ruler
table; the kill is Gate 2B, not Gate 6 occupancy.

## 3. The two closest papers' limitation text (verbatim, grep-verified)

**2611.06483** (no Limitations section; the sentences that bound the step-quality reading): sec. 3.3: "Strictly evaluating one-step
quality is challenging because reliable process rewards remain difficult to obtain in agentic settings" ... "To isolate action quality
from state visitation bias, we construct a state evaluation pool by sampling intermediate states equally from base and RL trajectories."
... "RL achieves better step-level decision quality on only 46.8% of DeepCoder-MT states and 42.8% of FrozenLake states, with average
quality no better than, even slightly below, the base model" ... "the pattern reverses under these error states: data points shift
predominantly above the diagonal, with the RL model selecting the superior action in 65.7% of cases on FrozenLake and 67.3% on
DeepCoder-MT" ... "the RL model shows little advantage early in execution and even underperforms the base model. As interaction proceeds,
however, the gap gradually narrows and reverses." ... "This indicates that the RL model does not make superior decisions at the beginning
of a trajectory; its advantage emerges as interaction unfolds." Sec. 3.4: "Yet higher trajectory success does not ensure better individual
actions." App. B.4: "construct a state pool with equal contributions from the two policies to mitigate bias". App. B.5: "Both constructions
serve as proxies rather than rigorous definitions of error states." App. E.3: "we construct a fixed pool of 512 states from base model
trajectories on held-out tasks". Conclusion: "these gains stem primarily from long-horizon execution and error recovery, rather than
consistently superior single-step actions".

**2410.08146 Rewarding Progress**, sec. 3.2: "Perhaps a natural starting point is to set the prover to be identical to the base policy"
... which "results in exactly the same policy gradient update as only optimizing outcome evaluation" ... "Here, $Q^{\mu}$ for a capable
prover before and after this irrelevant step will be identical since $\mu$ can succeed from either step. This means that $\mu$ fails to
distinguish steps, resulting in $A^{\mu} \approx 0$ in most cases." Abstract: "Crucially, this progress should be measured under a prover
policy distinct from the base policy." App. F.2, Lemma F.1: "[The performance difference lemma; (Kakade and Langford, 2002)]".
Limitations: "it is unclear how to automatically design a flexible class of optimal (or very good) prover policies for a sequence of base
policy iterates" ... "extending our approach to this setup is a good avenue for future work."

**Third, 2605.06241 Sparse Policy Selection**, sec. 3.2: "during deterministic generation from the base model, at every position where the
teacher disagrees ... we replace the base token with the teacher's preferred token and continue generating from the corrected prefix"
... "reproduces the teacher's pass@1 exactly on every pair" ... "RL's benefit is not a diffuse effect but is concentrated at a few branch

**Fourth, 2604.14877**, sec. 5: "RL pushes probability mass within the base model's existing strategy distribution toward the subset whose

## 4. Why not done, and why now

reason is not an enabling piece: the base policy, the checkpoint and a deterministic grid were all in 2611.06483's hands, and Monte Carlo

## 5. Gate-by-gate verdict

- **Gate 0 (premise hygiene).** Quantity written before the estimator (the lemma, finite horizon, discount 1, deterministic grid, no luck
  term): pass. Two defects: (a) the `if_true` reads the $R_{\mathrm{single}}$ decline as "a visitation shift", but $R_{\mathrm{single}}$ is
  measured on "a fixed pool of 512 states from base model trajectories" where visitation cannot shift — the honest reading is that the RL
  policy's actions on base-visited states it no longer reaches are off its own distribution; (b) the win share is a strict-inequality
  statistic on a grid where both policies at temperature 0.6 often agree, so ties and the 4-continuation binomial noise push it toward 50%
  by construction; the headline needs a tie rule and a noise floor before its 52% kill line means anything. Pass with repairs owed.
- **Gate 1 (cheap to kill).** Pass on paper: about 30 GPU-h marginal on the shared checkpoint, 130 GPU-h standalone, kill written. But the
  pre-registered kill ("win share below 52% with the identity check passing") is weak: the identity holds in expectation by theorem, so
  "passing" is a calibration of the estimator, not a finding; and a win share near 50% is the tie-and-noise outcome as much as the
  recovery-concentration outcome. The informative statistic is the share of the summed advantage on post-error histories, which the probe
  carries as a secondary readout.
- **Gate 2 (pressure).** Route B fails on 2B. The dependence of step quality on the continuation policy is PAV's theorem and its
  mechanism sentence; the dependence on state weights is the lemma, stated for LLM RL by 2609.39634; and the expected result (RL better
  under the base's continuation on the states RL steers into) is 2611.06483's own reading ("long-horizon execution and error recovery";
  "long-horizon resilience") in other words — the authors would call a 65% win share under $Q^{\pi_{\mathrm{B}}}$ a confirmation, not a
  reversal. The surprise equals the field's stated knowledge. **Failing gate.**
- **Gate 3 (consumers).** Who changes what if the share flips: 2611.06483's authors re-read one figure; process-reward builders already name
  the prover; critic papers gain a method note. One community at most, and the action ("name the continuation policy and the state weights")
  is a sentence in a methods section. Footnote on one paper's Figure 4, not a ruler the field adopts.
- **Gate 4 (mechanism and consequence).** The mechanism is sound (a safer longer route scores lower under $Q^{*}$ with $\gamma = 0.95$ and
  higher under $Q^{\pi_{\mathrm{B}}}$ when the base fails on the shorter one) and is PAV's sentence; the consequence is a reading rule the
  nearest community already follows. Pass, but owned.
- **Gate 5 (durability).** The identity applies to any pair; the FrozenLake number dies with the paper's v2 or with the first realistic
  environment. Lab-adjacent. Pass.
- **Gate 6 (ownership, density, timing).** Density: "what RL learned" analysis papers run about one per five weeks (Q7b: 15 in-window titles,
  5 on RL for LLMs; plus 2605.06241, 2604.14877, 2610.01509, 2507.14843); per-step credit rulers under the behaviour policy about one per two
  weeks (Q8, Q12). Pre-window precedents: Kakade and Langford 2002 (CPI), TRPO 1502.05477, Math-Shepherd 2312.08935 ($Q^{\pi_{\mathrm{completer}}}$

  1–2 week result: window ratio fine, but the owners of the only checkpoints (the paper's authors) can produce the table in a day.
  **Position clause.** *Solo:* killed — a response to one figure that its authors can answer faster, on a ruler their neighbours already
  name. *Lab:* killed for the same reason; a room with its own agent checkpoints would run the two-ruler table as an afternoon's sanity check

## 7. Lessons for `prompts/checklist.md`

- 2026-11-08 — A claim whose headline is "X is a ruler choice" is occupied the day the nearest community has a theorem about which ruler to
  use: search the process-reward community's role words (prover policy, completer, completion model, policy-dependent labels) before writing
  "the field reads Y as a property of the policy". PAV 2410.08146 §3.2 and Lemma F.1 held CL-W45-04's mechanism and identity two years early.
- 2026-11-08 — A re-reading claim must name the sentence of the target paper it would overturn and check that the paper's own words do not
  already say the re-reading: 2611.06483 removed visitation on purpose ("to isolate action quality from state visitation bias") and already
  reads its gain as "long-horizon resilience", so "RL wins on the states it steers into" confirms rather than reverses it (Gate 2B, third
  occurrence this month of a claim whose surprise equals the target's stated knowledge).
- 2026-11-08 — An identity that holds by theorem (the performance difference lemma; a full-substitution oracle under greedy decoding) is an
  estimator calibration, not a pre-registered finding; a kill clause that requires "the identity check passes" is satisfiable by
  construction. Pre-register the informative statistic (the share of the sum on a stratum) instead. 2605.06241's "reproduces the teacher's
  pass@1 exactly" is the same tautology in print.
- 2026-11-08 — A win share over matched states is a strict-inequality statistic; on deterministic grids where both policies agree at most
  states, ties and Monte Carlo noise drive it to 50% by construction. Write the tie rule and the noise floor before setting a kill line
  near 50%.
