**Terms (one gloss each).** *Iteration cap K* (`iterations` in the XML): the maximum number of constraint-solver
iterations per physics step; MuJoCo's default is 100, Playground ships 1 (Go1) and 5 (Leap hand). *Warm start*:
starting the solver from the previous step's accelerations. *Exact Newton on a quadratic spline*: MuJoCo's solver
minimises a convex, piecewise-quadratic cost; Newton with an exact line search reaches the minimiser of the current
quadratic piece in one step, so convergence proceeds by *active-set identification* (finding which constraints are
active) rather than by a fixed shrink factor per iteration. *Certificate*: the gradient norm of that cost, which bounds
the distance to the converged solution. *e_t(K)*: the record's one-step error, the distance between the state after one
step at cap K and after a converged step, from the same state. *E_t*: the global error, the distance between the cap-K
trajectory and the converged trajectory at time t. *rho_L5*: the record's exploit share, the fraction of return earned
on steps whose e_t(K_default) exceeds a threshold kappa. *2x2 return matrix*: train a policy in each of two engines and
evaluate each in both; the interaction term is what "exploitation" means operationally. *Domain randomization (DR)*:
re-sampling physical parameters every episode so a policy cannot overfit one model. *float32 floor*: the smallest
relative change a 32-bit number can represent (about 1.2e-7); errors below it are rounding, not physics.

## 0. Verdict in one paragraph

**Killed as a probe of CL-W43-01; its residue is a zero-GPU engineering note.** Two objections attack what the probe can
establish and were not answered after two rounds. (i) The "law" half is the wrong model and the wrong instrument:
MuJoCo's documentation states that the Newton solver is exact on a piecewise-quadratic, strongly convex cost, returns
with zero iterations when the warm start already satisfies the tolerance, and spends iterations only when constraint
states change (§1, T-O3); so e_t(K) is not a·rho^K, a per-state contraction factor does not exist by theory, the
pre-registered kill (a) cannot fire, and the "bimodal" result the record pre-registers as a surprise is the engine
manual's own description. The per-iteration trace the probe would reconstruct from eight re-steps is already a public
field of CPU MuJoCo (`mjData.solver[]`: improvement, gradient, nactive, nchange per iteration; `fwdinv`), so the law
half is an afternoon's profiler reading, not a 10-GPU-hour experiment, and "why now" is false (P-O1). (ii) The exploit
half is not measured by rho_L5: for deterministic engines the return gap between the capped and the converged engine is
a signed, value-weighted sum of one-step errors (equation in §1, T-O1), not a reward-weighted count of threshold
crossings; rho_L5 can be 1 with zero exploitation and 0 with full exploitation. The direct estimator — the 2x2 return
matrix (cap-1-trained and cap-100-trained Go1, each evaluated in both engines) — is cheaper than the probe and absent
from its kill criteria (T-O2). Around those two, the red team narrowed everything else: MJX-JAX runs float32, so the
1e-8 reference is unreachable without `jax_enable_x64` (S-O1); K = 1 is a separate code path in MJX (S-O3); Playground
ships one checkpoint, not four (S-O5); the cap is a model-file attribute carried to CPU MuJoCo and to every engine, so
"GPU physics engines" names the wrong actor (S-O6, P-O5); the MJX docs state the trade and justify it by DR noise, so
"silent" is wrong (S-O4, T-O5); the shipped cap-1 Go1 policies "transfer robustly" to hardware, which is the prior
against the exploit clause (P-O3); and the exploit clause itself is 2017 folklore (Peng et al.) whose cross-engine version
PolySim measured in-window with a bound — a paper inside the kill search's own Q4 hit set that was not opened (P-O2).
Effect on the claim: `CL-W43-01` stays open but loses its cheapest probe, its headline carries two defects for the next
`/claims` run (actor; exploit clause), and Gate 1 is not satisfied for the claim until a 2x2 probe under the training
protocol is written (§6). Raised 20 objections; 15 conceded, 3 answered, 2 open (claim-attacking, after two rounds).

### T-O1 — rho_L5 does not estimate exploitation (OPEN → kill)
- **Objection.** Attacks kill (b) and the pre-registered separation of the two Go1 policies. Let x_t be the state, u_t the
  action, w_t the warm start, F_1 and F_inf one physics step at cap 1 and at the converged reference, pi the policy,
  J_K(pi) the finite-horizon return of pi rolled in engine K, V^{pi,inf}_t(x) the value-to-go from x under pi in the
  reference engine, and delta_t = F_1(x_t,u_t,w_t) − F_inf(x_t,u_t,w_t) the *signed* one-step error. For deterministic
  engines the telescoping identity over hybrid rollouts (engine 1 for the first k steps, the reference afterwards) is

  J_1(pi) − J_inf(pi) = Σ_{t<T} E_{x_t ~ pi in engine 1} [ V^{pi,inf}_{t+1}(F_1(x_t,u_t,w_t)) − V^{pi,inf}_{t+1}(F_inf(x_t,u_t,w_t)) ] ≈ Σ_t E[ ∇V^{pi,inf}_{t+1} · delta_t ].

  The weight on each step is the directional derivative of value-to-go along the signed error — not the reward r_t and
  not an indicator on ||delta_t||. Counterexample A (rho_L5 = 1, no exploitation): every rewarded step crosses kappa but
  delta_t alternates sign or lies where ∇V = 0. Counterexample B (rho_L5 = 0, full exploitation): a systematic bias
  delta_bar with ∇V·delta_bar = ε > 0 per stance step and ||delta_bar|| < kappa gives a gap of T·ε over T = 5,000 physics
  steps per Go1 episode (`joystick.py`: ctrl_dt 0.02, sim_dt 0.004, episode_length 1000), while kill (b) reads "premise
  refuted". B is the published shape of simulator exploitation.
- **Evidence.** [argument]; 2605.29032 [abstract-only] (trick: train a learned world model as the min player against an
  exploiting policy, because "powerful RL optimizers inevitably exploit minor model inaccuracies"; it bounds "the global
  policy-value gap by the local critic's loss" and proves an "Error-MDP duality" where the worst-case policy's reward is
  the one-step critic error).
- **Generator's answer (round 1).** rho_L5 was a screening statistic, not an estimator of the gap; the identity is
  correct and the counterexamples are real. The gap estimator the claim needs is the 2x2 matrix in T-O2.
- **Round 2.** Replacing rho_L5 by the 2x2 matrix changes the probe into a different experiment with a different kill
  criterion; under `prompts/04_redteam.md` a changed probe is a new candidate. **Status: open** (attacks the claim's
  exploit clause as this probe tests it).

### T-O3 / P-O1 — the geometric law is the wrong model and the instrument already ships (conceded → the law half is a note)
- **Objection (T-O3).** All three tasks use the default pyramidal cone and Newton solver (Go1, Leap and cheetah XMLs carry
  no `cone`/`solver` attribute). MuJoCo's cost is a convex quadratic spline with curvature at least M (the inertia
  matrix); exact Newton with an exact line search converges by finite active-set identification, and MJX's line search
  solves the 1-D quadratic exactly (`solver.py`: `lo.alpha − lo.deriv_0/lo.deriv_1`, accept when |deriv_0| < gtol). So
  e_t(K) reaches the number-format floor after finitely many active-set changes; it is not a_t·rho_t^K; a single-rho fit
  with R² ≥ 0.95 cannot occur except at the floor; kill (a) is unreachable; and "fraction of steps with R² < 0.9"
  measures model misspecification. The pre-registered "bimodal" surprise is the docs' own sentence. The record's
  `field_assumes` misreads the card: 2610.17221 obtained rho = 0.95 by freezing the Jacobian (chord Newton, App. F) and
  refused the SIMPLEC axis because exact iteration is not geometric — the card already knew.
- **Evidence.** mujoco.readthedocs.io computation docs [source]: "In the case of pyramidal friction cones it is a
  quadratic spline." / "This algorithm implements the exact Newton method, with analytical second-order derivatives and
  Cholesky factorization of the Hessian … When constraint states change between iterations … the Hessian factorization
  is updated incrementally" / "Because every zone of the piecewise-quadratic cost has curvature of at least M, the cost
  is strongly convex in the M-norm … If it is below tolerance, convergence is proven and the solver returns immediately
  with zero iterations; … In a quiescent, well-warmstarted scene this eliminates nearly the entire cost of the constraint
  solver." 2610.17221 App. F [full]: "The Jacobian is computed once at the initial guess … and frozen for all subsequent
  iterations. This design ensures linear convergence (O(rho^k)) rather than the quadratic convergence of standard
  Newton–Raphson." MJX docs [source]: "The NEWTON Solver delivers excellent convergence with very few (often just one)
  solver iterations".
- **Objection (P-O1).** CPU MuJoCo logs the entire per-iteration trace on every step: `mjData.solver[]` is an array of
  `mjSolverStat` with `improvement` ("cost reduction, scaled by 1/trace(M)"), `gradient`, `nactive`, `nchange`
  ("number of constraint state changes"), plus `solver_niter`; the `fwdinv` flag records a per-step forward–inverse
  discrepancy whose documented purpose is to show when "the forward solver—which is numerical and is usually terminated
  early—is not converging well." Run Playground's own `play_go1_joystick.py` (CPU MuJoCo, same XML, same ONNX policy)
  at iterations = 100, tolerance 1e-8, and read the trace: cost(a_K) − cost* = Σ_{k>K} improvement_k · trace(M) for every
  K at once, and strong convexity gives ||a_K − a*||²_M ≤ 2(cost(a_K) − cost*), hence e_t(K) = h·||a_K − a*||_M·(1+O(h))
  under Euler, where a_K is the acceleration after K iterations, a* the converged one, h the time step. `nactive` and
  `nchange` are the record's n_t and "active set changed" columns. MJX lists `FWDINV` as unsupported, so the GPU path
  cannot do this; the CPU path can and is the one Playground's sim2sim script runs.
- **Evidence.** `include/mujoco/mjdata.h` lines 86–94, 123 [source]; docs "Inverse dynamics" and "Warmstart" [source];
  `mjx/_src/types.py` line 96 "# unsupported: OVERRIDE, ENERGY, FWDINV, ISLAND, DIAGEXACT" [source].
- **Generator's answer.** Conceded on both counts. What is not in print is the *distribution* of residuals and
  iteration counts along a trained policy's rollout at the shipped cap; but with the fitting procedure gone and the
  mechanism in the manual, that distribution is a service (Gate 2B: expected result equals the field's stated
  knowledge), readable in a day on CPU at zero GPU-hours. The law half survives only as that note. **Status: conceded.**

### S-O1 / T-O6 — the 1e-8 reference and the large-K tail are float32 artefacts (conceded; design correction)
- **Objection.** MJX-JAX runs float32 unless `jax_enable_x64` is set; the solver code floors what the line search can
  resolve at 8·eps_f32 of the local scale, and Warp floors `gtol` at 1e-6. A tolerance of 1e-8 is unreachable; e_t(K)
  saturates at the floor after a handful of iterations; the eight-point fit {1,2,3,5,10,20,50,100} has at most four
  points above the floor; the "non-geometric" result would be manufactured by the number format.
- **Evidence.** `mjx/_src/solver.py` line 508 [source]: "gtol_accept = jp.maximum(gtol, 8 * jp.finfo(dmag.dtype).eps *
  dmag)"; `mujoco_warp/_src/solver.py` [source]: "gtol = wp.max(tolerance * ls_tolerance * snorm * scale, 1e-6)" and
  "_ALPHA_NOISE_EPS = 8.0 * 1.1920929e-07  # 8 * float32 eps"; [argument]: with eps ≈ 1.19e-7 and a per-iteration shrink
  of 0.1–0.5, the floor is reached at K ≈ 7–23.
- **Generator's answer.** Conceded. Any re-step reference must run in float64 (`jax_enable_x64`, or CPU MuJoCo whose
  `mjtNum` is double) and report the per-state floor; rho fitted only on points above 10× floor — moot once the fit is
  dropped (T-O3). **Status: conceded.**

### S-O3 — K = 1 is a different code path in MJX; the line-search cap is held fixed (conceded; design correction)
- **Objection.** In MJX `solve()`, `iterations == 1` runs `body(ctx)` once unconditionally (no tolerance check, no
  zero-iteration exit), while every K ≥ 2 enters a `while_loop` whose predicate checks the tolerance before the first
  iteration. So at the shipped Go1 setting "cap-hit" is 100% of steps by construction; when the warm start already
  satisfies the tolerance, K ≥ 2 runs zero iterations and K = 1 runs one, so e_t(2) ≥ e_t(1) is possible by branching,
  not physics; and `ls_iterations` stays at 5 (Go1) / 8 (Leap) through the sweep, so the "reference" at cap 100 is an
  inexact Newton with a 5-step line search.
- **Evidence.** `mjx/_src/solver.py` lines 634–637 [source]: "if m.opt.iterations == 1: ctx = body(ctx) else: ctx =
  jax.lax.while_loop(cond, body, ctx)"; `go1_mjx_feetonly.xml` line 4 [source].
- **Generator's answer.** Conceded: report `solver_niter` actually performed per step and per K; treat K = 1 as its own
  arm; set `ls_iterations` high for the reference. **Status: conceded.**

  PolySim 2510.01708 (trick: run environments from several physics engines inside one training run so the policy cannot
  overfit any engine's assumptions) measures the exploit at the engine level as a sim-to-sim success drop, proves a

  field's existing exploit test is "run the policy in a second engine"; the cap sweep is one knob of a difference the
  field already measures whole.
- **Evidence.** 1710.06537 §III [full]: "it has been observed that DeepRL policies are prone to exploiting
  idiosyncrasies of the simulator to realize behaviours that are infeasible in the real world [2], [7]. Therefore,
  instead of training a policy under one particular dynamics model, we train a policy that can perform a task under a
  variety of different dynamics models." 2510.01708 [abstract-only]: "Theoretically, we show that PolySim yields a
  tighter upper bound on simulator inductive bias than single-simulator training. In experiments, PolySim substantially
  reduces motion-tracking error in sim-to-sim evaluations; for example, on MuJoCo, it improves execution success by 52.8

### T-O4 — e_t(K) is a local truncation error with a free certificate; the policy sees the global error E_t (conceded)
- **Objection.** Under the Euler step delta_t = (h²·d_a, h·d_a) with d_a = qacc^(K) − qacc^(inf), so e_t(K) = h·||d_a||_M
  (1 + O(h)): the position part is 1/h = 250× smaller and the scale is set by h and M, so one kappa across tasks with
  h = 0.004 and 0.01 compares different units. Strong convexity gives ½||a_K − a*||²_M ≤ cost(a_K) − cost* ≤
  ½·g_Kᵀ M⁻¹ g_K, so e_t(K) ≤ h·||g_K||_{M⁻¹} with g_K the gradient MuJoCo already evaluates at the capped iterate: eight
  re-steps buy a number bounded by one gradient the engine has. What pi_1 experiences is E_t: with L the Lipschitz
  constant of F_inf∘pi, E_{t+1} ≤ e_t(1) + L·E_t, so E_t ≤ Σ_{s<t} L^{t−1−s} e_s(1), bounded by e/(1 − L) only in
  contracting stance and growing like L^t through flight and contact onsets; "e < kappa on 99% of steps" leaves 50 of
  5,000 substeps per episode feeding a possibly expanding map.
- **Evidence.** [argument]; docs "Warmstart" paragraph [source]; `mjx/_src/forward.py` lines 395–396 (`qacc_warmstart =
  qacc`) [source].
- **Generator's answer.** Conceded: the certificate ||g_K||_{M⁻¹} logged at the shipped cap is the per-state fidelity
  number the candidate wanted and is free on CPU (and is C-W43-17's object); E_t needs paired rollouts in both engines.
  **Status: conceded.**

## 7. Checklist lessons (2026-10-20, red team)

- Before pre-registering a "law" for an iterative solver, read the solver's own convergence statement in its manual:
  an exact Newton method on a piecewise-quadratic cost converges by active-set identification, not by a contraction
  factor, and a geometric fit is misspecified by construction (MuJoCo computation docs; C-W43-15).
- A re-simulation audit of an engine's solver must first read the engine's own per-iteration statistics (MuJoCo
  `mjData.solver[]`, `solver_niter`, `fwdinv`); when the trace is already logged, the sweep is a profiler read and "why
  now" is false.
- A "share of return on high-error steps" is not an estimator of a return gap; for deterministic engines the gap is the
  signed, value-weighted sum of one-step errors (telescoping identity), and the operational test of "exploitation" is
  the 2x2 train-in / evaluate-in matrix's interaction term.
- Number format is part of the conditioning set: a JAX/Warp simulator runs float32 by default and floors its tolerance
  at about 8 × 1.2e-7 relative, so a 1e-8 reference is unreachable there; state the dtype and the floor before any
  residual is read.
- When a kill search's hit set contains a paper whose title names the claim's mechanism at a coarser grain (PolySim:
  multi-simulator randomization, in the "sim-to-sim AND policy" hits), open it; a hit counted and unopened is a miss
  (second occurrence after Branching Policy Optimization, 2026-10-08).
- "Set for performance with no accuracy check" must be checked against the engine's performance-tuning page, where the
  trade and its justification are often stated in one sentence (MJX docs: solver accuracy subordinated to DR noise).
