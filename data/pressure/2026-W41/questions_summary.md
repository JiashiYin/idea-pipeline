Companion to `questions.jsonl` (88 records, 56 distinct sources, heuristics 1–9). Every claim below traces to text

Plain-language glosses: *pass@k* = chance that at least one of k samples succeeds (coverage); *pass^k* = chance that
all k repeats succeed (reliability); *harness/scaffold* = the code around the model that manages tools, retries and
submission; *simulator* = an LLM playing the user; *executed replay* = re-sampling an alternative action at a step and
running the episode forward to see if the outcome changes.

## 2. Contradictions between sources

1. **RL sharpens vs RL teaches, in the agentic domain.** Sharpening Tax (2610.01509): base models beat post-trained ones at pass@128 in 36 of 42 agentic cases; "post-training still mainly changes how reliably a model solves tasks rather than which tasks it can solve". PASS@(k,T) (2604.14877): "tool-use RL genuinely enlarges the agent's capability boundary ... the gap widens at the right tail rather than converging." How RL Reshapes (2610.04158) supplies the reconciling variable from math: coverage grows only where base success is "exceptionally rare" (BoxNet 34.0→51.0% Pass@1024) and shrinks elsewhere. Neither agentic paper measures base rarity of the rewarded strategy before training. Regimes differ by two orders of magnitude (14 model pairs vs one 7B model on 200 problems).
2. **Dense step credit is necessary vs outcome-only suffices vs credit is unmeasurable.** CANOPY (2609.01245): densifying reward "moves the result least, −1.8: partial credit was a compensation for under-exploration rather than a necessity." Credit Without Ground Truth (2608.19760): step credit signals are indistinguishable from shuffled controls and published comparisons "measure dose, not credit." Multi-Harness RL (2609.04518): grouping rule effect (+0.25 pp) below seed range (0.42–0.45 pp). Against this, the window holds a flood of dense-credit papers (DRACO, SWE-MILE, VICT, TRCA, SHARPO, GraphHCA; titles only) and Why Tool-Use RL Collapses (2606.26027) says RL alone is unstable without supervisory signals. Industry (Anthropic multi-agent) evaluates end states only; Cursor rewards outcomes plus tool-efficiency behaviour and mentions no critic.
3. **Asynchronous staleness is free vs costs 18.7%.** INTELLECT-2: four steps of lag "did not degrade results"; INTELLECT-3: "the future of RL is always a few steps off-policy ... the only practical way to efficiently scale RL to long-horizon agentic rollouts." PACE (2609.36830): unfiltered async loses 18.7% six-benchmark average accuracy at equal wall-clock, recoverable by staleness-aware rejection. Thinking Machines (nondeterminism): numerics alone turn on-policy into off-policy and "our reward collapses partway through training" without importance weighting. No source measures the staleness–accuracy frontier for rollouts of agentic length.
4. **LoRA suffices for RL (≈1 bit/episode) vs sharpening destroys sample diversity fast and industry trains full MoE.** TML: rank-1 LoRA "fully matches" full fine-tuning for policy-gradient RL on math; caveat that the bit bound "may only apply narrowly". Adapter Thickets: a single LoRA RLVR run drives the 160-vote below the untrained base and "voter errors grow steadily more correlated" with training. Cursor/Prime Intellect train full MoE models through hundreds of thousands of sandboxes with no statement about LoRA. Small information absorbed, large effect on the sampling distribution: the two framings are not reconciled.
5. **Forgetting: on-policy data vs KL proximity vs circuits vs "RL still forgets".** Retaining by Doing: on-policy data, "as opposed to ... KL regularization or advantage estimation", is the cause. TML OPD: SFT on the model's own samples at "any practical learning rate above zero" degrades IF-eval, so on-policy data alone is not protective. Mechanistic origins (2605.28860): "output-space proximity may not fully explain retention." How RL Reshapes: math RL drops ToolUse −8.3, IFEval −6.6, WildGuardTest −7.7 — RL "remains susceptible to forgetting" of exactly the agentic skills.
6. **τ-bench's framing (pass^k = agent reliability) vs its measurement (the simulator).** Sierra posts treat pass^k decay as the agent's "unsolved gap in reliability" and call for "improved user simulation during training." Mind the Sim2Real Gap: simulators inflate success from the 63.6% human baseline to 77.8%; Lost in Simulation: 9 pp swing across user LLMs; UserProxyBench: 15.2-point swing, 24.4% of successes contain a user-spec violation, over-disclosure removes 1.06 tool calls while preserving reward; One Frozen Simulator: RL against one simulator ends below the untrained baseline with humans. The leaderboard behaviour is partly a simulator property.
7. **Leaderboards as model capability vs harness as the dominant variable.** OpenAI "now report on all 500 tasks" as a model claim; More Tasks Won't Fix (2610.00651): model-ranking reliability 0.148–0.841 vs system 0.935–0.994; Multi-Harness RL: harness ×4.3 vs recipe ×1.16; Double Measurement Confound: a rule-based script scores 96.8 against an LLM mean of 97.5; Tessl position: harness components move scores "comparable to those between adjacent model generations."
8. **METR internal tension on 80% horizons.** Time-horizons page: "the trends for 50%- and 80%-time horizons are very similar." Jan 2026 note: 80% horizons "are not fully independent estimates from the 50% time horizon" and "improving performance on 20% horizon tasks can lower 80% horizon." Mar 2026 note: alternative fits move 80% horizons by up to 100% and 50% by up to 35%; excluding public tasks cuts Opus 4.6 from 11 h 59 m to 7 h 11 m.
9. **Multi-agent gain vs token budget.** Anthropic: +90.2% over single-agent Opus 4 with "token usage by itself explains 80% of the variance" and 15× tokens, no compute-matched single-agent arm; the window carries "The Illusion of Multi-Agent Advantage" (2606.13003) and "When More Is Less" (2609.19759) (titles only, not read).

## 3. Questions no source in this set asks, that the evidence jointly points to (my noticing)

Each is marked as my inference; the two or more sources whose own text jointly suggest it are named.

- **Q-A. Is step-level credit in coding-agent RL distinguishable from harness identity and seed noise under executed replay?** 2608.19760 audited credit against replay only in ALFWorld ("Our audit is confined to a single environment") and found nothing beyond shuffled controls; 2609.04518 found the harness carries ×4.3 of the solve rate and that "an out-of-fold classifier recovers the generating harness from Cross's advantage"; 2606.28433 says resets/resampling are simulator-exclusive actions, and INTELLECT-3 says sandboxes now provision in sub-seconds, so replay in a coding sandbox is affordable. Nobody has run the audit where the credit methods are actually deployed. (T1.)
- **Q-B. Does agentic RL move coin-flip tasks into always-solved or into never-solved, and at what horizon?** METR: GPT-5 "sometimes succeeds and sometimes fails on the remaining third"; Sharpening Tax: "post-training pushes tasks toward two extremes, always solved or never solved"; Sierra: pass^k decays while pass^1 saturates; 2608.18280: the stochastic component of task outcome is "irreducible" noise it cannot predict. No source tracks per-task success probability before and after RL on long-horizon tasks. (T1/T2.)
- **Q-C. How much of the advantage signal in simulated-user RL is attributable to the simulator's choices rather than the agent's?** UserProxyBench leaves "no measurement of downstream trained-policy effects"; One Frozen Simulator shows the policy learns the simulator's mode; Simulated Customers Never Walk Away shows the error is outcome-correlated and prompt-resistant; Mind the Sim2Real Gap shows binary reward misses human-visible outcomes. A per-episode decomposition of reward variance into simulator-side and agent-side causes does not exist. (τ-family, T2.)
- **Q-D. What is the stack-and-seed noise floor for agentic RL method comparisons, and how many published 1–5 pp deltas sit below it?** TML: 80 unique completions of 1000 at temperature 0 from batch-size variance; Luck Is Not Skill: a second accelerator type moved AUC 0.902 vs 0.852, "larger than the mean C4 design difference"; Agents Are Systems: 54% of outcome variance from repeats; Leni: ~3 pp unresolved; Multi-Harness RL: seed estimates "change sign." No source estimates the floor across stacks and seeds for one method at one budget. (systems.)
- **Q-E. What is the cost per bit of policy information in agentic RL, and does on-policy distillation dominate it for agents as it does for math?** TML LoRA: ~1 bit per episode; TML OPD: 1,800 vs 17,920 GPU-hours; OLMo 3: 1.01% MFU, 21 days × 224 GPUs for +5 AIME; Terminal-Bench audit: $105,933 for 28,801 trials; Cursor: hundreds of thousands of sandboxes, no cost; Sharpening Tax leaves the OPD tax unmeasured. Nobody relates rollout cost to information absorbed or coverage retained. (T2, systems.)
- **Q-F. Can outcome-reward RL learn information gathering when the training simulator leaks the information?** hyper-τ: zero questions 5%, two questions 25%, agents spend 0.45× budget; UserProxyBench: over-disclosure "causes the agent to make 1.06 fewer tool calls" with reward preserved; Humans are Missing and Specification Paradox put specification recovery at the centre. The incentive to ask is removed by the simulator exactly where the benchmark says asking decides success. (τ-family.)
- **Q-G. Does agentic RL on one environment family forget other agentic skills, and is the loss on-policy-protected?** How RL Reshapes: math RL drops ToolUse by 8.3 and IFEval by 6.6; Retaining by Doing tests only IF, knowledge and arithmetic; TML OPD: no mix preserves IF-eval. No source measures SWE-RL → τ-style policy following or the reverse. (Narasimhan lines, T2.)
- **Q-H. Which fraction of "unsolved frontier" tasks that feed RL curricula are instrument failures, and what does training on them teach?** Terminal-Bench audit: 47 of 125 all-fail tasks are broken oracles, infra, bypass-only or uncertified; hyper-τ: 17–42% of runs attempt cheating; INTELLECT-2 filters tasks by solve rate but not by oracle validity; Prime Intellect's hub lists 500+ community tasks with no validity screen mentioned. (T1, systems.)
